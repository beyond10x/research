## Question

Can a small model that sees only the trajectory so far and the proposed action decide, before the
action runs, whether to allow it, send it back with feedback, or pause for a human, and does that
raise task completion (§1, §4.1)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · pre-execution-action-sentinel · E4
- practice: a trained 0.6B or 1.7B model reviews each proposed action before execution and chooses ALLOW, REDIRECT (withhold the action, give corrective feedback, the agent proposes again) or HARD-PAUSE (suspend and ask a human); compared with the same agent and tasks without it (§4.1, Table 1, §5.1).
- claim: HiSentinel "consistently improves task completion across Sentinel scales and coding-agent families, with gains of up to 14% and 10%" (Abstract).
- evidence: Table 2, resolved % without and with sentinel, SWE-bench Verified Mini then Ask or Assume. Qwen3-Coder: 30 to 36 (0.6B) and 44 (1.7B); 23 to 28 and 33. Devstral: 20 to 26 and 30; 19 to 24 and 27. Sonnet-4.6, 1.7B only: 60 to 66; 58 to 61. Differences are +3 to +14 points in all 10 pairs (reader's arithmetic). One cell is 50 tasks (Mini) or 100 tasks (Ask or Assume); repeats and confidence intervals are not stated.
- effect: helps
- reader: an A/B on equal tasks, so E4. The authors built, trained and evaluated the system. One run per cell as far as the text shows, 50 tasks, so one task is 2 points. The 1.7B model is the one with the largest gains; the 0.6B gains are 5 or 6 points. The sentinel and the Qwen3-Coder agent are in one model family.

### F2 · practice · pre-execution-action-sentinel · E4
- practice: as F1; the measurement is tokens per trajectory (Table 2: "average input and output tokens per trajectory (millions)").
- claim: "competitive token consumption" and the cost "remains comparable to other feedback-based baselines" (Abstract, §5.2).
- evidence: Table 2 cost without and with the 1.7B sentinel, SWE-bench Verified Mini then Ask or Assume. Sonnet-4.6: 0.26 to 0.41 and 0.47 to 0.82. Qwen3-Coder: 0.29 to 0.43 and 0.26 to 0.39. Devstral: 1.16 to 1.34 and 1.25 to 1.44. Increases of 15% to 75% (reader's arithmetic).
- effect: hurts
- reader: every sentinel cell costs more than its base. The paper's claim is about comparison with other monitors, not with no monitor. Whether the sentinel's own tokens are in the Table 2 figure is not stated in the parts read; proxy-user tokens are excluded (A.3). The `effect` here is on cost only; F1 is the success effect.

### F3 · practice · periodic-critic-feedback · E4
- practice: a critic gives feedback every five executed steps (SWE-PRM, taxonomy-guided; or Steer, Don't Solve, an SFT and DPO trained 4B critic at the same interval) on the same agent and tasks (§5.1).
- claim: HiSentinel improves over "competing intervention methods"; the baselines' resolution rates sit near the unassisted agent (§5.2, Table 2).
- evidence: Table 2, resolved % against the base agent. Qwen3-Coder, Mini: SWE-PRM 32 (0.6B backbone) and 20 (1.7B) against 30; SDS 28. Ask or Assume: SWE-PRM 22 and 25 against 23; SDS 24. Devstral, Mini: SWE-PRM 22 and 24, SDS 24, against 20. Ask or Assume: SWE-PRM 21 and 22, SDS 20, against 19. Intervention count on Mini: SWE-PRM 284 interventions, 3 beneficial (1.1%), 1 rescue, 6 regressions (§5.4.1).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 by design (against the unassisted agent). Differences from base are -10 to +4 points on 50 or 100 tasks, with no sign pattern. The baselines are run by the authors on their harness; SDS used the public checkpoint because the training data were not released (§5.1). The SWE-PRM rows are listed under the 0.6B and 1.7B backbones in Table 2; the text does not say what that backbone does for SWE-PRM.

### F4 · practice · prompt-only-action-monitor · E4
- practice: the same Qwen3 model (0.6B or 1.7B) with no training, prompted to inspect each proposed action before execution (Step-by-Step baseline) (§5.1).
- claim: not claimed as a result; it is the prompt-only baseline for HiSentinel (§5.1).
- evidence: Table 2, resolved % against the base agent. Qwen3-Coder, Mini: 18 (0.6B) and 32 (1.7B) against 30; Ask or Assume: 20 and 21 against 23. Devstral, Mini: 18 and 22 against 20; Ask or Assume: 18 and 20 against 19. Below base in 5 of 8 cells (reader's arithmetic). On Mini with the 1.7B model, 1 beneficial intervention of 24 (4.2%), 1 rescue, no regression (§5.4.1).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 by design; the paper reports it only as a baseline. The monitor is a small model with no task-specific training, so this says little about a prompted large model.

### F5 · practice · hindsight-teacher-distillation · E4
- practice: train the sentinel's routing with a teacher that also sees the recorded outcome of each action, and distil its binary allow-or-intervene judgment into the student that sees only the pre-action context; ablated against no distillation and against a teacher with no future (§4.2, §5.3).
- claim: removing distillation or using a causal teacher degrades routing and end-to-end resolution "despite their smaller gaps in static F1 and nearly identical token costs" (§5.3).
- evidence: Table 4(c), SWE-bench Verified Mini, 1.7B: resolved 44% full, 34% without distillation, 36% with a causal teacher; cost 0.43, 0.43, 0.42. Table 4(a) M-F1 81.77 full, 76.42 without distillation, 77.16 causal teacher, 76.30 self-distillation, 75.53 with a 3-class teacher. Only two ablations were run end to end (§5.3).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 by design: an ablation on equal tasks. 44 against 34 and 36 is 5 and 4 tasks of 50 in one run, and the full system's number is also the one used for the headline claim. The text's explanation (fewer false alarms but more missed interventions) is not backed by counts in the parts read. The teacher's judgments are themselves labels from an LLM annotator (F11).

### F6 · practice · helpfulness-gate-on-distillation · E4
- practice: distil from the teacher only where the teacher with the outcome predicts the right label with higher probability than the same teacher with the outcome masked (§4.2, Eq. 7); ablated by removing the gate (§5.3).
- claim: removing the gate "consistently degrades performance" (§5.3).
- evidence: Table 4(a), 1.7B, SWE-Intervene: M-F1 78.72 without the gate against 81.77 full; I-F1 77.41 against 78.97; R-F1 61.06 against 66.30; HP-F1 91.18 against 92.90. Not run end to end.
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 by design but static only: the held-out classification score, not task completion. The gap in M-F1 is 3 points on one training run. Other routing ablations in the same table: classification head 66.92 M-F1, no route SFT 68.91 (§5.3).

### F7 · practice · preference-tuned-intervention-feedback · E4
- practice: train the message the sentinel sends after it intervenes separately from its routing: supervised fine-tuning on 1,345 annotated feedback records, then DPO on 800 preference pairs, with routing frozen; ablated by joint training of routing and feedback, and by removing DPO (§4.3, §5.3, Table 7).
- claim: separate training with DPO gives more grounded and actionable feedback (§5.3).
- evidence: Table 4(b), pass rates for groundedness and actionability: 76.8% and 73.5% full; 66.9% and 63.2% with joint feedback; 69.4% and 65.8% without DPO. Routing quality with joint training: M-F1 78.67, HP-F1 92.49 against 81.77 and 92.90 (Table 4(a)).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 by design. The judge that scores groundedness and actionability is not named in the parts read, and the AI-use statement says LLM judges were used for evaluation. The number of test cases is not stated. Feedback quality is not tied to task completion in an ablation.

### F8 · observation · intervention-precision-over-frequency · E3
- practice: nobody assigned this; the interventions of three monitors on SWE-bench Verified Mini are each judged beneficial, neutral or harmful from the task, the proposed action and the trajectory before it (§5.4.1).
- claim: "H I S ENTINEL's gains reflect intervention quality rather than frequency" (§5.4.1).
- evidence: §5.4.1, Mini, 1.7B models. HiSentinel: 109 of 242 interventions beneficial (45.0%), 106 neutral, 27 harmful; 8 rescues and 1 regression, resolved 15/50 to 22/50. Step-by-Step: 1 beneficial of 24 (4.2%), 1 rescue, no regression. SWE-PRM: 3 beneficial of 284 (1.1%), 1 rescue, 6 regressions.
- effect: helps
- reader: a comparison between three different monitors on one benchmark, with no control for what else differs. Who or what judged an intervention beneficial is not stated in the parts read. Rescues and regressions are counts of single paired runs, and a stochastic rerun could flip them, which the authors note (§5.4.1). 27 harmful of 242 is 11% (reader's arithmetic).

### F9 · practice · monitor-kv-prefix-caching · E4
- practice: reuse the key-value cache of the sentinel's input across steps, since each monitoring input extends the previous prefix by one step, against recomputing the full prefix at every step (§5.4.2).
- claim: caching saves little and changes decisions, so the system recomputes (§5.4.2).
- evidence: §5.4.2, 1.7B sentinel on Mini. Reuse is 86.4% of input tokens on untruncated calls and 3.2% on the 906 of 1,869 calls that need left truncation, 29.8% token-weighted overall. Sentinel tokens per task 382K to 299K (22%); mean time 757 s against 763 s. Of 896 token-identical routing calls, 13 routing flips and 3 effective-intervention flips. Resolved 22/50 with full recomputation against 18/50 with caching.
- effect: hurts
- reader: E4 by design (same tasks, same sentinel, caching on and off). The cause of the flips is stated as numerical difference of BF16 exact-prefix caching; 4 tasks of 50 on one run is within the noise the paper itself describes for single trajectories. The result concerns a long-context small monitor, not caching in general.

### F10 · practice · pause-for-human-clarification · E3
- practice: the sentinel's HARD-PAUSE route suspends the agent and forwards its clarification question to a proxy user that holds the withheld full task specification; used on Ask or Assume, where task-critical details are masked (§5.1, A.3).
- claim: HiSentinel recognises missing information and initiates clarification, which the other methods rarely do (A.3, §5.2).
- evidence: Table 2, Ask or Assume resolved % without and with the 1.7B sentinel: Qwen3-Coder 23 to 33, Devstral 19 to 27, Sonnet-4.6 58 to 61. In A.3: "the other baselines rarely invoked" the proxy-user API, which was available to all of them; no count is given. Proxy-user latency and tokens are excluded from cost, wall-clock time includes the interaction. HP-F1 92.90 on the static test, of which 121 of 1,243 held-out instances are natural AskUserQuestion cases (§3, Table 4(a)).
- effect: helps
- reader: E3: no ablation turns the pause route off while keeping REDIRECT, so the gain on Ask or Assume is the whole sentinel's. The human is a model (a proxy-user API under an information boundary), not a person, and its answer quality is not measured in the parts read. HARD-PAUSE training data come from real SWE-Chat AskUserQuestion calls (A.1.3), so the pause decision is learned from where real agents did ask a real human.

### F11 · observation · llm-annotator-over-flags-intervention · E2
- practice: nobody assigned this; GPT-5.5, following human-written criteria and shown the recorded continuation, labelled decision points, and two stages of human audit then reviewed the provisional REDIRECT and HARD-PAUSE labels (§3, A.1.2, A.1.4).
- claim: the audit "aligns the dataset with the central objective ... intervening when doing so is likely to improve final task completion, rather than correcting every detectable imperfection" (A.1.4).
- evidence: Table 6: of 2,252 provisional intervention candidates, 570 rejected (25.3%) and 1,682 retained. The most common rejections: locally imperfect but recoverable actions, problems the agent had already recognised, and HARD-PAUSE labels placed before the information was needed (A.1.4). No agreement figure between annotator and auditors beyond this rate; the number of auditors is not stated.
- effect: hurts
- reader: a count with nothing to compare against, so E2. `effect: hurts` here means the machine labeller's output needed a quarter removed by people. The effect of the audit on the trained sentinel is not tested. The labels use hindsight and the paper states they are "a hindsight-informed judgment rather than a measured difference between outcomes under alternative routes" (§4.1).

### F12 · practice · trained-sentinel-vs-posthoc-trajectory-diagnosis · E3
- practice: a trained pre-execution sentinel on the causal prefix against untrained post-hoc diagnosers that read the whole trajectory (All-at-Once, AgentRx, TrajAudit, RCTA) and the prompt-only Step-by-Step, on a static test of intervention recognition, failure localisation and safety classification (§5.1, §5.2, Table 3).
- claim: HiSentinel "substantially outperforms prompt-based and post-hoc baselines" in-domain "while requiring fewer tokens per trajectory", and the advantage "transfers" to RootSE and R-Judge (§5.2).
- evidence: Table 3, 1.7B backbone. SWE-Intervene M-F1 81.77 against 27.31 to 29.94; I-F1 78.97 against 31.38 to 38.42; cost 5.91K against 8.10K to 11.42K tokens. RootSE T@0 9.80 against 2.94 to 3.92, T@1 15.69 against 8.82 to 12.75. R-Judge BAcc 66.14 against 66.14 for AgentRx and 57.48 at most for the others; M-F1 65.09 against 65.60 for AgentRx. For AgentRx, RootSE cost is 3.09M tokens against 0.08M. 0.6B: SWE-Intervene M-F1 79.99 against 25.78 to 30.23.
- effect: mixed
- reader: E3: different methods on one test, not an ablation. HiSentinel is trained on SWE-Intervene, so the in-domain gap compares a trained model with prompted ones; whether the baselines were tuned on it is not stated in the parts read. Out of domain the margin is small or zero on R-Judge, and T@0 is under 10% for every method. `mixed` because in-domain it helps by a wide margin and on R-Judge it ties.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Not stated: the parts read have no limitations section. §5.4.1 notes that "even rare stochastic continuations could make poor guidance appear successful", and reports rescues and regressions separately.
- §4.1: the labels are hindsight-informed judgments, not measured differences between outcomes under alternative routes.
- A.3: proxy-user latency and tokens are reported separately from inference cost.

## Reader's notes

- All end-to-end cells are 50 tasks (SWE-bench Verified Mini) or 100 tasks (Ask or Assume); no repeats, intervals or tests are stated in the parts read. Differences of 2 to 6 points are 1 to 3 tasks on Mini.
- The sentinel's training data (SWE-Intervene) come from trajectories of earlier coding agents, labelled by GPT-5.5 with the recorded outcome visible. The test agents and the sentinel's teacher are Qwen models. Only the Sonnet-4.6 transfer is outside that family, and it is run with the 1.7B sentinel only.
- Ask or Assume was built to test clarification behaviour, and the sentinel was trained with human-question examples. The benchmark is the one the HARD-PAUSE route is designed for.
- The intervention budget and cooldown applied to every method are mentioned in A.3 but their values are not given in the parts read.
- The mix of human-written guidelines, GPT-5.5 labelling and human audit makes the labels a judgment of what an intervener would do, not the behaviour of a human operator in a live session. SWE-Chat supplies natural AskUserQuestion cases only.
- The introduction cites a Terminal-Bench analysis by other authors (1,184 failed trajectories; half committed the decisive error by step 7 and became unrecoverable around step 12; prefix monitoring caught 3.7% to 8.7% of failures before lock-in). It is not this paper's data and is not a finding here.

## Discrepancies

- The abstract says gains "of up to 14% and 10%". Table 2 shows gains of 14 and 10 percentage points on resolved rate (30 to 44, 23 to 33); the relative gains are 47% and 43% (reader's arithmetic). The unit is not stated.
- Table 2 gives SWE-PRM on the Qwen3-Coder agent 32% (0.6B) and 20% (1.7B) on SWE-bench Verified Mini, a 12-point difference between two backbones of one critic; the text does not comment on it.
