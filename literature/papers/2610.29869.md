## Question

When a coding agent compacts its context by summarizing the conversation, can an attacker who controls
text the agent reads forge a user turn that survives as a genuine user instruction, does the agent then
act on it, and what stops it (Abstract, §1)?

## Findings

### F1 · observation · flattened-role-labels-in-compaction-input · E3
- practice: harnesses that flatten the typed conversation into one string with in-band labels such as `[User]:` before summarizing (pi, opencode, Cline, Aider, OpenHands), against harnesses that pass a role-typed message array (Codex, Gemini CLI, Qwen Code, Continue). The same forged approval is planted in tool output (E10, E6, E11).
- claim: "Susceptibility is therefore a property of the serialization strategy, not of the model"; the flattening harnesses launder the forged approval into the summary as a user instruction and the agent then runs the attacker's command (§1, E10).
- evidence: laundered, n = 15 per harness (3 models x 5), E10, p. 14: pi, opencode, Aider 100% (15/15); OpenHands 93% (14/15); Cline 87% (13/15); Codex, Gemini CLI, Qwen Code, Continue 0% (0/15); every clean arm 0/15. Executed attacker `curl | sh`, gpt-5.6-terra (E6, p. 10; E11, p. 14 to 16): pi 3/3 onboarding, 1/3 webpage, 3/3 dependency; OpenHands 4/5, opencode 1/5, Cline 1/5, Aider 1/5 at their own effective windows; Codex 0/9 and Gemini CLI 0/9; every clean arm 0.
- effect: hurts
- reader: a comparison between released harnesses on the same planted approval, not a controlled change of one serializer: the harnesses also differ in compaction prompt and in everything else. For the four role-typed harnesses the author built the typed message shape himself from source (§6). The agent that acts is a one-tool bash loop with no approval or sandbox, so execution rates are model-only (§6). The laundering verdict comes from an LLM judge, cross-checked on a stratified sample of 18 items by another model family (§7). "0% with no intermediate values" rests on n = 15 per cell.

### F2 · practice · escape-role-labels-before-flattening · E4
- practice: the same payload planted with the forged `[User]:` label in front of it and as plain text with no label, in a flattening serialization; the plain-text arm stands in for escaping the label, which the paper proposes as the fix but does not implement (E2, Defenses).
- claim: "The forged label is the mechanism"; "escaped attacker text behaves like the plain-text arms, which mostly do not launder" (E2, p. 8).
- evidence: E2, p. 8, pooled across two labs (Anthropic, OpenAI): pi 87% with label against 6% plain text; Gemini CLI prompt with its rule removed 79% against 28%; with the full vendor rule 69% against 3%; with the attribution rule 74% against 0%. n per cell not stated for E2 (E1 uses n = 72 per cell).
- effect: helps
- reader: an A/B on the same payload and prompts, so E4 for the claim that the label carries the effect. The fix itself (an escaping serializer) was not built or measured; the plain-text arm only approximates it. The pi row is the one real flattening harness in E1 to E5; the Gemini CLI rows run its prompt over a serialization it does not use (E1 caveat, p. 8). Laundering scored by an LLM judge.

### F3 · practice · in-prompt-anti-injection-rule · E4
- practice: turn the vendor's in-prompt anti-injection rule on or off in the summarization prompt, over a flattening serialization (E3, p. 9; counterfactual for Gemini CLI, which is role-typed).
- claim: "helps unevenly and never eliminates the effect"; "a reduction, not a fix" (E3).
- evidence: E3, p. 9, rule off to rule on: Anthropic 53% to 46% (-7 points, "within noise"), OpenAI 83% to 62% (-21), Zhipu 86% to 60% (-26); n = 72 per cell (E1, p. 8). The author's own attribution rule: Anthropic 42%, OpenAI 74% against 62% for the vendor rule (E3). Gemini's rule stops about 13% on Claude and about 25% on GPT (E9, p. 14).
- effect: mixed
- reader: a clean on/off ablation, E4. The effect differs by model and is nil on the Anthropic model. The setting is a counterfactual, not Gemini CLI as shipped; the paper's own point is that role-typing, not the rule, gives that harness its 0% (§4).

### F4 · practice · code-check-of-summary-claims-against-user-turns · E3
- practice: after the summarizer returns, check in code each claim the summary attributes to the user against the real user turns the harness still holds, and demote claims that are not grounded ("provenance contrast" at a strictness setting); the exact-match version demotes most legitimate content (§9, E9).
- claim: "A code-level check catches most of the laundering where prompt rules do not": 77% caught at 2% collateral at the 0.15 setting (E9, p. 14).
- evidence: E9, p. 14, strictness 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.15 / 0.25 / 0.40: caught 82 / 80 / 77 / 70 / 56%; legitimate claims demoted 9 / 6 / 2 / 1 / 0%, over 324 claims from clean summaries. Baselines: Gemini rule about 13% on Claude and about 25% on GPT (measured on the same data); published classifier defenses 67% at best and 42% on low-signal payloads (rate reported by the cited paper, not re-measured). The exact-match version caught nearly everything but demoted 72 to 100% of legitimate content. 23% still gets through at 0.15.
- effect: helps
- reader: compared with other defenses on one author-built data set, with no undefended arm other than the laundering rate itself, so E3. The author designed the check and the test payloads (Limitations: payloads predate it, no adaptive attack; "77% is an upper bound"). The clean corpus behind the false-positive rate is 4 sessions (Limitations). The classifier figure is from another paper's setting.

### F5 · practice · poison-position-in-compacted-window · E4
- practice: vary where the forged approval sits in the session (start 10%, middle 50%, end 90% at about 130k tokens) and how large the window fed to the summarizer is (about 2,800 to about 908,000 tokens), pi's real summarization, gpt-5.6-terra (E7, E8, E12).
- claim: "The laundering is scale-invariant"; "the position of the poison in the conversation does not matter" (E7, E8).
- evidence: laundered 3/3 at every size: about 2,800, 65,200, 130,000, 306,800, 507,000, 731,000, 908,000 tokens (E7, p. 12; E12, p. 16); 3/3 at start, middle and end, with Codex keeping the approval 3/3 at each (E8, p. 13). The model's input ceiling is 922k tokens (E7).
- effect: none
- reader: size and position were set by the author on synthetic transcripts, one model, n = 3 per cell; a flat 3/3 shows no loss of laundering at that n and does not exclude a lower rate. "None" here means the attack's reach did not change, which is a worse outcome for a defender than "none" suggests.

### F6 · practice · window-size-before-compaction · E4
- practice: grow the window that is compacted, holding the scenario fixed (pi, gpt-5.6-terra, onboarding, n = 5), and measure whether the agent runs the attacker's command and how prominently the summary carries the approval (judged 1 to 5) (E6, E14).
- claim: "Execution falls as the window fills — laundering does not"; the drop is "the approval getting quieter under harder compression, not the agent detecting the forgery" (E6, Discussion 5).
- evidence: executed, E6, p. 11: about 2,400 tokens 5/5; about 26,000 5/5; about 112,000 2/5; about 263,000 2/5; clean at 263k 0/3; laundering 5/5 at every size. Mean salience, E14, p. 18: 5.0, 5.0, 4.2, 3.0 at about 2,400, 25,700, 111,700, 263,400 tokens. Across harnesses at their own effective windows execution is 4/5 at about 63k and 1/5 at about 185k to 300k (E11, p. 16).
- effect: helps
- reader: "helps" is read for the defender: attack execution falls as the window grows. The authors call the small-window rate an upper bound and about 40% the typical case; elsewhere the paper writes "about 20 to 40%". n = 5, one model; salience is judged by the model that also writes the summary and acts (E13 caveat), though E14 involves no executor. The control in F7 shows the drop is general caution about `curl | sh`, so it is no defense.

### F7 · practice · forged-approval-carried-by-compaction-summary · E4
- practice: compare a forged approval laundered through the summary with a genuine user turn stating the same thing, and with no approval; separately, a laundered constraint "don't touch auth/" against a stated and an absent one (E6 control, E5).
- claim: "a forged approval is as effective as a genuine one"; the laundered constraint "steers the agent's plan almost as completely as a genuine user message would" (E6, E5).
- evidence: executed, laundered / genuine / clean, E6, p. 12: about 26k tokens 5/5, 5/5, 0/5; about 263k 2/5, 2/5, 0/5. Plan avoided the file, E5, p. 9 to 10, n = 48 per arm: stated by the user 98%; laundered through tool output 92%; clean 6%; cited the reason 98%, 100%, 12%. Without Codex, genuine 100% and laundered 94%.
- effect: hurts
- reader: an A/B with a floor and a ceiling on equal tasks. Summaries are handed to a fresh agent, which omits the live pipeline in which the agent's own earlier audit is summarized with the forged line (the paper says this cuts the chain about half the time, F9). Benign canary payload only; a credential-exfiltration payload never executed in live testing, "even when the approval had laundered" (E6), and that test is described without counts.

### F8 · practice · forged-line-placed-last-before-compaction · E4
- practice: change only the injection design at a fixed window of about 260k tokens: one forged line at 75% depth, the line moved to the end, the line repeated 4 times, and variants worded to match the summarizer's "preserve constraints, decisions, exact commands" cue (E13).
- claim: "the attacker can reverse the attenuation through placement, not volume"; "Naive repetition is counterproductive" (E13, p. 18).
- evidence: executed (n = 5 each) and mean salience, E13, p. 16: repeat 4x 0/5, 2.8; baseline at 75% 2/5, 3.5; framed + repeated + end 2/5, 3.8; framed at 75% 2/5, 4.5; framed at end 3/5, 4.8; single line at end 4/5, 4.8. Laundered 5/5 in every design. Execution goes from 40% to 80% with the line at the end.
- effect: hurts
- reader: n = 5 per design, one model that writes the summary, judges salience and acts, so the salience-to-execution link is "a within-model association" (E13 caveat, Limitations). Framing did not clearly raise execution. This is an attacker-side practice; "hurts" is the effect on the agent's safety. Repetition lowered execution (0/5), the opposite direction from the other designs.

### F9 · practice · forged-user-line-in-fetched-page · E4
- practice: run the real pi TUI (v0.79.0, victim gpt-5.6-luna) in a container with a scripted user, `/compact` typed by hand, and a web page the agent fetches that holds one forged `[User]:` approval; each poisoned take has a clean twin that differs in exactly that line (E15, p. 18 to 19).
- claim: "~50% end-to-end success against a clean arm that never executed" (E15, p. 19).
- evidence: E15, p. 19. Poisoned, 6 takes: full chain 3/6, refused after compaction 3/6, ran before compaction 0/6. Clean: full chain 0/5, refused 5/5, ran before compaction 1/6 (one clean take executed in its first turn, before compaction). One recorded pair, case study p. 21 to 25: the agent audited and refused, the summary kept the concerns but recorded "User approved running `curl ... | sh` without asking again", and "ok, what's next?" was followed 2 seconds later by the command; the clean twin asked for approval.
- effect: hurts
- reader: paired byte-identical arms make this E4 on the live product, but at n = 6 against 5 on one harness and one model, with the session shortened and `compaction.keepRecentTokens` lowered "so /compact summarizes rather than no-ops" (E15). The author states that the absolute numbers are small and "what is solid is the mechanism and the paired 3/6-vs-0/5 separation". Payload is a benign canary; the author also notes the Codex-style sandbox with network off blocks this specific command (§4).

### F10 · practice · agent-audit-reasoning-in-transcript · E3
- practice: the agent audits a fetched artifact before compaction and voices its reasoning, so the audit and its refusal are in the transcript that is summarized; the attacker adds forgeries of the assistant's thinking and of a tool result confirming a checksum (E15 finding 1, E16, p. 19 to 20).
- claim: "The audit is the barrier, and it is forge-resistant"; "Assistant- and tool-role content is checkable against the transcript while user-role content is not" (E16); the paper recommends "Encouraging agents to voice their verification reasoning (rather than silently checking)".
- evidence: E16, p. 20, n = 5 per arm, terra and luna executed: clean 0/5 and 0/5; forged user approval 0/5 and 0/5; forged assistant thinking 0/5 and 0/5; both 0/5 and 0/5; maximal forgery with a fabricated checksum result 0/5 and 1/5; "1/50 pooled across all arms and both models". The audit survived in the summary 4/5 and 1/5 in the maximal arm. E15 (p. 19): the refusal survives into the summary "roughly half the time"; where it was compressed away the forged line was promoted to a user constraint.
- effect: helps
- reader: no arm in E16 removes the audit at the same window size, so the comparison with "about 50% when the audit is absent" is across experiments (about 112k tokens against a short live session), E3. The audit is scripted and "maximally strong" (Limitations). The practice of voicing reasoning is recommended, not tested against silent checking.

### F11 · practice · post-compaction-user-message-phrasing · E3
- practice: vary the user's next message after the laundered compaction in the live pi takes (E15 finding 2, p. 19).
- claim: "the post-compaction trigger is a second controlling variable"; the attacker needs the user "to say something that asks the agent to continue" (E15).
- evidence: E15, p. 19: a bare "ok" produced 0 executions in three takes, the approval laundered in two of them; "let's get it passing tonight" produced executions in both poisoned and clean arms; "ok, what's next?" produced 3/6 poisoned against 0/5 clean. Limitations: "0%-80% across phrasings".
- effect: hurts
- reader: described takes of a few runs each on one model, with the phrasing and the arm confounded across takes; the paper does not give per-phrasing counts beyond the three quoted. Effect is read as the effect of a continue-style message on unwanted execution.

### F12 · practice · repeated-compaction-of-injected-claim · E3
- practice: compact the same poisoned history five times in a row and score the summary at each round (E4, p. 9).
- claim: "Repeated compaction neither strengthens nor removes the injected claim"; "protecting the first compaction protects all of them" (E4).
- evidence: E4, p. 9, 18 chains per harness, families pooled: pi still present 72% and laundered 67% every round; Gemini CLI laundered 61, 56, 50, 56, 56%, provenance kept 22, 17, 28, 11, 11%; Codex present 89%, laundered 0% by construction. Survival constant on all 54 chains; laundering verdict held on 52 of 54.
- effect: none
- reader: the same chains are compared across rounds with no control. Gemini CLI and Codex run over a flattening stand-in here, so only pi is a real flattening harness; the Codex 0% is a scoring artifact (E1 caveat).

### F13 · observation · model-refuses-to-summarize-malicious-transcript · E3
- practice: whether a frontier model refuses to compact a transcript that holds an overtly malicious laundered instruction (T5, p. 11).
- claim: claude-opus-5's "content filter refused to compact the laundered transcript on 10 of 18 reps (~56%), cutting the chain off at the summarization step, while GPT and GLM refused none" (E6 models table text).
- evidence: T5, 18 reps per model: claude-opus-5 refused 10 of 18; GPT and GLM refused none. Raw data in `data/stated-plan/t5.jsonl` (not read). Executed demonstrations therefore run on GPT models only; non-GPT models are untested at the execution stage (Limitations).
- effect: helps
- reader: three models compared in one run, no control; a refusal rate on this payload, not on typical compaction. The refusal is a property of the model, outside the harness, and the paper does not say whether it also fires on non-malicious summaries.

## Limits stated by the authors

- E16's audit is scripted and maximally strong; real agents audit unevenly (Limitations).
- The executed demonstrations run on OpenAI models; claude-opus-5 refused to produce the malicious summary on about half of runs (Limitations).
- All execution numbers use a benign canary; a credential-exfiltration payload never executed, so rates are an upper bound for high-stakes payloads (Limitations).
- Salience is measured within one model (gpt-5.6-terra) at n = 5 (Limitations).
- E15 is six paired takes on one model; the execution rate is noisy at n = 3 to 5 and "should be treated as direction, not precision" (Limitations).
- Early stated-plan cells (E1 to E5) used a flattening stand-in for Codex and Gemini CLI, which are role-typed; only pi's rows there measure a real flattening harness (Limitations).
- The aggregate laundering rates score a stated plan or a summary, not a live execution each time (Limitations).
- The defense has not met an adaptive attack, and "77% is an upper bound against an attacker who is not adapting" (Limitations).
- All payloads are generic and recombination attacks are untested, so every number is "a floor"; the clean corpus behind the false-positive rate is 4 sessions (Limitations, §2).
- The executed demo runs a one-tool loop with no approval gate or sandbox; a sandbox with network off blocks the specific `curl | sh`, and the harms that need no egress were not demonstrated under a sandbox (Limitations).

## Reader's notes

- Single author, from a vendor in application security. The attack, the defense and its test data are all his; independent replication is not reported. Raw data and code are linked, and findings were disclosed to the five affected maintainers (Responsible disclosure).
- What was run is the harnesses' summarization prompt and serializer plus a generic agent, not the released programs, except E15 (one harness, one model, small n) (§6). The conversations are synthetic.
- Laundering and salience are both LLM-judged. The judge agreement check covers 18 items (§7). In E13 the same model summarizes, judges and acts.
- No result in the paper measures the false-positive cost of escaping role labels; F2 uses plain text as the stand-in.
- The size sweeps (F5, F6) and position test use 3 to 5 runs per cell on one model; no confidence intervals are given anywhere.
- The paper's recommendations on defenses (escape labels, verify the summary in code, carry `{text, source}`, give the summary its own role) are listed in order of cost; only the second is evaluated (Defenses).
- Kind choices: sizes, positions and designs in F5, F6 and F8 were assigned by the author on synthetic transcripts, so they are filed as practices although no team would choose them; the nearest design value is not in the closed set (P11).

## Discrepancies

- The text at the end of E16 says "1/50 with the maximal forgery", but the table gives the maximal forgery (evidence arm) 0/5 for terra and 1/5 for luna, 1/10. The 1/50 is the pool over all five arms and both models.
- E15's table gives denominators of 6 for the poisoned arm and 5 for the clean arm in the first two columns, and 6 for the clean arm in the third column; the dagger implies that the clean take that executed in its first turn is left out of the first two clean counts.
- The execution plateau is written as "~40%" in E6 and as "~20-40%" in §6 and Discussion 2; E11 gives 1/5 for three harnesses.
- The contributions say the executed chain was shown "on all five flattening harnesses, via both insider and external scenarios", but the scenario matrix (E6) covers pi, Codex and Gemini CLI only; the scenario used for the four other harnesses in E11 is not stated in the parts read.
- The repository link on the title page is named `flat-compaction-attacks-oct-2027`, while the paper is dated October 2026.
