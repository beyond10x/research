## Question

With the backbone model, hardware and time budget held fixed, which layers of an autonomous
machine-learning-engineering harness (chat loop, search tree, orchestration, nudges, tools) improve
on a single long coding-agent session, and do published harnesses beat that session (§1)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · coding-agent-environment-over-chat-loop · E4
- practice: give the model a coding-agent session (read, write, edit, bash in a working directory, "Oneshot") in place of a single chat completion that must return a full script and is re-queried with the error trace on failure ("Chat"); same prompt template, 6 h limit, same tasks (§4.1, B.1).
- claim: "the performance of frontier LLMs — GLM 5.2 and Kimi K3 — dramatically improves when they get direct access to the execution environment"; the coding-agent environment is "the highest-leverage intervention" (§4.1, §4).
- evidence: Figure 2 on default29, values in the figure only. Text: even Gemma 4 31B benefits; the DeepSeek V4 final releases gain more than the Preview releases (§4.1). Preview releases show "close to no gap" between Chat and Oneshot, final releases a clear gap, narrower than GLM 5.2 (G.2). Best-of-N Oneshot at 6 h scores "significantly better" than a single Chat iteration, which the authors offer against a memorisation reading (Appendix F, behavioural check).
- effect: helps
- reader: an A/B on equal tasks and backbone, so E4, but no number is in the text. The two arms differ in prompt, data overview and tools together, not in one switch. The release-to-release pattern is the authors' hypothesis "post-training ate the harness" and is not tested (G.2).

### F2 · practice · search-policy-over-agent-iterations · E4
- practice: chain agent iterations of one session each at a matched 24 h budget under a search rule that picks the parent solution: Chain (latest), Greedy (best validation score), UCB1 (rank-based), Best-of-N (independent, empty tree) (§4.2, B.3).
- claim: "the search strategy has a very small effect on performance"; "no strategy provides a statistically significant advantage in either percentile or medal rate" (§4.2).
- evidence: paired-by-task differences on default29, Table 5. Largest percentile gap: UCB1 ahead of Best-of-N by 2.71 points, 95% CI (−1.44, +6.60); medal rate −5.3 [−11.8, +1.1] for Best-of-N against UCB1. All six pairs have intervals that contain 0.
- effect: none
- reader: E4 by design. "No significant difference" is limited by 3 to 4 seeds per task at 24 h (G.1); intervals of 4 to 12 points are wide. The search rules are "the simplest faithful version" re-implemented by the authors, not the published systems (§4).

### F3 · practice · single-long-session-agent · E4
- practice: run one coding-agent session over the whole 24 h budget ("Malena"), re-prompted with a short message reporting remaining time whenever the agent ends its turn, with a submission-registry tool, a system-status tool and background-job tools, in place of a harness that picks parents and launches separate sessions (§4.3, B.1).
- claim: Malena reaches "the highest average medal rate" and a significant medal-rate advantage over every manual search algorithm (§4.3).
- evidence: Table 5, default29, 24 h, Malena against Best-of-N, UCB1, Greedy, Chain: percentile +5.09 [+1.34, +8.98], +2.38 [−1.06, +5.88], +3.11 [−0.39, +6.71], +4.58 [+0.97, +8.35]; medal rate +11.9, +6.6, +6.6, +7.7, all with intervals above 0. Against Best-of-N by backbone, Table 8: GLM 5.2 +5.52 percentile and +12.0 medal rate, Kimi K3 +4.51 and +10.5, DeepSeek V4 Pro +9.39 and +12.8; DeepSeek V4 Flash +1.15 and +3.3, intervals contain 0.
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 by design. The percentile gap to UCB1 and Greedy is not significant. The comparison sets one session of 24 h against many separate 6 h sessions with a validation-score selector, so the session's gain includes a lower selection gap (F4). Authors evaluating their own design.

### F4 · practice · best-of-k-sampling · E3
- practice: run independent iterations in parallel (Best-of-N) and submit the one with the best validation score that the agent itself reported ("self-select"), against the best hidden test score ("oracle") (§3, §4.2, C.3).
- claim: Best-of-N has "the largest selection gap among all explored search strategies"; strategies to reduce it "should be a priority" (§4.2).
- evidence: Table 6, default29, 24 h, selection gap in percentile points: Best-of-N 6.832 [4.336, 9.634], UCB1 3.973, Greedy 2.788, Chain 2.152, Malena 1.876 [0.805, 3.205]. Best-of-N self-select 64.21 against oracle 71.04.
- effect: hurts
- reader: gaps compared between strategies, with no control that changes only the selector. The paper offers a mechanism, that independent iterations design their own validation splits so their scores are not on one scale, and states it is a hypothesis, not a result (C.3).

### F5 · practice · validation-baseline-iteration-for-best-of-n · E4
- practice: before the independent iterations, run a baseline iteration that builds a fast pipeline under strict validation-split guidelines; later iterations start from its workspace ("BI"). Two other arms: strict validation guidelines in the prompt ("+V"); a harness-made hidden validation split ("HV", reported as a ceiling, not deployable) (C.3).
- claim: BI gives "the smallest gap of all interventions ablated, at the cost of a significantly worse performance ceiling" (C.3).
- evidence: Table 7, default29, 12 h, Oneshot Best-of-N. Self-select percentile / oracle / gap: base 62.56 / 67.99 / 5.433; BI 61.24 / 63.26 / 2.024 [1.125, 2.901]; +V 63.74 / 67.57 / 3.833; HV 61.55 / 66.03 / 4.479.
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 by design but the authors say "at our current statistical power level, we cannot make strong conclusions" (C.3). The self-select score, which is what a user gets, did not improve with BI (61.24 against 62.56).

### F6 · practice · sub-agent-delegation · E4
- practice: enable background sub-agents through the agent's native task tool and encourage their use in the prompt, on top of a Malena session ("+D") (§4.4, B.2).
- claim: "The base Malena iteration performs no significantly worse than any intervention tested" (§4.4).
- evidence: Table 1, fixed14, 24 h, self-select: percentile 63.62 [59.27, 68.26] against 66.51 [62.46, 69.97], medal rate 45.2 [38.1, 54.8] against 55.7 [48.2, 62.9]. Table 10: base against +D +2.88 [−2.94, +8.73] percentile, +10.5 [−1.0, +22.0] medal rate. At 12 h with time nudges, +N against +N+D −2.43 [−7.77, +3.48].
- effect: none
- reader: 14 tasks; point estimates favour no delegation, intervals contain 0. The prompt addition that encourages delegation is part of the treatment.

### F7 · practice · parallel-independent-sessions · E4
- practice: run three independent Malena sessions at once on the same machine ("+P3") (§4.4).
- claim: parallelism "provides a higher ceiling in oracle-selected percentile score", but the selection gap "increases under such setting so much that the final self-select submission is of similar quality" (§4.4).
- evidence: Table 1, fixed14, 24 h: self-select percentile 67.77 [64.58, 70.62] against 66.51; oracle percentile 71.79 [69.34, 73.83] against 69.29; self-select medal rate 52.4 against 55.7. Table 10: base against +P3 −1.26 [−6.09, +3.21] percentile, +3.3 [−8.2, +14.9] medal rate.
- effect: none
- reader: the three sessions share one GPU, which the authors name as a risk of hardware contention (§4.4). Differences are inside the intervals.

### F8 · practice · message-channel-between-parallel-agents · E4
- practice: with three parallel sessions, give each a broadcast tool; messages queue and arrive when the recipient's turn ends ("+B+P3") (§4.4).
- claim: the paper reports no gain; the arm is the lowest in Table 1 (§4.4).
- evidence: Table 1, fixed14, 24 h, self-select percentile 58.91 [53.50, 64.05] against base 66.51; medal rate 33.3 [23.8, 40.5] against 55.7. Table 10: base against +B+P3 +7.59 [+0.99, +13.86] percentile, +22.4 [+10.8, +33.7] medal rate. At 12 h with nudges, +N against +N+B+P3 +2.55 [−2.55, +7.49], intervals contain 0.
- effect: hurts
- reader: the 24 h arm is significant on both metrics; the 12 h arm is not. The authors give no mechanism and call coordination "an open question" (§4.4).

### F9 · practice · periodic-elapsed-time-nudges · E4
- practice: queue a short message to the session at 25%, 50%, 75% and 90% of the time budget that reports elapsed time ("+N") (C.6, B.2).
- claim: "the addition of the time nudges results in a performance loss, perhaps due to a small distraction effect of unexpected messages" (C.6).
- evidence: Table 9, default29, 24 h: self-select percentile 63.85 [59.73, 67.77] against 69.30 [66.74, 71.78]; medal rate 48.9 against 59.4. Table 10: base against +N +5.46 [+0.70, +10.67] percentile, +10.6 [+3.7, +17.9] medal rate.
- effect: hurts
- reader: the cause is a guess. The first nudge at 6 h falls on the Oneshot time limit, and the base session already gets a remaining-time line each time it ends a turn (B.1), so the nudge duplicates information.

### F10 · practice · time-and-resource-status-tool · E4
- practice: give the agent a tool that returns hardware resources, utilisation and remaining time ("S"). The ablation removes it together with the background-jobs tools ("-S-J") (§4.3, C.6).
- claim: "the performance of the agent is negatively impacted when removing the system tool, suggesting that the agent struggles to track time correctly without it" (C.6).
- evidence: Table 9, default29, 24 h: -S-J self-select percentile 65.33 [62.05, 68.61] against 69.30; medal rate 50.6 against 59.4. Table 10: base against -S-J +3.97 [−0.07, +7.97] percentile (interval touches 0), +8.9 [+1.7, +15.9] medal rate. Removing only the jobs tools: 70.47 against 69.30, base against -J −1.17 [−5.64, +3.34], medal rate −2.6 [−10.2, +4.7].
- effect: helps
- reader: the arm removes S and J together, and J alone changes nothing, so the loss is attributed to S by subtraction. Percentile is borderline; medal rate is significant.

### F11 · practice · resource-utilisation-nudge · E4
- practice: queue a message when CPU or GPU utilisation falls under 50%, at most one per 5 minutes ("R"), on top of time nudges (C.6, B.2).
- claim: none stated beyond "None of the interventions provide significant performance improvements" (C.6).
- evidence: Table 9, fixed14, 12 h: +N+R self-select percentile 51.08 [45.76, 55.70] against +N 58.84 [55.58, 62.11]; medal rate 30.4 against 40.3. Table 10: +N against +N+R +7.76 [+1.67, +13.87] percentile, +10.0 [−0.9, +20.4] medal rate.
- effect: hurts
- reader: the baseline already carries the nudge that F9 shows is harmful, so this compares one harmful addition with two. Percentile is significant, medal rate is not.

### F12 · practice · ml-strategy-skills-on-demand · E4
- practice: give the session a skill tool with a one-line description of each of eight ML-engineering strategies and load a skill body only when the agent calls it ("s"); the skills cover baseline-first, ablation probes, runtime estimates, stagnation pivots and cross-iteration synthesis (B.2, C.6).
- claim: "None of the interventions provide significant performance improvements at our current statistical power"; further interventions "either hurt performance, or do not produce significant improvements" (C.6).
- evidence: Table 9, fixed14, 12 h: +N+s self-select percentile 58.05 [54.68, 61.02] against +N 58.84; medal rate 38.1 against 40.3. Table 10: +N against +N+s +0.79 [−3.86, +5.24]. Bundle +N+D+H+V: 63.10 against 58.84, +N against bundle −4.26 [−9.16, +0.98].
- effect: none
- reader: 14 tasks, one run of the skills' wording, and the baseline carries the nudge from F9. Whether the agent loaded any skill is not stated in the parts read.

### F13 · practice · constrained-context-with-compaction · E4
- practice: cap the session's context at 64k tokens so that OpenCode's summarisation compaction fires, against the native 1M window with GLM 5.2 (C.7).
- claim: "Malena is sensitive to operating near a hard context ceiling, though the point estimates remain within overlapping 95% CIs" (C.7).
- evidence: Table 12, fixed10 (10 tasks, 4 seeds, n = 40 per arm): self-selected any-medal 50.0% [35.2, 64.8] against 57.5% [42.2, 71.5]; mean percentile 73.5 [65.3, 81.7] against 77.1 [68.0, 86.2]; oracle any-medal 50.0% against 65.0%. 381 compactions over 40 runs, mean 9.5 per run (range 3 to 63); each discards about 76% of the context (C.7).
- effect: hurts
- reader: every interval overlaps, so the claimed drop is a point-estimate direction. The compaction is the agent framework's own routine, so the finding is about that routine at a 64k cap.

### F14 · practice · minimal-harness-vs-published-harnesses · E3
- practice: run the single-session design from F3 and four published open-source MLE harnesses (MLEvolve, AiScientist, Arbor, ScienceFlow) under the same backbone, hardware and 24 h budget, as released (§5, D.1).
- claim: "Malena matches or beats every harness on every tested pair" except at Gemma 4 31B; "none of the published harnesses we tested significantly outperforms the plain agent session with frontier backbones" (§5.1, §6).
- evidence: Table 15, fixed30, self-select percentile / medal rate: GLM 5.2 Malena 69.74 / 62.5%, Arbor 59.26 / 45.8%, AiScientist 61.39 / 47.1%, MLEvolve 54.32 / 39.2%, ScienceFlow 47.59 / 24.4%; Kimi K3 Malena 72.75 / 60.0%, Arbor 68.46 / 60.0%; DeepSeek V4 Flash Malena 58.53 / 44.4%, Arbor 61.23 / 50.0%. Table 16 paired percentile at GLM 5.2: +10.48 over Arbor, +8.36 over AiScientist, +15.42 over MLEvolve, +22.16 over ScienceFlow, all with intervals above 0; at DeepSeek V4 Flash against Arbor −2.71 [−8.26, +2.50]. NatureBench surpassed-SOTA rate, Table 2: GLM 5.2 21.7% against 17.5% (AiScientist) and 10.8% (MLEvolve); Kimi K3 26.7% against 27.1% and 11.7%. 17 harness-backbone pairs on MLE-bench, 6 on NatureBench (§5.1).
- effect: helps
- reader: E3: whole systems are compared, so the gain cannot be assigned to a component, and the evaluators wrote one of the systems. The authors port the baselines to local backbones and fix bugs in them (D.3), and say tuning effort may be asymmetric (§6). Some baselines were built for chat-completion units, which the authors state (§5).

### F15 · practice · single-session-context-resend-cost · E3
- practice: keep one ever-growing session over 24 h, which resends its whole context each turn, in place of harnesses that reset into fresh shorter threads per branch or submission (§5.1).
- claim: the single session costs far more in modeled inference, driven by cache-read tokens (§5.1).
- evidence: Figure 4 and §5.1, GLM 5.2: Malena modeled cost $12.12 against $1.95 for AiScientist, about 6.2 times; input and output token volumes comparable, cache-read volume about an order of magnitude higher. 24 h of A100 80GB compute costs between $24 and $85 depending on provider (§5.1, footnote 3).
- effect: hurts
- reader: the cost is modeled with a flat cache-read price of 0.1 times input, because no harness reports real cache hits (A.4). The comparison names AiScientist only; other harnesses are in a figure. The authors state hardware dominates total cost.

### F16 · observation · harness-gain-shrinks-on-strong-models · E3
- practice: none applied; the single-session design is compared with Best-of-N sessions and with published harnesses on backbones of different strength (§5.1, C.5, D.2).
- claim: "weaker backbones still benefit from hand-crafted workflow priors, but as agentic capability grows, a plain coding agent loop closes the gap" (§1, G.2).
- evidence: Gemma 4 31B, the weakest model: Malena against MLEvolve −10.53 [−14.72, −6.36] percentile, medal rate −4.4 [−10.0, +1.1] (Table 16); Malena against Best-of-N −3.97 [−7.98, −0.35] percentile but +7.9 [+4.3, +11.8] medal rate (Table 8). For comparison GLM 5.2: +15.42 against MLEvolve (Table 16). Malena 32.35 against MLEvolve 42.88 self-select percentile (Table 15).
- effect: mixed
- reader: backbones differ in size, context window (256k for Gemma, C.5) and post-training together, so the cause is not isolated. The authors name context compaction as one possible bottleneck and do not test it on Gemma.

### F17 · practice · reasoning-effort-scaling · E4
- practice: run MLEvolve on DeepSeek V4 Flash at maximum reasoning effort against medium, same 10 tasks, seeds and 24 h timeout (D.3, Table 17).
- claim: lowering to medium "alone is enough to move outcomes substantially" for a harness that issues many short independent calls (D.3).
- evidence: Table 17, any-medal rate 0.0% (max) against 25.0% [10.0, 30.0] (medium); oracle mean percentile 42.6 [36.0, 48.5] against 53.1 [44.9, 59.8]; median reasoning length per call 37,124 against 7,069 characters; mean search nodes per run 18.5 against 62.8; buggy-node rate 61% against 62%.
- effect: hurts
- reader: the effect of max effort is harmful here through fewer completed steps under a fixed wall-clock budget; per-node quality did not change. The medium setting was then adopted as MLEvolve's canonical setting for that backbone only (D.3), so that baseline was tuned after seeing results.

### F18 · practice · bundled-harness-aids-on-single-iteration · E4
- practice: add six harness aids together to a single Oneshot iteration: system and jobs tools, time nudges, HuggingFace model-search MCP, delegation encouragement, strict validation guidelines ("+S+J+N+H+D+V") (C.8, B.2).
- claim: "most interventions provide a modest point-estimate improvement and only H resolving as statistically significant for GLM 5.2"; "only when running all of them together we can observe a larger performance improvement" (C.8).
- evidence: Table 13, default29, per-iteration, GLM 5.2: percentile 57.94 [56.07, 59.77] against base 55.15 [54.31, 55.98]; medal rate 41.8 against 36.8. Table 14: base against bundle −2.80 [−4.83, −0.74], medal rate −5.0 [−8.4, −1.5]; base against +H −1.93 [−3.85, −0.003]; +S+J, +S+J+N, +D, +V intervals contain 0.
- effect: helps
- reader: the one place the paper shows a harness gain, on a single iteration and a single backbone. The same additions on the 24 h session did not help (F9, F11, F12), which is the paper's contrast between Oneshot and Malena (C.6).

### F19 · practice · minimal-task-prompt · E4
- practice: simplify the Oneshot prompt to the crucial information, removing general guidelines and examples ("+I") (B.2, C.8).
- claim: the paper lists it among interventions that give a point-estimate improvement and does not discuss it separately (C.8).
- evidence: Table 13, DeepSeek V4 Pro (Preview), default29, per-iteration: percentile 43.72 [41.94, 45.42] against base 39.63 [38.10, 41.11]; medal rate 23.9 against 20.3. Table 14: base against +I −4.10 [−6.46, −1.74], medal rate −3.6 [−7.3, −0.2].
- effect: helps
- reader: one backbone, a Preview model whose Chat-versus-Oneshot gap was close to zero (G.2), and a single iteration; GLM 5.2 was not run with this arm in the parts read.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Most results rely on MLE-bench, which has task-preparation issues and is a plausible contamination target; the authors fixed broken tasks, ran two similarity checks and added NatureBench (§6, G.1).
- Statistical power: 3 to 4 seeds per task at 24 h, 6 to 10 at 6 h; some intervals are too wide for effect sizes, and the authors consider only the no-worse-than claims well supported (G.1).
- Scope: nearly all interventions are tested with a single worker; coordination between agents is left to future work (G.1).
- Possible asymmetry in tuning effort between Malena and the external harnesses (G.1, D.3).
- The explanations in G.2 ("post-training ate the harness") are hypotheses, not results (G.2).
- No-valid-submission runs count as the worst percentile, which gives a few large gaps outsized weight; seed counts per task are uneven (D.2).

## Reader's notes

- The authors built Malena and ran every arm of every ablation; the paper has no outside replication. One baseline setting was chosen after results were seen: MLEvolve's medium reasoning effort on DeepSeek V4 Flash (F17, D.3).
- The session design's "continue" loop is a deterministic stand-in for a human turn: when the agent ends its turn the harness sends a short message that reports the remaining time (B.1). F3 supports it against search rules, but the paper has no arm without that message, so what it contributes is not separated from the long session itself.
- Oneshot's deliverables (main.py, submission.csv, design.md, score.txt) are written "as guarantees": the harness checks the file set at turn end and re-prompts until it holds (B.1). This is a harness gate that is not ablated.
- The outcome is a leaderboard percentile or medal on benchmark tasks, set by hidden test scores the agent never sees (§4.1). Nothing measures code quality, review, or the human's role.
- Intervals are bootstrap over seeds with the task set fixed (A.5); no correction for the many paired comparisons across Tables 5, 8, 10, 14 and 16 is stated in the parts read.
- The technique analysis in §5.2 (Figures 5 and 6) rests on LLM labelling of checkpoints (Appendix E, not read); it is the paper's evidence that the agent "searches on its own", and no number from it is used here.
- Inference cost is modeled from OpenRouter prices with a flat 0.1 cache-read factor (A.4); GPU cost is outside it.
- The headline "no advantage over a minimal harness" holds on MLE-bench and NatureBench with open-weight models served locally; Gemma 4 31B is the stated exception (§5.1).

## Discrepancies

- §5.1 says Malena "matches or beats every harness on every tested pair" and then says the picture changes at Gemma 4 31B; Table 16 shows Malena significantly behind MLEvolve there on percentile (−10.53 [−14.72, −6.36]). §1 states the narrower form, "not outperformed at any frontier backbone".
- The abstract says the open-source harnesses "provide no advantages" over the baseline; Table 15 gives Arbor a higher point estimate than Malena on DeepSeek V4 Flash (61.23 against 58.53 percentile, 50.0% against 44.4% medal rate), not significant (Table 16).
- §4.3 and C.4 refer to the validation-gap result as Appendix C.2 and C.3; the selection-gap table (Table 6) sits in C.2 and the mitigation table (Table 7) in C.3, so the pointers are loose but not wrong.
