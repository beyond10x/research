## Question

Who has instruction files for coding agents, what do they contain, how are they expressed, where
does their content come from and what does it vary with (§1)? The paper makes no claim about the
effect of the files on agent behaviour (§2).

## Findings

The paper reports prevalences and associations among files and project traits. Nothing was assigned
as a treatment, so every finding is an observation. The `effect:` of each is the direction of the
association (higher share or odds with the condition), not an effect on agent work. Descriptive
results with no comparison are in Reader's notes.

### F1 · observation · instruction-file-adoption-by-popularity · E3
- practice: share of repositories with a root AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md by star tier and by primary language, census of 22,854 (§4.1, Tables 4 and 16).
- claim: "adoption rises with popularity in every tier" (§4.1).
- evidence: Table 16, either file: 23.2% of 16,619 repositories with 1k to 5k stars, 31.2% of 3,087 with 5k to 10k, 37.0% of 3,059 with 10k to 100k, 59.6% of the 89 with 100k+. Overall 6,006 of 22,854 (26.3%). By language (Table 4): TypeScript 40.8% (4,048 repositories), Rust 32.7%, Go 28.9%, Python 24.3%, JavaScript 17.5%, C 13.0% (1,272).
- effect: helps
- reader: a comparison between groups with no control, so E3; the frame is popularity and activity, not agent use. Stars can be gamed (§5). Adoption is read as presence of a root file at one date, and Claude Code started reading AGENTS.md four weeks before the snapshot (§5).

### F2 · observation · llm-and-agent-projects-among-adopters · E3
- practice: project type of adopters against matched repositories without agent files, matched within star tier and language (§4.5, Table 14).
- claim: "Projects about LLMs, generative AI or agents are 27.9% of adopters against 13.3% of matched controls" (§4.5).
- evidence: odds ratio 2.76 (95% CI 2.48 to 3.08) within star tier and language, 4,699 controls (Table 14). Post hoc, with age and activity held fixed and agent-tooling projects excluded, 1.54 (1.28 to 1.84) (§4.5). Library or framework 0.58 (0.53 to 0.64), docs or learning 0.50 (0.39 to 0.62) (Table 14).
- effect: helps
- reader: matching on two variables is not a control for what the project does or how old it is; the post hoc check shows the odds ratio nearly halves under age and activity. Project type was classified by Haiku from repository metadata and agrees with the human reviewer at kappa 0.43 (0.23 to 0.63) on 29 repositories, AI focus at 0.80 (Table 15), so the paper calls type contrasts indicative.

### F3 · observation · compiled-language-build-instructions · E3
- practice: whether compiled-language repositories more often state how to build and run the project (prespecified H3, §4.5).
- claim: hypothesis supported.
- evidence: odds ratio 1.68 (1.48 to 1.91), 5,587 repositories, Benjamini-Hochberg over 11 tests; holds on the Sonnet sample and with either of the two largest languages left out (§4.5, Table 2).
- effect: helps
- reader: the hypotheses were committed in git after the descriptive results had been inspected (§3.4, A.1.6), so the direction may have been informed by the data. Outcome is Haiku's uncorrected statement codes; Haiku agrees with Sonnet at kappa 0.47 to 0.81 (§3.3). Still observational; language is not assigned.

### F4 · observation · popularity-and-pr-and-ai-policy-rules · E3
- practice: whether more-starred repositories more often have rules on pull requests or AI contribution policy (prespecified H6, §4.5).
- claim: hypothesis supported.
- evidence: odds ratio 1.36 (1.29 to 1.44) per standard deviation of log stars, 5,587 repositories (Table 2). Collaboration rules name concrete parts: test plan required in 27.2% of files with a pull-request-description rule, Conventional Commits in 36.7% of files with a commit-message rule (Table 1, Haiku codes, uncorrected).
- effect: helps
- reader: cross-sectional; stars may track project age, size and review culture, which the model only partly controls (age, language and star tier are controls, but stars are the predictor here so tier is dropped, §3.4). The detail on what rules require is E2 description, and is not graded here.

### F5 · observation · ai-marked-share-and-agent-rules · E3
- practice: whether repositories with a larger share of AI-marked commits have more rules for the agent, more permission or escalation rules and more prohibitions (prespecified H1, §4.5).
- claim: "not supported", with "small significant effects against the prediction" (§4.5).
- evidence: Table 2, incidence rate ratios per standard deviation: more agent rules 0.977 (0.959 to 0.996), permission or escalation rules 0.992 (0.963 to 1.021), prohibitions 0.981 (0.965 to 0.997).
- effect: none
- reader: effect sizes are near 1 with 5,587 repositories, so significance at this n carries little. AI-marked commits capture one tool (88.0% of adopters with an AI-marked commit have a Claude marker) and are depressed where projects ban trailers (§4.4, §5); a post hoc check removed 141 repositories whose files prohibit AI attribution (A.11), the result of which is not in the text read.

### F6 · observation · emphasis-and-failure-mode-rules · E3
- practice: whether repositories that use more typographic or severity emphasis have more rules on agent failure modes (prespecified H4, §4.5).
- claim: "not supported" (§4.5).
- evidence: odds ratio 0.93 (0.88 to 0.99) per standard deviation of emphasis rate (Table 2), the opposite direction to the hypothesis. Emphasis and severity labels are in a minority of directives (§4.3, Figure 3; values in the figure only).
- effect: none
- reader: same limits as F5. This is about how often emphasis co-occurs with a content topic, not about whether emphasis changes agent behaviour, which the paper says "remains open" (§6).

### F7 · observation · project-activity-and-instruction-content · E3
- practice: content coverage of the instruction set against commits in the past year, age and other repository traits, 99 exploratory tests (§4.5).
- claim: "Activity is associated with the largest number of tested content outcomes" (§4.5).
- evidence: 49 of 99 tests pass the false-discovery correction; commits in 12 months 11 of 13 tests (Table 13). Per standard deviation of log commits, odds of covering version control 1.50, lower for project knowledge 0.77. Older repositories cover testing more, 1.37 per standard deviation of age (§4.5).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the paper calls these "exploratory leads", with false-discovery control within the family only (§5). The 99 tests were planned before computing but are not a confirmatory set. Activity counts may include commits made by agents (§5). Direction is more of one content and less of another.

### F8 · observation · claude-md-more-descriptive-than-agents-md · E3
- practice: within one repository that keeps two files with content of their own, the share of descriptive statements in CLAUDE.md against AGENTS.md (prespecified H5, §4.5).
- claim: hypothesis supported.
- evidence: +6.3 percentage points (4.2 to 8.4), Wilcoxon p = 1.3e-7, 484 repositories (Table 2, A.1.6).
- effect: helps
- reader: a within-repository paired comparison, which removes between-project differences, but it is not an assignment of file name to content. The hypothesis was informed by a pooled difference already seen (§3.4, A.11), so it is not independent of the data; the paper says the percentage-point estimate is post hoc.

### F9 · observation · edits-after-creation-of-instruction-files · E3
- practice: whether instruction sets carrying a generator signature (the opening line of claude /init or the headings of Codex /init) are edited after creation as often as sets without one (§4.4).
- claim: "Those with generator signatures were edited about as often as those without" (§4.4).
- evidence: 82.4% against 81.7%, "neither share is adjusted for age" (§4.4). Overall 4,860 of 5,942 sets (81.8%) have more than one commit (Table 8). 948 of 5,998 sets (15.8%) have a generator signature, 692 (11.5%) the claude /init line (Table 8).
- effect: none
- reader: no model, test or interval is given for the 82.4% against 81.7%. Signature detection is uneven: claude /init leaves a verbatim line, Codex /init only headings and other tools none, so the signed group is mostly Claude Code output (§4.4). Edited here means one more commit, which includes a rename or a bridge conversion (A.5). Sets whose generated text was later fully rewritten are not signed.

### F10 · observation · model-agreement-exceeds-agreement-with-human-coder · E3
- practice: agreement of three models (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus 5.5) with each other against agreement with one human reader on the same statements, used to annotate subject, kind and function of statements (§3.3, A.2).
- claim: "The models agree much more closely with each other than with the human reviewer's topic assignments, a caution for automated annotation" (Abstract); "agreement among models alone therefore offers limited assurance" (§6).
- evidence: Sonnet and Opus kappa 0.76 to 0.89 by dimension on 43 validation files (2,151 statements); Haiku with Sonnet 0.47 to 0.81 (§3.3, A.1.5). Human blind reading of 60 statements: description against rule agreement 82 to 83%, three kinds 62 to 67% (kappa 0.33 to 0.40), subject area 43 to 50%, subject 32 to 37% (Table 3). Against the earlier study's coders, kappa 0.67 to 0.80 between models and 0.46 to 0.52 with humans (A.2).
- effect: hurts
- reader: the human reviewer is the paper's author, a single coder who stopped at 60 of the 100 drawn statements, so human-human agreement is unknown (A.2). The comparison is between groups of different size and no control, E3. The same model family that wrote the codebook also applied it. Applies to any label set produced by an LLM and checked by another LLM, including stages 2 and 3 of this repository's pipeline.

### F11 · observation · instruction-type-mix-against-earlier-study · E3
- practice: the 16 instruction types of an earlier study applied to the 6,578 files of this census, against that study's human coding of its own files (A.7, Table 9).
- claim: "calibrated ... so that our population can be compared with theirs" (§3.2); the table's verdicts say 7 types are more common here, 2 less, 6 similar or unclear (Table 9).
- evidence: corrected share, here against the earlier study: AI Integration 71% against 22%, Documentation and References 72% against 29%, Development Process 91% against 68%; Testing 67% against 83%, Build and Run 49% against 71%; Architecture 70% against 71% (Table 9).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the two samples differ in frame (popular and active against AI-development repositories) and date, so, in the paper's words, "a difference shows that the populations differ, not why" (Table 9). The labeller is an LLM calibrated on 324 human-labelled files and corrected with the Rogan-Gladen estimator; intervals are wide for some types (Maintenance 55 to 100).

## Limits stated by the authors

- The study measures presence and content of root AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md files, not agent use or the instructions agents receive at run time. Nested agent files are excluded; other formats are counted only in RQ1 (§5).
- AI-marked commits capture part of AI-assisted work, depend on whether a tool adds a trailer and are depressed in projects that ban trailers; project type agrees only moderately with a human reading (§5).
- Content categories were discovered and applied by language models and diverge from the human reviewer's reading, including on broad areas. The review covers 60 statements and describes that sample only (§5).
- Generator signatures and shared text are evidence of possible reuse, with unknown false-positive rates (§5).
- The models analysed files of their vendor's format and may treat Claude-specific conventions differently; this could not be tested (§5).
- History covers only files that survive at the snapshot, and path history misses renames. All correlations are cross-sectional. Claude Code began reading AGENTS.md four weeks before the snapshot, so recent shares may reflect it (§5).
- Popular, recently active repositories in thirteen languages on one date; stars can be gamed. Small effects become significant at almost six thousand repositories, and the exploratory results are leads (§5).

## Reader's notes

- Descriptive results left out of the findings for want of a direction, with their numbers. Adoption: 2,566 repositories have only AGENTS.md, 1,052 only CLAUDE.md, 2,388 both; of those 1,540 have CLAUDE.md defer to AGENTS.md, 182 the reverse, 598 keep two files with their own content (§4.1). AGENTS.md-first among new adopters rose from 23.9% (2025-01 to 2025-08) to 60.0% (2026-05 to 2026-10-16) (Table 7); in the 29 days around Claude Code's AGENTS.md support, 60.8% before and 68.6% after, which the paper reads as the existing trend (A.5). Content: median 818 words; 98.2% have a rule; statements are 52.6% rules, 29.8% descriptions, 17.6% references (§4.2). Verification before declaring work done is a rule subject in 49.7% of repositories, test writing 48.2%; code conventions 74.4%, build and delivery 72.6%, agent workflow 67.3% (§4.2, Table 12). Test-writing rules prohibit trivial tests in 8.1% of files and skipping or weakening tests in 4.9% (Table 19). Expression: 80.9% mark a rule mandatory, 77.8% prohibit something, 82.9% give a reason at least once but 74.0% of directives carry none, and 10.8% cite an incident or evidence (§4.3). AI attribution: 7.5% of repositories prohibit it in commit-message rules, 59 require it; 5.8% require disclosure in pull-request rules (§4.2). Origin: 33.1% of sets carry a trace of a generator, a tool block, shared text or an AI-marked creation commit; 1.6% hold a tool-written block, 44 of them from the Next.js development server (§4.4).
- Every statement, kind, subject and project type here was labelled by Claude models, and the files are in the vendor's format (§5). The one human check is the author on 60 statements. The headline census shares in §4.2 and §4.3 use Sonnet on a weighted sample of 992 repositories; the element tables and the correlations use Haiku's uncorrected codes (§3.3).
- The author used Claude Code for analysis code and drafting and OpenAI models for revision (AI-use statement). The author does not evaluate a system of their own, hence `independent: true`.
- The generator-signature share (15.8%) mostly measures claude /init; the paper states no false-positive rate (§4.4, §5).
- The paper says nothing on whether files with reasons, mandatory wording or concrete requirements make agents succeed more. Its shares describe what projects write, not what works; they bear on the sample of files that controlled studies such as 2602.11988 draw on, since the paper notes that a file can mix tool text, reused guidelines and later edits (§6).

## Discrepancies

- §3.1 says 6,578 files are coded; A.1.5 says 341,852 statements in 6,577 files.
- §3.3 uses 992 sample repositories for the headline estimates; A.1.7 says the models are repeated "on the Sonnet sample (927 repositories)". The text read does not say why the counts differ.
- §3.4 says the exploratory models use 5,586 instruction sets; the Table 13 caption says its adopter-versus-control row uses 5,286 adopters, and the text elsewhere has 4,699 controls while Table 10 lists 5,920 controls. None of the three differences is explained in the parts read.
