## Question

Can a coding agent that localizes, plans and verifies a repair on a per-function control-flow or
data-flow graph use fewer tokens than the vanilla agent, without losing task success, and how does that
compare with five recent token-reduction methods (§1, §4.1)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · abstraction-guided-repair · E4
- practice: attach five MCP tools that return a per-function CFG or DFG, and prepend a skill (`/flowfix`) that tells the agent to locate the fault on the graph, state the repair as typed graph deltas before editing, edit, check the rebuilt graph against the plan, and run a final verification before stopping. The native tools stay available and nothing is enforced (§3.2, §3.3, §4.2).
- claim: FlowPlan "reduc[es] token consumption in every setting, significantly in five of six" settings (abstract).
- evidence: ratio of mean total tokens to vanilla, CFG and DFG, Table 3. Haiku 4.5: SWE-bench Pro 0.47 and 0.46 (vanilla 3,715k tokens), Verified 0.32 and 0.35 (vanilla 3,414k), SecBench 0.68 and 0.74 (vanilla 767k). Sonnet 5: Pro 0.72 and 0.76 (vanilla 1,152k), Verified 0.73 and 0.74 (vanilla 803k), SecBench 0.94 and 0.92, not significant (vanilla 681k). 10 of 12 reductions significant, p < 0.001, paired permutation test with Holm correction over 40 comparisons (§5.1, §4.4). Cache reads are 96-98% of vanilla tokens (§5.1).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4: the same instances run with and without, paired tests. Each method was run once per instance (§4.2, §6). The outcome is a token count; cache-read tokens dominate the count and are cheaper than other tokens, so the ratios are not cost ratios and the paper reports no dollar figure. There is no ablation of FlowPlan itself: graph tools, plan-before-edit and verify-before-stop were never run separately, so the saving cannot be assigned to the graph abstraction. The authors built FlowPlan and the evaluation harness.

### F2 · practice · abstraction-guided-repair · E4
- practice: as F1; the outcome is the resolve rate under each benchmark's official harness.
- claim: FlowPlan "does not significantly change resolution rate relative to the vanilla agent" (abstract); "within the power of our evaluation" there is no sign of a systematic gain or loss (§5.1).
- evidence: differences from −4.6 to +4.5 percentage points across the twelve FlowPlan configurations, none significant under McNemar's exact test (§5.1). Haiku, CFG: Pro 43.0 to 46.8, SecBench 29.0 to 30.8, Verified 61.0 to 58.7. Haiku, DFG on Verified 61.0 to 56.4 (Table 3).
- effect: none
- reader: E4 by design. The samples were sized for a 95% confidence level and a 5% margin of error (§4.3), which fixes the precision of a rate, not the power to detect a difference of a few points; "no significant change" is not evidence of no change. One run per instance. Resolution is the benchmark's tests passing.

### F3 · practice · plan-then-verify-before-stop · E3
- practice: the repair is stated as graph deltas before the edit, and the agent stops when `flow_verify` reports that all planned deltas are matched, no function was changed outside the plan and the patch is non-empty. A failed check allows one more edit and one re-plan, a failed verification one corrective edit (§3.3).
- claim: the saving comes from replacing the post-edit loop of re-reading, diff inspection and testing with "an explicit completion criterion" (§5.3).
- evidence: Haiku 4.5, Fig. 5. Vanilla spends 2.42M of 3.71M tokens after the first edit on Pro and 2.19M of 3.41M on Verified, about 31 more model calls each. FlowPlan CFG cuts that phase to 18 and 12 calls and to 0.96M and 0.48M tokens. The post-edit phase is 75% of the total reduction on Pro and 74% on Verified; DFG 75% and 73%. Planning and verification add 2.8 to 3.7 calls and 0.08M to 0.17M tokens per session, under one tenth of what is saved (§5.3). Paper's motivating case: 50 calls and 2.70M tokens against 17 calls and 0.53M, both resolved (§1, Fig. 1).
- effect: helps
- reader: E3: a phase breakdown of FlowPlan against vanilla; no run keeps the graph tools and drops the verify step, or the reverse. The vanilla runs are split at the first edit and FlowPlan calls are assigned to protocol phases by tool call, so the two splits are not the same measurement. Haiku 4.5 only. The paper does not report how often the agent actually followed the policy; it says the policy is advisory and the phases were recorded (§3.3).

### F4 · practice · no-execution-instruction · E4
- practice: reproduce "To Run or Not to Run" (ISSTA 2026): add the released no-execution instruction to the task prompt and withhold project dependencies, so the agent cannot run the program during repair (§2.4).
- claim: No-execution is "the strongest prior method" and "significantly reduces token consumption in all six settings (0.56-0.81x)" (§5.2).
- evidence: token ratios, Table 3. Haiku: Pro 0.81, SecBench 0.68, Verified 0.71. Sonnet: Pro 0.79, SecBench 0.81, Verified 0.56. It used fewer tokens than FlowPlan in two settings, both Sonnet 5 (§5.2). Resolve rates, vanilla to No-execution: Haiku Pro 43.0 to 48.1, SecBench 29.0 to 31.4, Verified 61.0 to 57.3; Sonnet Pro 52.5 to 50.6, SecBench 37.9 to 39.1, Verified 73.9 to 75.2; the paper reports no significant resolution difference for it (§5.2).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 on equal instances, but this is a reproduction by the authors of a competing system, and absolute numbers may differ from the original study (§6). The same low-power resolution comparison as F2 applies. A benchmark's tests can score a patch that was never executed by the agent; the paper does not measure defects outside those tests.

### F5 · practice · trajectory-rewriting-by-second-model · E4
- practice: reproduce AgentDiet (FSE 2026): a second LLM periodically rewrites and compresses the accumulated trajectory; its calls are counted in the tokens (§2.4, §4.4).
- claim: SHERLOC, AgentDiet and SeeRepo "introduce additional ... machinery whose overhead can offset the context they save" (§5.2).
- evidence: token ratio 0.79 to 1.13 (§5.2). Haiku: Pro 0.82, Verified 0.79, SecBench 1.04 not significant. Sonnet: Pro 1.13, SecBench 0.89, Verified 0.93, all not significant (Table 3). On Haiku Pro the input tokens rise from 0.3k to 131.3k per instance (Table 3).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 on equal instances, reproduced by the authors with the original trigger and prompts but inside Claude Code (§6). Two of six settings show a significant saving, and one of them is the only one near FlowPlan's size.

### F6 · practice · localization-stage-before-repair · E4
- practice: reproduce SHERLOC (EMNLP 2026): a separate localization agent runs first and its findings go to the repair agent; both sessions are counted (§2.4).
- claim: as F5, the extra stage offsets the saving (§5.2).
- evidence: token ratio 0.85 to 1.33 (§5.2). Haiku: Pro 0.93 not significant, SecBench 1.33, Verified 0.85. Sonnet: 0.90, 0.86, 0.89, all not significant (Table 3). Cache-write tokens on Haiku Pro 137.4k against 63.6k for vanilla (Table 3).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 by design, reproduction caveat as F4. The SecBench 1.33 is an increase and is not marked as not significant in Table 3.

### F7 · practice · experience-retrieval-and-early-termination · E4
- practice: reproduce EET (ACL 2026 Findings): retrieve prior repair trajectories and use confidence estimates after key events to stop earlier. The experience base uses only instances outside the evaluation sample (§2.4).
- claim: EET "remains close to the baseline (0.90-1.10x)" and reduces tokens significantly only with Haiku 4.5 on the two SWE-bench benchmarks (§5.2).
- evidence: token ratios Haiku 0.90, 1.05, 0.90 and Sonnet 0.99, 0.95, 1.10 for Pro, SecBench, Verified (Table 3). With Sonnet 5 on SWE-bench Pro the resolve rate rises from 52.5% to 63.3%, p = 0.002, the only significant resolution difference in the study (§5.2).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 by design, reproduction caveat as F4. The +10.8 points (reader's arithmetic) is one setting out of 28 reproduced-method resolution comparisons (reader's arithmetic: 5 methods in 6 settings, SeeRepo not run on SecBench), with no correction stated for resolution tests and one run per instance; the paper does not explain it. The paper notes that EET's stopping rule relies on the agent's self-reported confidence, FlowPlan's on a structural check (§2.4).

### F8 · practice · repository-graph-exploration · E4
- practice: reproduce SeeRepo (ASE 2026): an AST-derived repository graph, rendered as images and queried through MCP, replaces part of textual exploration; Python only (§2.4).
- claim: SeeRepo "uses 0.96-1.13x of the baseline" (§5.2).
- evidence: Haiku: Pro 1.11, Verified 1.13, both above vanilla and not marked as not significant; Sonnet: Pro 0.96, Verified 1.08, both not significant (Table 3, Fig. 3). Resolve rate Haiku Pro 43.0 to 44.9, Verified 61.0 to 61.9 (Table 3).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E4 by design. Tokens rose with no resolution gain in the settings where the rise is significant. The authors exposed SeeRepo's graph builder and renderer through MCP and kept its prompting (§2.4); that is an adaptation of the original.

### F9 · observation · post-edit-token-tail · E2
- practice: none applied. Cost was measured on vanilla Claude Code: every model call replays the whole conversation from the prompt cache.
- claim: "Repair cost ... has two drivers: the number of model calls and the context each call replays" (§1); about two-thirds of the vanilla tokens are spent after the first substantive edit (§1, §5.3).
- evidence: Haiku 4.5 on Pro: 3.7M tokens per issue, 98% cache reads, 54 calls (§1, Fig. 5). Cache reads are 96-98% of vanilla tokens in all settings (§5.1). Post-edit share 2.42M of 3.71M on Pro, 2.19M of 3.41M on Verified; on SecBench the baseline is about 15 calls and splits 0.36M and 0.36M of 0.77M (Fig. 5).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E2: a description of one agent and two models. It shows where tokens go; it does not show what the post-edit tests and re-reading buy, so whether they are waste is not measured. "First edit" is placed by the authors for vanilla sessions.

### F10 · observation · agent-stated-repair-plan-accuracy · E2
- practice: none applied. Each Haiku 4.5 FlowPlan session's tool calls were compared with the reference patch, step by step (§5.3, Table 4).
- claim: "The main difficulty is therefore formulating the correct repair at the abstract level"; once a plan exists the edit usually realizes it (§5.3).
- evidence: pooled over 1,090 sessions: the plan matches the reference in 421 (39%) and is partly right in 224 (21%), a different change in 420 (38%); the edit matches the agent's own plan in 989 (91%) and not in 47 (4%). The right function is reached in 842 (77%) and the graph is faithful in 913 (84%), but the graph element the reference changes is pinned in 510 (47%) (Table 4). 19 of 97 SecBench CFG sessions whose plan differs from the reference still resolve (§5.3).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E2: counts with nothing to compare against. The authors say disagreement with the reference does not make a repair wrong, and the 19 of 97 shows alternative repairs; the "different change" counts are therefore an upper bound on planning errors. Judgements against the reference patch were made by the authors, the method of judging is not given in the parts read. Haiku 4.5 only.

### F11 · observation · crash-site-as-fault-location · E2
- practice: none applied. On SecBench the agent starts from the sanitizer's crash report.
- claim: the agent "frequently pins the crash statement reported by the sanitizer even when the repair belongs elsewhere"; this is "locating a failure symptom" and not the code that must change (§5.3).
- evidence: Haiku 4.5, Table 4. SecBench right function in 61% (CFG) and 65% (DFG) of sessions (reader's arithmetic from 89+13 and 92+18 of 169) against 86% to 90% on Pro and 80% to 82% on Verified (§5.3). Graph element pinned correctly in 25% (CFG) and 28% (DFG) on SecBench; plan matches the reference in 31% of both. The paper's explanation, that the reference may change a caller that validates input, is not tested (§5.3).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E2. This is about the abstraction's use on C and C++ security patches, with a different graph extractor (Joern) and fewer calls per task, so benchmark and tool are confounded.

### F12 · observation · intermediate-step-correctness-with-resolution · E3
- practice: none applied. Sessions in which all five protocol steps agree with the reference patch are compared with the others.
- claim: "the protocol's intermediate decisions are meaningful indicators of whether the repair is on track" (§5.3).
- evidence: all steps agree in 243 of 1,090 sessions (22%), 171 of them resolved, 70% (reader's arithmetic). Per benchmark and view, those sessions resolve 53% to 81% against 28% to 46% for the rest (§5.3, Table 4).
- effect: helps
- reader: E3: a grouping, not an intervention. Agreement with the reference patch and resolution both measure closeness to the reference, so they are expected to move together. It does not show that the check inside the loop (flow_check) predicts resolution, which would be usable by the agent at run time; the reference patch is not available then.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Each method is run once per instance, so model nondeterminism may affect outcomes; paired tests mitigate it, repeated runs were too costly (§6).
- The comparison with prior methods depends on reproduction fidelity; some needed integration or language adaptation, and absolute performance may differ from the original studies (§6).
- CFGs and DFGs may omit information needed for some fixes: cross-function context, semantics outside control or data flow, new code. The ordinary tools remain for that (§6).
- Results cover Claude Code, two models from one provider and three benchmarks; other agents, models, reasoning settings, languages or tasks may differ. The sample size does not establish generalization beyond these benchmarks (§6).

## Reader's notes

- Both models ran at low reasoning effort (§4.2). Token savings at higher effort, where output and thinking tokens weigh more, are not measured.
- The FlowPlan arm differs from vanilla in three things at once: added MCP tool descriptions, a skill with a four-phase procedure, and a stop criterion. No arm adds only the procedure without the graph tools, so "abstraction" in the title is not isolated from "plan before you edit and check before you stop".
- Token counts treat cache reads like other tokens. They are the bulk of the count and cost less per token, so a 0.47 ratio of tokens is not a 0.47 ratio of price. FlowPlan's cache-write tokens rise (Table 3); the paper bounds the offset at 1.3% and 1.5% of the cache-read saving on Pro and Verified and 9% to 16% on SecBench (§5.1).
- The paper states Holm correction for the 40 token comparisons only; no correction is stated for the resolution tests (§4.4).
- The no-execution arm saves tokens by removing test runs and lost no significant resolution, while FlowPlan keeps test running available. The paper does not compare FlowPlan with a prompt-only variant of "stop after a self-check".
- The runs were graded on the benchmark's tests. The patches were not reviewed for quality, size or side effects, other than the unplanned-edit check inside flow_verify.
- Table 4 and Fig. 5 cover Haiku 4.5 only (§5.3), the model with the largest savings.

## Discrepancies

- §5.2 says No-execution "uses fewer tokens than FlowPlan in two settings, both with Sonnet 5", and the RQ2 box says "ties No-execution in a fourth": the tie is Haiku 4.5 on SecBench at 0.68 for both, and FlowPlan's DFG is 0.74 there (Table 3). The tie holds for CFG only.
- §5.1 says "Ten of the twelve reductions are statistically significant" and the abstract and conclusion say "five of six settings"; these agree only because CFG and DFG are non-significant together in the one setting (Sonnet 5 on SecBench).
