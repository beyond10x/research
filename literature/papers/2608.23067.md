## Question

Does injecting a matched Agent Skill into a coding agent's prompt improve its success on web
development tasks, is the effect a matter of content or of added prompt length, and which parts of
a helpful Skill carry the gain (§1)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · matched-skill-injection · E4
- practice: put the SKILL.md of a Skill whose declared stack matches the project into the prompt (C1), against no Skill (C0), on the same Web-Bench tasks, workspace, order, greedy decoding and Playwright tests; auxiliary files are mounted in the workspace, not in the prompt (§3).
- claim: injection "reduces mean Pass@2 by 1.3% to 4.2%", lowers task completion depth, and only 17% to 36% of pairs gain (Abstract, §4.1).
- evidence: Table 1, 117 core pairs, N=3, change in Pass@2 in pp with 95% paired-bootstrap CI: Sonnet 4 −4.2 [−6.9, −1.9], GPT-5.1 −1.3 [−3.4, +0.5], Qwen3 Coder 30B −2.3 [−3.4, −1.3], DeepSeek V4 Flash −2.0 [−3.8, −0.2]. Pass@1: −2.8, −1.6, −3.2, −4.1. Task completion depth −0.85, −0.26, −0.47, −0.40. Win/tie/loss share of pairs, Sonnet 30/9/61, GPT-5.1 35/20/45, Qwen 17/35/48, DeepSeek 36/16/48.
- effect: hurts
- reader: an ablation on equal tasks, so E4, on the success outcome. The pairs were judged core by the two authors, with 117 of 1,550 pairs kept, so the Skills were on-stack and the result is for the best case of routing (§3, Limitations). Aggregate Pass@2 varies by 4.4 pp (C0) and 3.6 pp (C1) across the three Sonnet replicates, about the size of Sonnet's effect (Limitations). The GPT-5.1 Pass@2 CI includes zero. Greedy decoding with N=3 is replication of a near-deterministic run.

### F2 · practice · matched-skill-injection · E4
- practice: as F1; the measurement is total token use.
- claim: injection "increases token cost by 72% to 394%" (Abstract); "Injecting a long Skill at session start, on average, reduces reliability while raising cost" (§5).
- evidence: Table 1, relative total-token overhead C1 against C0: Sonnet +72%, GPT-5.1 +74%, Qwen +91%, DeepSeek +394%. The authors say the DeepSeek figure is "amplified by early C0 failures that shrink its denominator" (§4.1). Removing the example code of a Skill saves 34,482 input tokens per run on average (§4.5).
- effect: hurts
- reader: the cost rise comes from the same paired comparison as F1. The 394% is not comparable to the other three, by the authors' own account. Cost is tokens, not money or latency (Limitations).

### F3 · practice · length-matched-irrelevant-skill · E4
- practice: replace the target Skill by an irrelevant "skip-tier" Skill of about equal byte length (±5%) (C2), to split the C1 effect into a length part (C2 − C0) and a content part (C1 − C2) (§3).
- claim: two failure modes: Sonnet and Qwen are "length-distracted" (an equally long irrelevant Skill reproduces most of the loss), GPT-5.1 and DeepSeek are "content-misled" (length neutral, content lowers Pass@2 by 1.1% to 1.4%) (Abstract, §4.3).
- evidence: Table 3, change in Pass@2 in pp, total, length, content, share of C1 wins that stay positive: Sonnet −4.2, −3.3 (CI excludes zero), −0.9 (CI includes zero), 60%; Qwen −2.3, −3.5, +1.2, 95%; GPT-5.1 −1.3, −0.2, −1.1, 71%; DeepSeek −2.0, −0.6, −1.4, 64%. CIs for GPT-5.1 and DeepSeek length terms include zero (§4.3).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the control is a real ablation on equal tasks, so E4. Content terms of three of the four models have CIs that include zero in the text read, so the "content-misled" group rests on small differences next to the seed spread of F1. The C2 prompts are 109 unique runs across 117 pairs because some are shared, and no cluster bootstrap was done (Limitations).

### F4 · observation · skill-effect-by-task-difficulty · E3
- practice: none applied; the C1 − C0 effect is split by Web-Bench's own difficulty label of each task (§4.2).
- claim: injection "incurs its largest and most reliable losses on the tasks the model already handles correctly", the easy ones; the paper tests the opposite hypothesis, that Skills help most on challenging late tasks, and does not find it (§4.2).
- evidence: Table 2, change in Pass@2 in pp for easy: Sonnet −5.3 [−10.0, −0.7], GPT-5.1 −4.0 [−7.8, −0.4], Qwen −10.7 [−16.7, −5.4], DeepSeek −7.3 [−14.6, −0.5]; every easy CI excludes zero. Moderate: Sonnet +3.3, GPT-5.1 −7.5, DeepSeek +11.7 [+0.4, +24.3]. Challenging: Sonnet −2.6, GPT-5.1 +6.6, DeepSeek +0.8; all moderate and challenging CIs except DeepSeek moderate include zero. Qwen has fewer than five pair-cells outside easy.
- effect: mixed
- reader: a comparison between difficulty buckets with no assignment, so E3. Moderate and challenging cells are small and noisy, as the authors say, so "no loss on hard tasks" is absence of evidence. Because the chain is sequential, a task's position and the earlier failures change which tasks are scored at all.

### F5 · practice · matched-skill-injection · E1
- practice: as F1, one trace: Sonnet 4 on the zustand project with react-expert (S07). Pass@2 falls from 55% to 40% (−15 pp), from a single seed whose chain ends at task 4 (App. A).
- claim: "retry lock-in": a Skill that fixes naming choices "converts recoverable first-attempt mistakes into chain-terminating failures" and reduces self-repair flexibility (§4.2, App. A).
- evidence: both conditions fail the first attempt with the same Playwright strict-mode locator collision. Without the Skill the retry relabels the submit button to "Submit" and the chain continues to task 11; with the Skill the retry uses "Create Blog Post", which still matches the heading as a substring, and fails again (App. A, Figure 1).
- effect: hurts
- reader: one seed of one pair. The mechanism is shown in one trace and not measured over the 117 pairs, so it is a hypothesis, not a result. It is the explanation the authors offer for the F4 pattern.

### F6 · practice · matched-skill-injection · E1
- practice: as F1, one trace: Sonnet 4 on the pull-loading project with js-dom-web-components (S31) (App. B).
- claim: a Skill's content can produce a gain far above the model-level average; "the decomposition describes tendencies rather than per-pair guarantees" (App. B).
- evidence: Pass@2 0% on all three seeds without the Skill, 18% with S31, 0% with the length-matched control, so +18 pp content effect with no length effect. With the Skill the retry adds text inside the empty wrapper and an explicit visibility rule, which satisfies Playwright's `toBeVisible()` (App. B, Figure 2).
- effect: helps
- reader: one pair, picked by the authors as an illustration. Shows that the C2 control can separate a content win from a length effect on one pair; says nothing about how often that happens, which F1 puts at 17% to 36% of pairs.

### F7 · observation · skill-utility-across-models · E3
- practice: none applied; the per-pair C1 − C0 effects of the four models are correlated over the 117 core pairs (§4.4).
- claim: "a Skill's measured utility on one model barely predicts its utility on another"; static rankings and validating on a frontier model for deployment on a cheaper one are unsafe (§4.4, Table 4 caption).
- evidence: Table 4, Pearson r of per-pair change in Pass@2 between model pairs from −0.08 to +0.12 (|r| ≤ 0.12; Spearman |ρ| ≤ 0.16). 74% of pairs have at least one positive and one negative model sign, 1% gain on all four, 4% lose on all four. One pair, lowdb with database-optimizer (S14): Sonnet 33% to 67% (+33 pp), GPT-5.1 22% to 20%, DeepSeek 42% to 20% (−22), Qwen 22% to 0% (−22) (§4.4, Table 6).
- effect: mixed
- reader: a between-model comparison, E3. With N=3 and greedy decoding, per-pair estimates are noisy (seed spread, F1), and noise alone lowers a correlation toward zero, so near-zero r is not proof that the true per-pair effects are uncorrelated. The paper reports no noise ceiling for r.

### F8 · practice · anti-pattern-rules-in-skill · E4
- practice: leave out the anti-pattern ("don't") slice of a Skill's SKILL.md, keeping the header and the other slices (C3 leave-one-out), on five pairs that already gain; contribution = C1 − variant (§4.5, App. D).
- claim: "anti-patterns (Rn) are the only slice with a directionally reliable task-level effect (p=0.008)"; cheap proscriptive content is the most cost-effective signal (§4.5).
- evidence: Table 5, contribution to Pass@2 in pp: all 20 model×project cells +3.1 [−3.3, 9.8]; without Sonnet (15 cells) +6.1 [0.3, 12.9]. Pooled McNemar discordants b=111, c=74, p=0.008 (App. D). The whole Skill on the same cells is +5.1 [1.0, 9.3] and +6.1 [1.6, 10.9].
- effect: helps
- reader: leave-one-out is an ablation, so E4. The five pairs were chosen because they gain on at least two of four models in the C1 run, so the gross effect is positive by selection and the slice contributions are probably inflated by it (the authors say the +5.1 must not be read as Skills helping on average, App. D). The all-cell CI includes zero; the p=0.008 is a task-level McNemar test over attempts, which treats tasks of one run as independent. Dropping Sonnet is a post-hoc subset, 15 of 20 cells. The text read does not give the size of each slice, so the tokens removed per variant are unknown except for X.

### F9 · practice · example-code-in-skill · E4
- practice: leave out the fenced example-code slice (X) of a Skill's SKILL.md; same C3 protocol as F8 (§4.5).
- claim: example code is "expensive and model-dependent"; "Example-heavy Skills should thus not be a default" (§4.5).
- evidence: Table 5, contribution in pp: all 20 cells −0.7 [−5.6, 3.6]; without Sonnet +4.2 [2.0, 6.8], Wilcoxon p=0.005, the most significant slice effect (App. D). Per model, in Figure 4: DeepSeek +8.3, Qwen +3.7, GPT-5.1 +0.7, Sonnet −15.3 (§4.5). Removing X saves 34,482 input tokens per run, about 22.7% of the full SKILL.md budget (§4.5).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 by design, with the F8 limits: five selected pairs, 5 pairs × 4 models, one model (Sonnet) carries the negative sign, and the sign-by-model split is read from a figure and a post-hoc subset. "Strongest model" for Sonnet 4 is the authors' wording and is not defined; Sonnet 4 is older than GPT-5.1 and DeepSeek-V4.

### F10 · practice · positive-rules-in-skill · E4
- practice: leave out the positive-rules ("do" conventions) slice (Rp) of a Skill's SKILL.md; same C3 protocol as F8 (§4.5).
- claim: positive rules "are neutral" (§4.5).
- evidence: Table 5, contribution in pp: all 20 cells +0.0 [−5.1, 4.9]; without Sonnet +4.6 [1.1, 8.4].
- effect: none
- reader: E4 by design with the F8 limits. The without-Sonnet interval excludes zero, so "neutral" holds only for the pooled cells; the text does not comment on the +4.6.

### F11 · practice · skip-skill-on-early-tasks · E0
- practice: skip the Skill on early tasks of a chain and inject one only once error rates rise; inject Skills only when a pair-level signal crosses a utility threshold (§5).
- claim: this contradicts "the common pattern of attaching Skills before any task begins" and is "a reasonable heuristic" (§5).
- evidence: none. The heuristic was not run; it is drawn from F4.
- effect: helps
- reader: asserted, E0. F4's easy-task losses are the only basis, and F4 itself is E3 on a sequential chain whose task difficulty label is fixed in advance, while an agent in practice does not know in advance which task will be hard.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Seed spread: across three Sonnet replicates aggregate C0 and C1 Pass@2 vary by 4.4 and 3.6 pp, comparable to the headline effect, so pair-level estimates need caution (Limitations).
- C2 uses 109 unique length-matched runs across 117 pairs; a cluster bootstrap and per-pair de-duplication are open (Limitations).
- C1 runs only on the 117 core pairs, so off-target deployment is not covered (Limitations).
- The Skills come from high-visibility public repositories; closed enterprise Skills or fine-tuned routers may differ (Limitations).
- A pre-deployment benchmark, not an online A/B test: no live traffic, human intervention or product acceptance criteria (Limitations).
- Functional correctness by Playwright only, and token overhead instead of latency; gains in readability, accessibility, design or review time are not measured (Limitations).
- C3 uses five positive-tail pairs, so it says how a helpful Skill is built, not how often Skills help (§4.5, App. D).

## Reader's notes

- The routing and the core/skip labels were made by the two authors, who also designed the benchmark; Cohen's κ ≈ 0.74 between them, and 55 disagreements went to discussion (§3). A skip label was the default for unresolved pairs.
- Agent harness, tool set, and how a "Skill" is read by the agent (whether the agent can open the mounted `.skills/` files) are not described in the parts read. The mounted auxiliary files are therefore not part of what was tested for effect.
- Models were run at temperature 0 with 3 replicates (§3). The paper does not say where the run-to-run variation comes from, and the Sonnet seed spread is large (Limitations).
- Pass@2 is two attempts per task (§4.2), so the retry is part of the effect in F1 and F5.
- Sonnet 4 (May 2025 snapshot) is not the newest model on the panel; the finding that length hurts it most may not carry to later models.
- No finding here tests a Skill the agent loads on demand: every Skill is in the prompt from the start, which is the paper's stated deployment pattern, and the loading policy of the vendor tools is not varied.
- The 34,482 input tokens per run saved by removing the examples (§4.5) is larger than any SKILL.md in the suite (up to 22K characters, §3), so it is a sum over the turns of a run, and the "22.7% of the full SKILL.md budget" cannot be reproduced from the text.

## Discrepancies

- Abstract and §1 give effects as "1.3% to 4.2%" and "1.1% to 1.4%"; Table 1 and Table 3 give these in percentage points of Pass@2.
- §4.5 calls anti-patterns "the only slice with a directionally reliable task-level effect (p=0.008)", while Table 5's CI for the pooled anti-pattern effect, +3.1 [−3.3, 9.8], includes zero; the p-value is a different test (task-level McNemar, App. D) and unit than the cell-level CI.
- §4.5 says the example slice is "null on average (−0.7 pp)" and then that it is "the strongest and most significant of all" without Sonnet (+4.2 pp); both are stated, from different cell sets.
- §4.5 says the whole Skill "raises Pass@2 by +5.1 pp", opposite in sign to §4.1's average; the authors explain this by selection of five positive-tail pairs (App. D).
- §4.4 says GPT-5.1 is "unchanged" on lowdb with database-optimizer; Table 6 gives 22% to 20%, −2 pp.
