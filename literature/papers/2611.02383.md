## Question

How are agent instruction files in four formats adopted, how consistent are they when several
formats coexist in one repository, and how are they maintained (§1)? From the answers, derive a
category template for instruction files and test whether template guidance changes what LLMs
generate (§1, §5, §6).

## Findings

### F1 · practice · template-guided-instruction-generation · E4
- practice: ask GPT-4o-mini to generate an instruction file with a category checklist derived from the 16-category template, against the same model, base prompt and repository inputs without the checklist; 62 evaluation files (§6.2, §6.4).
- claim: "explicit category guidance is associated with broader coverage of the information identified by our empirical template"; the authors call the comparison exploratory (§6.4).
- evidence: structural coverage, the share of the 16 template categories present in a generated file, without against with the template (Table 5): all categories 0.187 against 0.828, Required (10) 0.168 against 0.737, Gap (4) 0.080 against 0.977, Recommended (2) 0.500 against 0.990. Categories per file 3.00 against 13.25 (§6.4). Domain models appear in 31% of the guided files, repository structure in 55% (Table 6).
- effect: helps
- reader: an A/B with one varied element, so E4 by design. The outcome is the presence of the categories the checklist names, so a rise is close to what the prompt asks for; the paper does not say how a category is detected in generated text (not stated in the parts read). One run per condition, no test, and nothing about whether the files help an agent (§9). The authors built the template and the benchmark.

### F2 · practice · retrieved-examples-in-instruction-generation · E4
- practice: add three retrieved instruction-file examples from the repository-disjoint training split (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 cosine ranking) to the template-guided GPT-4o-mini prompt, against the same prompt with no examples (§6.2).
- claim: RAG "achieves the highest full-file ROUGE-1 F1 (0.459), compared with 0.423 for zero-shot"; zero-shot has the higher structural coverage (§6.4, §7.3).
- evidence: on the same 62 files. Structural coverage 0.786 with RAG against 0.828 zero-shot (§6.4). Full-file ROUGE-1 F1 0.459 against 0.423, cosine similarity 0.683 against 0.681; overlap-only ROUGE-1 F1 0.433 against 0.386, cosine 0.708 against 0.710 (Table 7).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the two arms differ only by the retrieved examples, so E4 by design. The differences are 0.036 in ROUGE and 0.002 in cosine, single run, no interval or test. Both metrics compare generated text with the developer text after GPT-4o-mini turned both into generic intents (§6.1), and the same model family generates and normalises.

### F3 · practice · fine-tuned-instruction-generator · E4
- practice: adapt CodeLlama-13B to instruction-file generation with QLoRA (4-bit NF4, rank 8, scaling 16, dropout 0.05, learning rate 1e-4, five epochs, trained on the training split), against the same model unadapted, both template-guided with greedy decoding and at most 1,536 output tokens (§6.2).
- claim: "QLoRA configuration achieves higher semantic alignment than CodeLlama Base despite its substantially lower structural coverage"; "stronger alignment within generated content does not imply that a model represents the complete template" (§6.4).
- evidence: full-file cosine similarity 0.608 against 0.373, ROUGE-1 F1 0.273 against 0.304; overlap-only cosine 0.647 against 0.434, ROUGE-1 F1 0.347 against 0.309 (Table 7). Structural coverage 0.166 against 0.645 (§6.4); Gap tier 0.044, Maintenance 0.00, Ownership 0.02 (§6.4, Table 6).
- effect: mixed
- reader: an adapted model against its base on the same files, E4 by design. Cosine rises, ROUGE falls in the full-file setting, and category presence collapses; which metric matters depends on the use. The training files and the references are the same kind of text, so the cosine gain may reflect style. Single run.

### F4 · observation · generated-instruction-intent-preservation · E2
- practice: observed, not assigned. 278 category instances from the RAG-generated files (at least 10 per category, all 16 categories) were compared by hand with the developer-written reference section and labelled Match, Partial Match, Wrong or Missing (§6.3, §6.4).
- claim: "only about one in five fully preserves the intended requirements"; generated instructions "frequently capture the general intent without preserving all developer-specified requirements" and should be treated as "drafts requiring verification" (§6.4, §7.4).
- evidence: 54 instances (19.4%) Match, 170 (61.2%) Partial Match, 54 (19.4%) Missing, 0 Wrong; 224 (80.6%) capture at least some of the intent (§6.4). Partial matches omit, for example, a project's technology stack or navigation requirements (§6.4).
- effect: hurts
- reader: counts with nothing to compare against, E2. The outcome is fidelity of the generated section to the developer's section, not agent behaviour. The number of annotators, the agreement and the selection of the 278 are not stated in the parts read. No Wrong label in 278 instances is as notable as the partial share, and the paper does not discuss it. It does not say whether the comparison used the generic intents or the original text.

### F5 · observation · cross-format-instruction-divergence · E2
- practice: observed, not assigned. Repositories that keep two or more instruction formats; sections mapped to the same category in different formats were compared by two annotators as equivalent or non-equivalent (§4.2).
- claim: 16.1% of pairs "provide divergent guidance that may lead to different agent behavior depending on which file is consulted"; no pair is contradictory (§4.2).
- evidence: 386 randomly drawn pairs, Cohen's kappa 0.85 (§4.2). Same guidance in different words 126 (32.6%), compatible and complementary 198 (51.3%), divergent 62 (16.1%), contradictory 0 (Table 1). Only 186 pairs (48.2%) share a repository-wide or directory scope; 193 (50.0%) do not (§4.2). AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md in the same repository: median category-level Jaccard 0.929, heading-level 0.273 (§4.2). Multi-format repositories: 42.2% of instruction-bearing repositories in the higher-popularity cohort, 21.6% in the lower; AGENTS.md with CLAUDE.md in 231 repositories (§4.1). Example, one repository: AGENTS.md says Prettier, CLAUDE.md says Biome (§2).
- effect: mixed
- reader: counts of manual labels, E2. The harm to an agent is not measured: no agent was run on divergent files. Half the pairs differ in scope, so divergence can be legitimate; the authors say so (§7.1). Several pairs can come from one repository, so they are not independent (§9). The compatible-but-different 51.3% is the large share and `none` or `helps` for an agent could be argued; `mixed` records the 83.9% and 16.1% split.

### F6 · practice · evidence-derived-instruction-template · E0
- practice: author an instruction file against a 16-category template with three tiers. A category is Required when its mean prevalence over the four formats is at least 50%, Gap when it is below 50% and its category addition ratio (additions over additions plus deletions of headings) is at least 0.80, Recommended otherwise (§5.1).
- claim: the template is "an empirically motivated authoring aid rather than a universally optimal instruction structure" with ten Required, four Gap and two Recommended categories (§5). The Gap tier, Security, Maintenance, Ownership and Git/PR, is the information developers add often and write rarely (§5.2).
- evidence: no outcome measured. Derivation inputs: Table 2 prevalence (Do/Don't rules 81.2% to Ownership/contacts 18.8%) and Table 3 CAR (0.709 for Domain models to 1.000 for Ownership; Ownership 7 additions and 0 deletions, Maintenance 7 and 1). The thresholds are "heuristic design decisions ... rather than statistically optimized cutoffs or evidence that individual categories improve agent performance" (§5.1).
- effect: helps
- reader: asserted, E0: the paper reports that the template is a sound authoring structure but gives no data that a file written to it changes agent success, cost or errors. Prevalence measures what developers write, and the paper itself cites an experiment (2602.11988) where context files did not raise success and raised cost (§5.2). The Gap tier rests on small counts for two categories.

### F7 · practice · instruction-file-review-triggers · E0
- practice: review the instruction file on three triggers: an agent error or heavily reviewed agent pull request, a change to build commands, tests, directory structure, dependencies or interfaces, and a periodic check of documented commands, paths, constraints and contacts; when several files describe one practice, check them for consistency; record the triggers and the reviewers in the file's Maintenance and Ownership categories (§5.3).
- claim: these are "practical recommendations informed by our observations rather than experimentally validated interventions"; an agent failure "should not automatically result in a new rule without first establishing whether the instructions contributed to the problem" (§5.3).
- evidence: no outcome measured. Motivating observations: 48.2% of Copilot-instruction modifications follow a failed or heavily reviewed agent pull request (14-day window), 65.2% of CLAUDE.md modifications are changes in the same pull request as code, 52.5% of AGENTS.md modifications are standalone, 40.4% of 448 files have one recorded modification event, median touch count 2.0 (§4.3).
- effect: helps
- reader: asserted, E0. The observed update patterns are descriptive and the paper says they "do not establish the developers' motivations" (§4.3); a single recorded edit does not show the file is outdated. The edit history covers only pull requests in AIDev, so edits outside them are not counted.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Results may not generalise to private repositories, other formats or repositories outside the dataset (§9).
- The 62 evaluation files were chosen so that each category has at least 25 reference instances; the set does not follow the natural distribution (§6.1, §9).
- Structural coverage and text similarity do not show that generated instructions are correct; converting instructions to generic intents may remove commands and constraints; operational correctness is not measured (§6.1, §9).
- The impact of generated or template-based files on downstream agent performance is not measured (§7.4, §9).
- Pairs in the consistency sample may come from the same repository, which affects independence (§9).
- Edit history cannot show why a file was changed; frequency, CAR and trigger associations are read as patterns, not intent or quality (§9).
- The template tiers and thresholds are heuristic, not optimised, and not evidence that a category improves agent performance (§5.1).
- LLM output variation may influence the template comparison (§9).

## Reader's notes

- Findings left out for want of an effect direction (P9 in SCHEMA.md): adoption is higher in popular repositories, 66.2% against 50.2% of repositories with at least one file, quartiles 50.4%, 50.0%, 62.8%, 69.6%, odds ratio 1.171 per log star (§4.1); AGENTS.md leads at 50.0% and 31.6%, CLAUDE.md 40.4% and 24.6% (§4.1); heading additions outnumber deletions in all 16 categories, CAR 0.709 to 1.000 (Table 3); one author's mean share of edits is 0.885 to 0.962 by format (§4.3); churn medians 0.0 (AGENTS.md, Copilot), 0.014 (CLAUDE.md), 0.399 (Cursor rules) (§4.3).
- The sample is repositories with at least one agent pull request in AIDev, split at the median stars; the cohorts' star ranges and the pull-request window of the dataset are not stated in the parts read.
- The 16-category taxonomy and its manual coding by one author after a kappa of 0.80 on 400 files are borrowed from earlier work [9]; a file counts a category once however many sections map to it (§3).
- Mean prevalence is unweighted over formats (§5.1), so Cursor rules (65 files) weigh as much as AGENTS.md (1,677).
- The "with template" arm of F1 is the zero-shot configuration of F2 and F3's table: Table 5's 0.828 equals Table 6's zero-shot coverage, and the tier means of Table 6 reproduce Table 5's tiers (reader's arithmetic).
- The same author group built the template, the benchmark, the generation prompts and the metrics, so the template-guided gain is measured with an instrument the authors chose.
- The comparison to Gloaguen et al. is by citation only: that work finds context files do not consistently improve success and may raise cost (§8.2); this paper does not test success.
- The abstract says the template has "ten Required, four Gap, and two Recommended"; Table 4 agrees.

## Discrepancies

- §1 and §6.1 give the benchmark as 1,815 files from 522 repositories, while the dataset-construction text first gives 2,853 files from 552 repositories after the two-section filter; the 1,815 follows a second filter ("generic-intent filtering", §6.1) whose rule is not given in the parts read. The sum 1,432 + 321 + 62 is 1,815 (reader's arithmetic).
- §4.1 labels the adoption figure "Figure 2" in the text and "Figure 3 Instruction file adoption" in the page header; §4.2's similarity figure is "Figure 3" in the text and "Figure 5" in its header; the maintenance figure is "Figure 4" in the text and "Figure 8" in its header.
