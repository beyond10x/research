## Question

When an agent's harness (prompts, memory, skills, routing) is updated from experience around a frozen
model, how can new behaviour be gained without losing behaviour that worked before, and does a commit
gate that checks current gain, historical retention and validity achieve that (§1, §3)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · harness-update-gated-on-current-gain-and-anchor-retention · E3
- practice: a Continual Optimizer proposes candidate harness changes from post-execution feedback; a Continual Evaluator commits a candidate only if it improves current validation cases by at least the margin, keeps the loss on a sampled evaluation-only anchor set within a budget, and passes validity checks. The harness is four jointly versioned parts: Task Interface, Experience Memory, Capability Map, Adaptive Router (§3.2, §3.3, Eq. 10). The baselines are a zero-shot or static harness, RAG, MemP and MemRL (§4).
- claim: HCL "achieves the strongest overall performance" on the textual and multimodal streams, with "relative gains exceeding 10% over corresponding baselines in multiple settings" (§4.2, abstract).
- evidence: Table 2 textual, final average, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: zero-shot 63.65, MemP 65.45, MemRL 68.85, Plasticity-HCL 76.10, Stability-HCL 70.65. Multimodal, Qwen3.6-27B: zero-shot 46.77, MemP 65.16, MemRL 64.75, Plasticity-HCL 67.96, Stability-HCL 68.92. Against the best baseline the gain is 10.5% on the textual stream for Plasticity-HCL, 5.8% on the multimodal stream for Stability-HCL (reader's arithmetic). Qwen3.6-27B textual stream, Table 5: zero-shot 62.00, MemP 62.55, MemRL 62.20, Plasticity-HCL 66.15, Stability-HCL 62.00.
- effect: helps
- reader: E3. The whole system is compared with other memory methods, not the gate with and without it in the same system (that is F2, F3). One run per cell, no variance or test in the text. The authors built the method and reimplemented the baselines "under the same execution framework" (§4.1). The best GSM8K score is 96.00 against 65.20 zero-shot (Table 2), the largest single gain, with thinking disabled and exact-match scoring (Appendix C.1); the paper does not say how much comes from output-format fixes. On the Qwen3.6-27B stream Stability-HCL has no gain over zero-shot and Plasticity-HCL gains 4.15 points (Table 5; reader's arithmetic). No task is a coding task.

### F2 · practice · anchor-loss-budget-on-harness-commit · E4
- practice: vary only the historical-loss budget b, the number of anchors the deployed harness solves that the candidate fails, over b in {0, 1, 3, infinity}; current-improvement and validity criteria fixed; 80 anchors per earlier task; frozen DeepSeek-V4-Flash; 40 proposal opportunities (§4.3, Appendix C.1, D).
- claim: "limited tolerance for historical regression improves final average accuracy in this setting, but further relaxation reduces both final accuracy and retention" (§4.3).
- evidence: Table 3, final average and average forgetting: b=0 61.25 and 0.39%; b=1 63.46 and 1.22%; b=3 62.04 and 2.00%; b=infinity 60.13 and 3.45%. b=1 is 2.21 points above b=0 (§4.3). Multimodal stream: stage-wise final forgetting 0.22% for Stability-HCL against 0.81% for Plasticity-HCL (Appendix D, Figure 5).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4, a single-parameter ablation on one stream. One run per b; the 2.21-point gain of b=1 over b=0 and the 1.12 points between b=0 and b=infinity (reader's arithmetic) have no variance or test stated. The budget limits loss on a sampled anchor set, not on the test set: "b = 0 does not guarantee zero test-set forgetting" (§4.3). The forgetting numbers are all below 3.5%, so the absolute retention at stake is small.

### F3 · practice · anchor-loss-budget-on-harness-commit · E4
- practice: the same HCL system run with b=0 (Stability-HCL) and b=infinity (Plasticity-HCL), which "differ only in the historical-loss budget" (§4).
- claim: the Evaluator "controls the stability-plasticity trade-off between capability acquisition and retention" (§4.1, §4.2).
- evidence: final average and average forgetting, Plasticity-HCL against Stability-HCL. ALFWorld Table 1: 62.98 and 10.94% against 61.74 and 2.64%. Textual Table 2: 76.10 and 0.40% against 70.65 and 0.27%. Multimodal Table 2: 67.96 and 0.81% against 68.92 and 0.22%. Qwen textual Table 5: 66.15 and 7.87% against 62.00 and 1.27%.
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 by design (one parameter differs), one run per cell. The gate lowers forgetting in all four pairs but also lowers final accuracy in three of them (ALFWorld, textual, Qwen textual) and raises it in one (multimodal). On the textual stream forgetting is 0.40% against 0.27%, so the gain in accuracy of the ungated variant costs almost no retention there. Only the Stability-HCL and Plasticity-HCL pairs isolate the gate; the comparison to MemP and MemRL does not.

### F4 · practice · external-harness-proposer · E4
- practice: use a stronger model, Qwen3.6-27B, only to generate candidate harness updates while the frozen Qwen3-4B runs the task (Opt-HCL), against Qwen3-4B for everything (Base-HCL) and against Qwen3.6-27B for per-example structuring, planning and selection online (Online-HCL). All variants use b=0 and the same data and update schedule (§4.3, Appendix C.1).
- claim: "stronger-model assistance can improve HCL through persistent harness updates without requiring the stronger model during online execution" (§4.3).
- evidence: Figure 4 and §4.3: final four-task accuracy Base-HCL 35.10%, Opt-HCL 45.75% (+10.65 points), Online-HCL 44.80%; online latency 9.95, 9.72 and 39.31 seconds per sample.
- effect: helps
- reader: E4, an assignment of one variable on equal data. One run, no variance on accuracy stated; the latency has one standard deviation in the figure only. The 4B model is far below the 27B model, so the gain may be larger than for a pair that is closer in size. The weak and strong models are not compared with a 27B-only HCL or with an unchanged 27B baseline in the text.

### F5 · practice · per-component-harness-update-ablation · E4
- practice: on the multimodal stream with frozen Qwen3.5-4B, keep one of Interface, Memory, Capability or Router fixed at its initial contents while the other three keep updating (§4.4, Appendix F).
- claim: "all four components contribute to continual adaptation"; fixing Memory causes the largest drop and the most forgetting (§4.4).
- evidence: Table 4 and Table 7, final average and forgetting: full 63.41 and 0.45%; w/o Interface 62.37 and 0.11%; w/o Memory 62.28 and 0.83%; w/o Capability 63.12 and 0.06%; w/o Router 62.77 and 0.14%; zero-shot 34.84. The ablated variants are 0.29 to 1.13 points below the full system (reader's arithmetic).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 as an ablation, but the effect is small and one run per variant has no variance. The differences between the four ablated variants, 62.28 to 63.12, are under one point. The authors say lower forgetting in some variants is because they "acquire less new capability" (§4.4). Disabling Memory also removes the source of new skills (Appendix F.1), so the Memory and Capability ablations are not independent. Nearly all of the gain over zero-shot, 28.57 points for the full system (reader's arithmetic), is present in every variant.

### F6 · practice · llm-judged-acceptance-of-harness-update · E3
- practice: in interactive environments (ALFWorld, Minecraft) replace the strict replay of anchors with "an LLM-based evaluation that judges candidate acceptance given historical task records", because replaying a candidate changes environment state (Appendix C.1). Skill tests act as anchors in Minecraft.
- claim: HCL accumulates capability in long-horizon interaction: it completes all 50 Minecraft tasks where the static harness stops at 15, and has the best ALFWorld final average (§4.1).
- evidence: Minecraft, Figure 3: Plasticity-HCL completes 50 of 50 tasks, the static harness 15; 83 cumulative environment actions against 88 for MemRL and 91 for MemP. ALFWorld Table 1, final average: static 47.12, RAG 55.56, MemP 53.15, MemRL 60.68, Stability-HCL 61.74, Plasticity-HCL 62.98.
- effect: helps
- reader: E3. HCL is ahead of the best baseline, MemRL, by 1.06 and 2.30 points on ALFWorld (reader's arithmetic) with 134 evaluation episodes and one run, and no variance stated. The text says the best non-HCL baseline scores 55.56% (see Discrepancies). Whether MemP and MemRL complete all 50 Minecraft tasks is not stated; only their action counts are. The retention check here is an LLM judgement, a weaker gate than the one used on the textual and multimodal streams.

### F7 · observation · harness-level-forgetting · E2
- practice: no treatment applied; forgetting measured for harness updates that are not blocked on historical loss, and for memory baselines.
- claim: a harness update can turn an earlier success into a failure with the model frozen, and this "harness-level forgetting" is measurable (abstract, §1).
- evidence: average forgetting for the ungated Plasticity-HCL: 10.94% on ALFWorld (Table 1), 7.87% on the Qwen3.6-27B textual stream (Table 5), 3.45% at b=infinity in the textual sweep (Table 3), 0.81% on the multimodal stream (Table 2). MemP has 5.18% on ALFWorld. MemRL and MemP have 0.00% on the multimodal stream.
- effect: hurts
- reader: numbers that describe the condition, with comparison only through F2 and F3. Forgetting is measured on the final test sets as the mean fall from each earlier task's best score (§4, Appendix C.2), and ranges from 0.00% to 10.94%, so the size of the problem depends on the stream and the backbone. ALFWorld forgetting is computed over the first five categories (Appendix C.1).

### F8 · observation · harness-gain-varies-with-backbone · E3
- practice: none applied; the same textual stream is run with two frozen backbones, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash in the main text and Qwen3.6-27B in Appendix C.3.1.
- claim: "the realized stability-plasticity outcome depends on the frozen backbone and optimization trajectory, even when the data budget and task order are matched" (Appendix C.3.1).
- evidence: Plasticity-HCL gain over zero-shot: +12.45 points on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (76.10 against 63.65, Table 2) and +4.15 points on Qwen3.6-27B (66.15 against 62.00, Table 5; reader's arithmetic for both). Plasticity-HCL forgetting 0.40% against 7.87%. Stability-HCL on Qwen3.6-27B equals zero-shot, 62.00, and is below MemP, 62.55.
- effect: mixed
- reader: E3, two different backbones, no control. The Qwen run is called "the earlier Qwen3.6-27B textual experiment" kept "for completeness" (Appendix C.3.1), while the DeepSeek run is the main result; the paper does not say why the main stream changed.

### F9 · practice · calculator-tool-routed-by-evolving-router · E3
- practice: register an external arithmetic calculator in the Capability Map of frozen Qwen3.6-27B and let the evolving selector and Router decide per input whether to use it (Appendix E).
- claim: capability-aware HCL gains most where the new tool applies, GSM8K (Appendix E).
- evidence: Figure 6, final accuracy against an earlier Stability-HCL run: MuSiQue 58.60 to 58.60, ProofWriter 73.80 to 82.80, GSM8K 53.20 to 90.40, HotpotQA 64.40 to 65.80; 254 of 500 GSM8K test examples routed to the calculator.
- effect: helps
- reader: E3 at best. The authors say the runs "also differ in prompts, retries, and memory trajectories, these changes do not isolate the causal contribution of tool execution alone" and call it exploratory. The earlier run is not a no-tool control, and the earlier run's GSM8K score, 53.20, is already above the zero-shot 44.80 (Table 5), so part of the gain predates the tool.

## Limits stated by the authors

- The budget limits regression on sampled anchors, not on all historical test cases, so b=0 does not guarantee zero test-set forgetting (§4.3, Appendix D).
- Some restricted ablation variants show lower forgetting because they acquire less capability, so forgetting must be read with acquisition (§4.4).
- The calculator comparison is exploratory and does not isolate the tool's effect (Appendix E).
- In interactive environments strict historical backtesting is replaced by an LLM judge, and Minecraft completed tasks are not replayed after every update (Appendix C.1, C.2).
- The Qwen textual result is kept separate and not pooled with the main textual result (Appendix C.1, C.3.1).

## Reader's notes

- No experiment is a coding task, and no software-engineering benchmark is scored. The harness parts the paper updates, prompts, memory entries, skills and routing, are the ones a coding agent's instruction files and skills correspond to, but the evidence is by analogy only.
- All reported results come from the authors' own system. The memory baselines MemP and MemRL were reimplemented by the authors; their tuning is not described in the parts read.
- Every cell is one run; the text states no seeds, confidence intervals or tests, and many of the ablation differences are about one point.
- The main text and the budget sweep use different data budgets and model versions: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash against DeepSeek-V4-Flash, 250 against 300 adaptation examples, 500 against 600 test examples (Appendix D). Stability-HCL's accuracy there, 61.25, is not comparable with Table 2.
- "Plasticity-HCL" and "Stability-HCL" are two settings of the authors' method; which one is "HCL" differs between claims. §4.1 compares Plasticity-HCL with the baselines, and on the multimodal stream Stability-HCL is the best.
- Anchors are chosen by an LLM-based selector that prioritises failures, format violations and brittle cases (Appendix B.1), so the retention check is biased toward cases where the harness was already weak; the paper does not test this.
- The Continual Optimizer's proposals are prompt and context artifacts (Appendix B.2); memory and skill writes are evaluated jointly and retained or rolled back as one implicit update. Skills are LLM-written procedures from Abstract Memory, with no human-written skill comparison.

## Discrepancies

- §4.1 says Plasticity-HCL's 62.98% is "compared with 55.56% for the best non-HCL baseline"; Table 1 gives MemRL 60.68 and 55.56 is the RAG baseline. The stated gap of 7.42 points is 2.30 against MemRL (reader's arithmetic).
- The abstract says relative gains exceed 10% "in multiple settings"; against the best baseline in Tables 1 and 2 only the textual Plasticity-HCL row does (10.5%, reader's arithmetic). Against the zero-shot model many settings do.
- §4.4 says the ablation uses frozen Qwen3.5-4B while §4.3's external-model experiment uses Qwen3-4B; both are written as 4B and the paper does not say whether they are the same model.
