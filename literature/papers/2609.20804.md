## Question

Which of three harness components (planning, the action space, context management) helps a coding agent, and does the effect depend on the model's capability, the task type and the context-window budget (§1)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · context-management-by-window-budget · E4
- practice: compare four context-management tiers (T1 elision, T2 elision with recall, T3 summarization, T4 staged elision then summarization) against T0, which does nothing and ends the run with an error when the window is exceeded, at 32k, 64k, 96k and 128k windows, on the same tasks (§2.3, §3.1).
- claim: "The value of context management grows as the context-window budget shrinks", largely because it prevents premature truncation when the window binds (§3.2).
- evidence: model-averaged success gap of T1 to T4 over T0 at 32k, 64k, 96k, 128k: 35.7, 15.9, 5.5, 2.7 points on SWE-Bench and 9.5, 7.5, 4.8, 2.8 on Terminal-Bench (§3.2). T0 overflow rate falls from 78.7% to 8.7% on SWE-Bench and from 61.0% to 12.1% on Terminal-Bench; all managed tiers have zero overflow failures (§3.2). At 32k on SWE-Bench, Nemotron-3 550B T0 6.40 against T1 51.40 (Table 3). At 128k only Nemotron-3 550B shows a significant gain on SWE-Bench (T4 65.80* against T0 59.80; the other three models' T4 gains are not significant) (Table 3).
- effect: helps
- reader: an ablation on equal tasks, so E4. The control fails by construction when the window is exceeded, so the large small-window gap measures the cost of overflow termination, not the quality of a compaction policy. One run per task (Limitations); most Terminal-Bench cells are not significant (89 tasks).

### F2 · practice · context-management-by-window-budget · E3
- practice: the same tier comparison, read as a trajectory analysis: how runs under T1 to T4 differ from T0 in length and phase mix (§4, C.5).
- claim: "context management primarily extends execution trajectories without substantially altering agent behavior" (§4).
- evidence: at 32k T0 median length is 20 to 30 turns and most T0 runs end in the Localize phase; managed tiers have medians of about 50 to 180 turns (§4). At 128k the SWE-Bench median stays between 39 and 42 turns for Nemotron-3 30B and between 70 and 74 for Nemotron-3 550B across T0 to T4; re-patch counts differ by under 2 per task; the share of runs ending without an edit differs by under 4 points for three of four models and about 8 points for Nemotron-3 120B (C.5, Tables 11 and 12). The phase of each turn is labelled by an LLM judge (GPT-5.5); against 3 human annotators on 200 trajectories (15,610 units) raw agreement is about 94.2% and weighted Cohen's kappa 0.929 (C.2).
- effect: none
- reader: a comparison of descriptive statistics between tiers on the same tasks, no test; E3. The finding says that the tiers do not change behaviour at 128k, so it measures no outcome gain.

### F3 · practice · staged-elision-before-summarization · E4
- practice: T4, which elides bulky old tool outputs once the history passes 0.6 of the usable window and summarizes the oldest middle events only once it passes 0.85, against T1 (elision only), T2 (elision with recall) and T3 (summarization only) (§2.3, §3.1).
- claim: "T4 offers the best accuracy-cost trade-off among context-management strategies"; early elision reduces calls to costly LLM summarization (§3.2).
- evidence: T4 success is "comparable" to T1 to T3 and its cost is the lowest in seven of eight model and benchmark panels (Figure 4, text). Averaged over the eight pairs T4 has the lowest mean cost per task and the lowest peak-context ratio at every budget, and invokes summarization less often than T3 (§3.2, Figure 5; values in the figure only). Example, Nemotron-3 550B on SWE-Bench at 32k: success T3 58.40 at $1.25, T4 55.60 at $1.45, T1 51.40 at $2.57 (Table 3). At 128k on SWE-Bench the four managed tiers are within a few points of each other and no pairwise test between tiers is reported (Table 3).
- effect: helps
- reader: an ablation of the components on equal tasks, but the cost advantage is read from a figure and no test of cost is given. Costs are priced from OpenRouter list prices for models served locally (§3.1), so they are estimates. Two prompts, one threshold pair (0.6 and 0.85) and one run per task; T4 is also the configuration all later ablations use.

### F4 · practice · recoverable-elided-observations · E4
- practice: store elided tool outputs in files and give the agent a `recall_event` tool to read them back (T2 against T1; T4 includes it) (§2.3).
- claim: "Recall (M2) is rarely used and does not improve accuracy over elision alone" (§3.2).
- evidence: across 32 model, benchmark and window pairs T2 beats T1 in 15, loses in 14 and ties in 3; equal-weight mean difference -0.36 points (+0.40 on SWE-Bench, -1.12 on Terminal-Bench) (§3.2). Of 64 T2 and T4 settings, 36 (56.3%) never call `recall_event`; mean calls per task fall from 0.540 at 32k to 0.069, 0.011 and 0.007 at 64k, 96k and 128k (§3.2). The heaviest user, Nemotron-3 30B on Terminal-Bench at 32k under T2, makes 4.326 calls per task and scores 3.37 points below T1 (§3.2, Table 13).
- effect: none
- reader: E4 by design, a matched pair that differs only in recall. One run per task, so a difference of 1 point is within noise. The finding concerns this implementation and these models; recall use is concentrated in the weakest model.

### F5 · practice · todo-list-planning-tool · E4
- practice: a persistent plan the model keeps with an `update_plan` tool, an initial-plan reminder, and the plan appended to every model input but not stored in the history; compared with the same harness with the planning instructions, reminder, injection and tool removed, at T4 and 128k (§2.1, §3.1).
- claim: planning is an accuracy scaffold for the weakest model and mainly a cost saver for the stronger ones, with little change in accuracy (abstract, §3.2).
- evidence: Nemotron-3 30B SWE-Bench success 25.20 with planning against 13.60* without (cost $0.09 against $0.02); Terminal-Bench 13.48 against 8.99, not significant, cost $0.14 against $0.08, +75% (Tables 3, 4, §4). Nemotron-3 550B on SWE-Bench: 65.80 against 67.80, cost $2.33 against $3.31; Mistral: 68.60 against 69.00, cost $3.14 against $4.65, costs down about 30% and 32% (§3.2, Tables 3 and 4). Nemotron-3 120B: SWE-Bench 44.00 against 46.60 and $0.34 against $0.25, so no consistent gain (Table 3). Terminal-Bench cost change with planning: 120B -26%, 550B -3.6%, Mistral about -40% (§4).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 by design. Effect is mixed: success rises and cost rises for the weakest model, cost falls and success is 0.4 to 2.0 points lower (not significant) for the two strongest. Only the 30B SWE-Bench difference carries a significance star. Model size is a proxy for capability (Limitations); the 4 models do not separate size from family. One prompt and one run per task.

### F6 · practice · todo-list-planning-tool · E4
- practice: as F5; the measurement is where trajectories stop and how long they run, labelled by the LLM judge (§4).
- claim: planning "sustains the weakest model's trajectory long enough to attempt an edit" and shortens runs of the stronger models by removing post-edit verification (§4).
- evidence: Nemotron-3 30B SWE-Bench without planning: median length 5 turns against 40; 68.6% of runs end without an edit against 27.8%, and 58.4% end while still localizing against 10.4% (Table 6, Table 12); unresolved runs failing at file localization rise from 56.3% to 73.8% (Table 10). Nemotron-3 550B: median 108 turns without planning against 74 with; Mistral 68 against 53; the without-edit rate stays below 3% for both (§4, C.5, Table 11). The reduction is attributed to verification turns (§4; phase composition in Figure 8).
- effect: helps
- reader: an ablation measured on trajectory statistics, not on task outcomes; the attribution to verification rests on LLM-judge labels, whose agreement with humans is high on the sample (C.2) but is not reported per phase in the parts read. "Helps" here means fewer premature stops for the weak model and fewer turns for strong ones.

### F7 · practice · structured-file-tool-registry · E4
- practice: the predefined tool set (read, write, edit, list, glob, grep, fetch, bash) against a bash-only interface at T4 and 128k with planning on. The change also removes read-before-write checks, file-state tracking and automatic post-edit diagnostics (§2.2).
- claim: "The predefined tool set scaffolds weaker models, while bash-only can benefit stronger ones"; the crossover depends on the model and on how shell-centric the task is (§3.2).
- evidence: SWE-Bench success with predefined tools against bash-only: Nemotron-3 30B 25.20 against 10.20*, Nemotron-3 120B 44.00 against 42.40, Nemotron-3 550B 65.80 against 69.40*, Mistral 68.60 against 45.40* (Table 3). Terminal-Bench: 13.48 against 3.37*, 28.09 against 23.56, 44.94 against 50.56, 37.08 against 43.82 (Table 4). Mistral issues 71.9% of Terminal-Bench workspace actions through bash against 40.4% on SWE-Bench (§3.2); 32.8% of its bash-only SWE-Bench runs end without an edit against 1.2% (§3.2); file-localization failures among unresolved runs rise from 16.0% to 41.4% (C.3.1). For Nemotron-3 30B bash-only, 66% of Terminal-Bench trajectories end after out-of-interface tool emissions and the average length falls from 71 to 15 turns (§3.2; the sentence is split by a figure in the extracted text, subject read as 30B).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 by design, but the manipulated variable is the whole interface bundle (tools, prompts, validation, diagnostics), which the authors say (§2.2, Limitations). The two significant bash-only gains and losses are all on SWE-Bench; on Terminal-Bench only the 30B loss is significant. One run per task.

### F8 · practice · bash-only-tool-interface · E4
- practice: as F7; the measurement is cost per task and number of tool calls for the bash-capable models (§3.2).
- claim: bash-capable models "can operate effectively with a bash-only interface and achieve substantially lower cost, especially on command-line-centric tasks" (abstract).
- evidence: Nemotron-3 550B bash-only cost $1.11 against $2.33 on SWE-Bench and $1.70 against $2.43 on Terminal-Bench, reported as 53% and 30% lower; 32% fewer calls on SWE-Bench and 24% fewer on Terminal-Bench (§3.2, Tables 3 and 4). Mistral bash-only cost $1.72 against $3.14 on SWE-Bench and $2.75 against $2.22 on Terminal-Bench (Tables 3 and 4). For Nemotron-3 120B the predefined tools shorten runs from 101 to 77 turns on SWE-Bench and from 96 to 70 on Terminal-Bench without raising cost (§3.2).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 by design. Bash-only is cheaper in 3 of the 4 pairs given for Nemotron-3 550B and Mistral; Mistral's Terminal-Bench cost is higher ($2.75 against $2.22), and on SWE-Bench Mistral's cheaper run also scores 23.2 points lower. So "substantially lower cost" holds without an accuracy loss only for Nemotron-3 550B. Costs are list-price estimates.

### F9 · practice · bash-only-tool-interface · E4
- practice: as F7; the measurement is how code is written: repeated edits to already-edited files, size of the largest edit, and share of file-writing actions that create or replace a file (§4, Table 7).
- claim: "predefined tools lower the complexity of each individual action, but they often do so by increasing the number of interactions and incremental repair cycles"; bash-only lets capable models bundle operations (§4).
- evidence: mean re-patches per task with tools against bash-only: Nemotron-3 30B 3.3 against 0.4, 120B 2.8 against 2.2, 550B 4.6 against 1.5, Mistral 3.0 against 1.3 (Table 7). Create or replace share of file writes on Terminal-Bench: 28% against 64%, 39% against 76%, 51% against 76%, 28% against 57% (Table 7). Median largest edit on SWE-Bench: 550B 18 against 54 lines, 30B 26 against 13, Mistral 87 against 68, 120B about unchanged (C.5). Nemotron-3 550B Terminal-Bench median length 47 against 31 actions; write-code share 16% against 27% (§4).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 by design on a behavioural measure. Fewer re-patches are not shown to mean better patches: for Mistral and Nemotron-3 30B bash-only also lowers success (F7). Edit size moves in different directions by model.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Results are conditional effects of one implementation: planning is one prompt and update mechanism, context management one threshold policy (Limitations).
- The action-space change is a bundle and does not isolate tool count or action granularity (Limitations).
- Planning and the action space were ablated only at T4 and 128k for compute reasons; a full factorial design is needed to know whether the effects persist elsewhere (Limitations).
- Each setting ran once per task; Terminal-Bench has 89 tasks, so many contrasts are not significant and conclusions rest on consistent direction across models and budgets (Limitations).
- Four models only, SWE-Bench Verified is Python only, model size is an imperfect proxy for capability; the crossover points should be validated before transfer (Limitations).

## Reader's notes

- The models are open-weight models of modest capability served at temperature 0. The effects of planning and of tools are reported for models whose baseline success ranges from 25% to 69%; no frontier model is included, so the claims for "stronger models" mean the stronger of these four.
- The authors built the harness and ran every condition; no independent party evaluated it, and the Terminal-Bench rows of Table 4 are mostly below significance.
- Significance is shown per cell with a star. There is no statistic on cost or on the summary claims "lowest cost in seven of eight panels" and "equal-weight mean".
- Costs are token counts priced at OpenRouter list prices (§3.1), not measured spend.
- Safety gates, post-edit diagnostics (ruff or pyflakes on Python files) and stuck detection were held fixed (§2.4), so nothing is known about their own effect.
- The distribution of failure stage by capability (Table 10: for Nemotron-3 30B more than half of unresolved runs fail at file localization, for 550B and Mistral most fail at patch implementation) is a descriptive result with no claimed direction and is not a finding here.
- Turn labelling for §4 comes from an LLM judge (GPT-5.5, temperature 0.6); the human check covers 200 trajectories.

## Discrepancies

- §3.2 writes the gains of the predefined tools as "15.0%", "10.1%", "1.6%", "4.5%", "3.6%", "5.6%", "23.2%" and "6.7%". They equal differences in percentage points between table cells (for example 25.20 minus 10.20 is 15.0), and Appendix C.3.1 calls the 23.2 a "23.2-point drop" (reader's arithmetic).
- The text of §3.2 near Table 12 reads "rises from 16.0The Mistral result"; the sentence is cut in the extracted text. The value 16.0% to 41.4% is from Appendix C.3.1.
