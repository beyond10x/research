## Question

Which consequential decisions of a long-horizon agent warrant user verification, and does organising
the agent's context into a graph of behaviours with source-linked evidence help a monitor model find
them and locate their evidence (§1)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · behavior-graph-evidence-view-for-monitor · E4
- practice: give a monitor model the agent's repository or trajectory as an Evidence-Grounded Behavior Graph (EBG), built without model calls and rendered as task-oriented views, against the same monitor reading the raw repository (with search and read tools) or the raw trace; same 100 examples per subset (§4, §5.1, Table 1).
- claim: "EBG improves on Base across most settings"; "evidence-localization gains persist across input scales and hyperparameter settings" (§5.2.1, abstract).
- evidence: rule-based localization scores, Table 1, 8 monitors. EBG is above Base in 24 of 24 cells over LF1, LS and EHR (reader's arithmetic). SilentSwap LS: GPT-5.6 Terra 0.417 to 0.623, Sol 0.464 to 0.671, Kimi K3 0.555 to 0.681. SpecGAP LF1: DeepSeek-V4-Pro 0.171 to 0.360, Luna 0.305 to 0.418. Smallest gains: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash LF1 0.459 to 0.469, DeepSeek-V4-Pro EHR 0.340 to 0.370. No significance test, one run per cell.
- effect: helps
- reader: an A/B on equal instances with the same monitor, so E4. The authors designed the benchmark and the method (`independent: false`); the Base condition also differs in tool access (6 blocks of 400 lines per read against 6 seed files with all one-hop neighbours, App. D.4), and the cost or token use of the two methods is not reported in the parts read ("Actual token usage can vary across methods", D.4). Gold evidence of SpecGAP and SilentSwap was built with model help and 92.4% to 93.6% double-review agreement (§3.4, AI use statement).

### F2 · practice · behavior-graph-evidence-view-for-monitor · E4
- practice: as F1; the measurement is the six metrics scored by two LLM judges (GLM-5.2 and Qwen3.7-Max, mean reported): SF1, QQ, LC, CC, VPA, ESS (§5.1).
- claim: EBG improves decision identification "in most settings"; "the benefits vary across monitors and tasks" (abstract, §5.2.1).
- evidence: Table 1, 48 judge-scored cells: EBG above Base in 43, equal in 1 (Sol CC 0.598), below in 4 (reader's arithmetic). Below: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash SF1 0.376 to 0.372 and QQ 0.832 to 0.807, Terra VPA 0.513 to 0.508 and ESS 0.588 to 0.565. SpecGAP SF1: Terra 0.275 to 0.392, Sol 0.456 to 0.505. FeedbackTrace VPA gains are small: Sol 0.480 to 0.493, Luna 0.460 to 0.485. The two judges agree on the direction of change in 40 of 48 monitor-metric comparisons (83.3%), 5 of 8 for LC (App. E.1, Table E3).
- effect: helps
- reader: same design and confounds as F1. The gains on judge-scored metrics are smaller and less uniform than on localization, and the judge disagreement is concentrated on the metrics with the largest differences between judges (LC 0.097 and VPA 0.095 mean absolute difference). Both judges are LLMs from families that are also among the monitors (GLM-5.3, Qwen); no human scoring of monitor output is reported in the parts read.

### F3 · practice · repository-graph-exploration · E4
- practice: give the monitor an adaptation of RepoGraph (Python AST definition and reference graph, one-hop symbol subgraphs per read, up to 32 per read) in place of the plain source reader, on SpecGAP and SilentSwap (§5.1, C.2).
- claim: EBG "achieves higher scores than RepoGraph in most repository comparisons" (§5.2.1); the claim for RepoGraph against Base is not made in the text.
- evidence: Table 1, 8 monitors, 6 repository metrics: RepoGraph below Base in 41 of 48 cells (reader's arithmetic). Examples: SilentSwap CC Claude Sonnet 5 0.223 to 0.083, Kimi K3 0.690 to 0.545; SpecGAP SF1 Sol 0.456 to 0.410. Above Base mostly for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and DeepSeek-V4-Pro.
- effect: hurts
- reader: a comparison on equal instances with Base as control, so E4, but the paper does not test it and the RepoGraph here is the authors' re-implementation with AST parsing in place of Tree-sitter (C.2). It "does not add EBG's behavior grouping or task-document alignment", so it is a graph-navigation condition, not RepoGraph as published. The task is monitoring against a task document, not repair.

### F4 · practice · behavior-grouping-of-evidence · E4
- practice: in the EBG view, remove the grouping of evidence units into behaviours (condition, operation, result, or demand, action, response) and show the evidence units one by one within each scope; scopes and edges stay (App. E.2).
- claim: "the complementary roles of EBG components" are supported by the ablations (§1); every applicable ablation lowers its task's representative metric (E.2).
- evidence: Table E4, score of the ablated variant minus full EBG, SF1 / LS / ESS: GPT-5.6 Luna -0.102 / -0.049 / -0.040; Kimi K3 -0.043 / -0.090 / -0.115; GLM-5.3 -0.046 / -0.084 / -0.105. A few other metrics rise, for example Kimi K3 QQ +0.005.
- effect: helps
- reader: an ablation on equal instances, so E4; 3 monitors out of 8, one run each, no test. The authors' own method.

### F5 · practice · scope-and-relation-grouping-of-evidence · E4
- practice: in the EBG view, remove both the grouping of behaviours into scopes and the explicit relation edges between scopes, and show the behaviours as a flat list (App. E.2).
- claim: as F4.
- evidence: Table E4, SF1 / LS / ESS: GPT-5.6 Luna -0.201 / -0.112 / -0.050; Kimi K3 -0.088 / -0.120 / -0.128; GLM-5.3 -0.106 / -0.138 / -0.073. Luna QQ -0.412. SilentSwap CC rises for Kimi K3 (+0.005) and GLM-5.3 (+0.008).
- effect: helps
- reader: same as F4. Scopes and edges are removed together, so the paper cannot separate their contributions (E.2).

### F6 · practice · task-guided-evidence-selection · E4
- practice: remove task guidance from the EBG view: directory ranking by the task requirements in file selection and the alignment of document passages with the evidence view; SpecGAP and SilentSwap only (App. E.2).
- claim: as F4.
- evidence: Table E4, SF1 / LS: GPT-5.6 Luna -0.107 / -0.150; Kimi K3 -0.070 / -0.166; GLM-5.3 -0.083 / -0.208. SilentSwap LC GLM-5.3 -0.213.
- effect: helps
- reader: same as F4. This is the one ablation with the largest LS drop for two of three monitors.

### F7 · practice · evidence-expansion-depth · E4
- practice: expand the evidence subgraph by one, two or three hops from each seed; EBG with one hop is the default; GPT-5.6 Luna and GLM-5.3 on SpecGAP and SilentSwap, other settings fixed (App. E.4).
- claim: "Deeper expansion does not consistently improve scores"; gains over Base persist at every depth (App. E.4).
- evidence: for one monitor and task, SF1 or LS varies by at most 0.0303 across depths. EBG is above Base on all three SpecGAP metrics and on SilentSwap LS and LC at every depth. SilentSwap CC falls below Base for Luna at three hops and for GLM-5.3 at two.
- effect: none
- reader: a parameter sweep on equal instances with 2 monitors, so E4; "none" is the finding about depth, and the Base comparison supports F1. Per-depth values are in figures only.

### F8 · practice · monitor-interaction-round-budget · E4
- practice: cap the monitor's search-and-read rounds lower than the default of 8 (SpecGAP) or 6 (SilentSwap): 4, 6 or 8 rounds on SpecGAP, 4, 5 or 6 on SilentSwap, with one-hop EBG against Base at each budget; GPT-5.6 Luna and GLM-5.3 (App. E.4).
- claim: "The gains remain under reduced interaction budgets"; more rounds "do not consistently raise" scores (App. E.4).
- evidence: at every matched budget EBG improves all three SpecGAP metrics and SilentSwap LS and LC for both monitors; SF1 and LS vary by at most 0.0272 within a monitor and task. SilentSwap CC: Luna falls below Base at 4 and 5 rounds, GLM-5.3 declines slightly at 4.
- effect: none
- reader: same design and limits as F7; "none" is the effect of the round count.

### F9 · observation · monitor-accuracy-by-input-size · E3
- practice: none applied; the 100 examples of each subset are split into tertiles by input tokens and the main predictions are re-scored per tertile (§5.2.2, App. E.3).
- claim: "Average Base scores decrease as input size grows"; EBG improves average localization at every input scale (§5.2.2).
- evidence: mean over GPT-5.6 Luna, Kimi K3 and GLM-5.3, Base, Low to High input: FeedbackTrace EHR 0.667 to 0.422, SilentSwap LS 0.557 to 0.429; SpecGAP LF1 "declines more modestly". All 9 average comparisons favour EBG; High-group gains +0.078 LF1, +0.115 LS, +0.108 EHR. By monitor, EHR falls for Luna in the Medium group (-0.091) and for Kimi K3 in the Low group (-0.030) (App. E.3). Median input tokens of the Low, Medium and High groups: 9,468, 28,936, 86,138.5 on FeedbackTrace (Table B1).
- effect: hurts
- reader: the groups hold different examples, so the decline may reflect which examples are long; no control. Only 3 of 8 monitors. Effect is for longer input on Base.

### F10 · practice · behavior-graph-evidence-view-for-monitor · E4
- practice: ask the monitor to label a sample "disclosure warranted" or not, on 50 samples that warrant disclosure and 50 that do not, per subset, with Base and with EBG; GPT-5.6 Luna, Kimi K3, GLM-5.3 (App. E.5).
- claim: "EBG generally improves detection while reducing false alarms, but unnecessary disclosure remains a problem for some monitors" (E.5).
- evidence: Table E6, true-positive and false-positive rate in percent, Base to EBG. EBG raises the true-positive rate in 9 of 9 monitor-subset pairs and lowers the false-positive rate in 8. GLM-5.3 SilentSwap 72/24 to 76/12; Kimi K3 SilentSwap 86/38 to 88/22; Luna SilentSwap FPR 52 to 58; Luna FeedbackTrace FPR 88 to 84 with TPR 90 to 94.
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 by design, but 50 samples per cell and one run, so one sample moves a rate by 2 points. The samples without a decision to disclose are drawn from the same source datasets by the authors, and how they were checked for containing no such decision is not in the parts read. Luna's false-positive rate stays at 84% on FeedbackTrace, so the monitor there flags nearly everything with or without EBG.

### F11 · observation · monitor-failure-with-evidence-visible · E2
- practice: none applied; each failed reference unit of the EBG runs is assigned to its earliest failure stage: file not selected, relevant content missing from the rendered view, or reference evidence visible but the monitor failed to localise or disclose the decision (App. G).
- claim: "Most attributed failures occur after reference evidence is visible" (G.3).
- evidence: Table G1, GLM-5.2 scoring, judgment-stage share of failures: SpecGAP 74.0% to 81.2% (258 to 362 failures of 484 units per monitor), SilentSwap localization 69.4% to 76.9% (111 to 221 of 500). Evidence-omitted share 10.2% to 15.4% on SpecGAP and 14.5% to 24.3% on SilentSwap; file-selection omissions 6.3% to 12.0%. FeedbackTrace: 55 to 59 failures of 100, all 100% judgment. Qwen3.7-Max gives 75.0% to 80.9% and 72.0% to 79.0% (Table G2). Monitors: GPT-5.6 Luna, Kimi K3, GLM-5.3.
- effect: hurts
- reader: this describes the failures that remain and has no comparison; `hurts` is the closest value (schema proposal P9). On FeedbackTrace the whole trace is in the input by construction, so 100% judgment failure follows from the setup. The paper says the cause inside the judgment category (interpretation or presentation quality) is not separated.

### F12 · practice · review-hooks-with-evidence-tools · E1
- practice: in Codex, add a review harness with a PostToolUse hook (after the agent reads a plan it intends to execute), a Stop hook (end of response), three review skills (ambiguity, adjustment, result) and three evidence tools (`ebg review`, `ebg evidence`, `ebg record`); result review also triggers after 10 tool calls or 5 minutes; an assessment is recorded only after evidence inspection (§5.2.3, App. F.1, F.2).
- claim: "Codex without the harness does not disclose the key issues for user verification; with it, Codex identifies and discloses these issues" (§5.2.3).
- evidence: 5 research tasks adapted from public autoresearch problems, each run once without and once with the harness (App. F.3, Table 2). Threshold choice: without, picked threshold 0.7 (accuracy 0.70 to 0.80, recall 1.00 to 0.3333) with no question; with, asked and the user chose accuracy. RNG: serial fallback changed per-problem seeds; with the harness, seeds restored and rerun, mean objective 42.6374 to 28.5896 (without 30.6025). Leakage: 8 of 80 added samples derived from validation records; with, baseline kept. Cached API responses: 4/4 smoke pass with zero live requests; with, the verification claim was corrected. Search budgets: reference 1 fit against candidate 27 plus 72 refined fits; with, the conclusion was qualified.
- effect: helps
- reader: 5 cases, one pair of runs each, so E1. The authors built the tasks, the harness and the criteria, and the review skills name the failure kinds the tasks contain (randomisation, data separation, budgets, verification coverage), so the harness is prompted to look for them. The paper does not separate the EBG evidence tools from the hooks and skill prompts. The Codex model is not stated in the parts read, and the without-harness run of Case 5 used a 140-fit refined search and the with-harness run 72, so the runs differ in more than the harness (Case 5 box).

## Limits stated by the authors

- No section of limitations was found in the parts read.
- "The benefits vary across monitors and tasks" (§5.2.1).
- "Individual semantic scores and some model-specific conclusions remain judge-sensitive" (App. E.1).
- Unnecessary disclosure "remains a problem for some monitors" (App. E.5).
- "Attribution to a specific root-selection, expansion, or rendering rule remains unresolved"; the SilentSwap failure analysis covers localization failures only (App. G).
- RepoGraph is not applicable to FeedbackTrace and is not run there (App. C.2).
- The harness cases are "controlled local tasks" adapted from public problems (App. F.3).

## Reader's notes

- All evaluation instances come from the authors' own benchmark; 2 of its 3 subsets are synthetic (conditions deleted from task documents, behaviours swapped by a model builder). Only FeedbackTrace uses real user sessions (SWE-chat, Python only, at most one feedback event per session, §3).
- The gold labels of FeedbackTrace derive from user pushback after the fact, so decisions the user never questioned count as no verification point; the false-alarm test of App. E.5 is the only check on that.
- The scores are low in absolute terms: for Base, the best SpecGAP SF1 is 0.494 (Kimi K3) and the best FeedbackTrace VPA is 0.513 (GPT-5.6 Terra); with EBG the best are 0.558 and 0.518 (Table 1).
- Cost, latency and token counts of the monitors are not reported in the parts read, although the baselines and EBG read different amounts of source (App. D.4).
- Judge GLM-5.2 and monitor GLM-5.3 are the same family; the paper tests agreement between its two judges (App. E.1) and not against humans.
- The study measures a second model reading an agent's context. It does not measure whether a user who receives the flagged decisions decides better.

## Discrepancies

- App. E.1 says EBG achieves higher LS and LC than Base and RepoGraph across all eight monitors "in the main results"; the per-judge Table E1 has LC below Base for GPT-5.6 Luna (0.385 to 0.355), Kimi K3 (0.590 to 0.545) and GLM-5.3 (0.645 to 0.620) under GLM-5.2. The statement holds for the averaged Table 1 only.
- §5.2.3 and Table 2 call the five cases "real-world research task cases"; App. F.3 says they are adapted into "controlled local tasks".
- §5.2.2 says Base scores decrease "from Low to Medium to High" for all three means; the individual-monitor panels of Figure E1 show Luna and Kimi K3 EBG-minus-Base changes of both signs on EHR, so the per-monitor pattern is less regular than the mean (the Base per-monitor values are in the figure only).
