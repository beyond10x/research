## Question

Do published experiments with agentic coding tools report the harness, model, tasks and run
conditions well enough to isolate harness effects, and what would a shared reference harness need
(Abstract, §I)?

## Findings

### F1 · observation · harness-version-unreported · E2
- practice: of 325 annotated experiments, how many gave an exact release number, tag or commit for every harness that ran, the dates the experiments ran, and traces for every task attempt (§III).
- claim: the harness version, run date and traces are rarely reported, so a harness name "can refer to different implementations even in a short period" (§II) and the comparison cannot be repeated on the same code (§IV).
- evidence: exact version of every harness reported in 15 experiments (DQ3; 5%); experiment dates in 5 (DQ12; 2%); traces for every attempt in 31 (DQ13; 10%) (Table I, §III). Model version named for every model in 238 (73%), and in 30 of those 238 with a date (DQ7, §III). Only a release number, tag or commit in the paper counted; linked repositories were not inspected (§III).
- effect: hurts
- reader: counts of annotated reports with nothing to compare against, so E2. The harm to comparability is the authors' argument; the paper does not measure a replication. The annotation is by LLMs, with 8.5% disagreement between two models before resolution (§III).

### F2 · observation · harness-comparisons-confounded · E2
- practice: in 226 experiments that compared harness implementations or configurations, how many contrasts changed only the harness and how many changed the model or tasks with it (§III, Figure 1).
- claim: many comparisons "change model and harness together" and so "cannot isolate their effects" (Abstract).
- evidence: 191 of 226 had at least one contrast that changed only the harness; 35 did not (§III). 141 had a contrast that changed model or tasks together with the harness; in 115 the model changed with the harness. In 50 of those 115 the authors credited their proposed agent as a whole; 43 of the 50 also had a separate harness-only contrast. Of 298 contrasts that changed more than one degree of freedom, authors credited the proposed system as a whole in 111, the inner or outer harness specifically in 7, and gave no cause in 118 (§III). Of 782 contrasts, the inner harness was the treatment in 238 and confounded in 152; the model was the treatment in 190 and confounded in 155 (Figure 1). In 40 contrasts the inner and outer harness changed together and nothing else (§IV).
- effect: hurts
- reader: counts of coded contrasts, no control, so E2. The coding rule assigns "treatment" to the degree of freedom the authors credit, so the counts of isolation depend on the codebook, which was developed and applied on the same papers and not tested on an independent set (§III). Search by title and abstract can miss papers (§III).

### F3 · observation · harness-changes-model-ranking · E3
- practice: the same models run on more than one harness, cited from other papers (§I). This paper did not run it.
- claim: model and harness interact, so a comparison that changes one cannot be read as an effect of the other (§I).
- evidence: Zhang et al. ran three models on three harnesses; changing the harness reversed the ranking of two models in six of nine comparisons (§I, reference 2, arXiv:2605.23950). Kapoor et al. found Claude models scored highest with one harness and OpenAI models with another (§I, reference 3, arXiv:2510.11977). No numbers for the second are given here.
- effect: hurts
- reader: E3 because the comparison is between harnesses with the models fixed, without a stated control for tasks or sampling. Second-hand: the paper reports what the two cited papers found and the reader did not check them. The effect is on comparability of model rankings, not on task success.

### F4 · observation · harness-release-drift · E3
- practice: one model run on successive releases of one harness, cited from another paper (§II).
- claim: "Harness updates can change outcomes even when the model is held fixed" (§II).
- evidence: Ben Sghaier et al. compared 35 Qwen Code releases with the same model; tokens per task rose from about 391K to nearly 668K with no gain in task resolution rate. Across five harnesses, 1.5 to 18 releases per week (§II, reference 14, arXiv:2607.03691).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E3, a before-after across releases with no stated control for tasks, cited second-hand. Cost went up with no gain in resolution; the outcome measured is cost and resolution, not quality of the patches. It is the best evidence in the paper that pinning a release matters.

### F5 · observation · harness-feature-interactions · E3
- practice: four harness changes (tools, middleware, memory, system prompt) evaluated alone and in combination, cited from another paper (§II).
- claim: "Evaluating a component change alone does not establish how the harness will perform when other components change too" (§II).
- evidence: Lin et al.: on hard tasks, the memory changes alone resolved more tasks than combining all four changes (§II, reference 15, arXiv:2604.25850). No counts are given here.
- effect: hurts
- reader: E3 as a comparison of arms without a stated control, cited second-hand, one result on one subset ("hard tasks"). The effect is that the combination did worse than one part; the paper takes it as a case for factorial designs (F8).

### F6 · observation · experiment-reporting-gaps · E2
- practice: how many of the 325 experiments answered each of 21 disclosure questions on configuration, model, tasks, cost and validity (Table I).
- claim: papers leave out information a reader needs to compare outcomes, such as cost and variation (§III, Abstract).
- evidence: Table I, share among experiments where the question applies. Outer harness can be set up again 103 (32%); tools the model could call 78 (24%); sampling and reasoning parameters 82 (25%); variation across repeated sessions 93 (29%); cost per session in a stated unit 165 (51%); input and output tokens separately 28 (9%); a reader can derive the money spent 61 (19%); contamination checked or mitigated 49 (15%); who answered agent requests 27 (8%); how each measure was computed 262 (81%); task set and version 309 (95%).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E2 descriptive counts. The 21 questions were derived by the authors from the LLM guidelines (reference 4, arXiv:2508.15503) and weighted equally; a "no" does not mean the study was wrong, only that the item was not found in the full text. Annotator disagreement per question is in Table I, for example 62 of 278 on DQ21 and 49 on DQ1.

### F7 · practice · pinned-reference-harness-with-versioned-extensions · E0
- practice: the community agrees on one open-source reference harness at a pinned release; features are added as separately versioned extensions; papers hold the release and model fixed within each feature comparison, report all five degrees of freedom and execution dates, and share traces for every attempt; venues run tracks around it (§IV, Abstract).
- claim: independent teams could then "repeat feature comparisons and combine evidence about their effects" (§IV).
- evidence: none beyond the argument and a feasibility check. Of 11 open-source harnesses, six met all four requirements (pinnable release, separately versioned extensions, support for open-weight and commercial models, open source): Codex CLI, OpenClaw, opencode, OpenHands, Qwen Code, pi (§IV). The assessment used documentation, release records and source files of the latest releases (§IV). No study has been run on a reference harness.
- effect: helps
- reader: E0 for the claimed effect on evidence quality; the harness assessment is a description by the authors with no stated procedure for checking it. The four requirements were derived from the same annotation (15 of 325 versions, 115 experiments with model and harness changing together).

### F8 · practice · fractional-factorial-feature-ablation · E0
- practice: add five extensions to one harness (pi) - a permission gate, subagents, plan mode, a to-do list, a compaction strategy - and run 16 of the 32 combinations in a resolution V fractional factorial design, with one open-weight and one commercial model at dated snapshots on the same tasks with executable oracles and a fixed environment, repeating attempts (§V).
- claim: it "halves the number of configurations while estimating each extension's average effect and pairwise interactions", assuming interactions of three or more extensions are negligible (§V).
- evidence: none; the experiment is planned, not run (§V).
- effect: helps
- reader: E0, a plan. The assumption of negligible higher-order interactions rests on a cited study of configurable systems (reference 25) that is about performance of software, not agent outcomes. F5 is one case where a combination of changes did worse than one part.

## Limits stated by the authors

- The search could miss eligible papers with no arXiv record and no query term in the title; reference lists and citing papers were not searched; preprints without an eligible peer-reviewed full paper were excluded (§III).
- Only full texts were annotated, not linked code or supplementary material (§III).
- Screening, annotation and judging relied mainly on Claude models, so errors may be correlated; the 8.5% disagreement rate "does not establish how often the models would agree with human annotators" (§III).
- The codebook was developed and applied on the same papers, with no test on an independent set (§III).

## Reader's notes

- Disagreement was 8.5% overall (95% CI [7.5%, 9.5%]) and 12.7% for the codes of the five degrees of freedom, over the 278 experiments both models found; the two models found 358 distinct experiments, so 80 were found by one model only (reader's arithmetic from §III). The counts in F1, F2 and F6 are the post-resolution annotation, not a human count.
- 32 unresolved codes were set to "unclear" by the first author, and two experiments were included although the judges disagreed (§III).
- "Held fixed" is coded when the paper reports the same value or describes no change, even if the value is unreported (§II), so the held-fixed counts in Figure 1 include unverified equality.
- Figure 1 column order is garbled in the extracted text; the held-fixed values are inferred so that each degree of freedom sums to 782 (reader's arithmetic): outer harness 586, inner harness 310, model 351, tasks 603, user interaction 770. They are not used in the findings.
- Nothing in the paper measures agent task success under a treatment. It bears on method only through what a study must report to be read as evidence, which is the reason the findings are observations about reporting.
- The 185 settings keys of Claude Code, from which the paper states 2^185 combinations, is arithmetic on an assumption of independent binary choices (§II).
- "Opus 5.5", "GPT-6.1-Sol", "Fable 5.1" and the other model names are as written in the paper.
