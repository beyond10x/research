## Question

When an agent harness repeats a query to the environment (refresh) after a stale or missing observation, does that rescue or harm the run from the same starting state, how does that depend on timing and on what refresh contains, and can a policy refresh only where it pays (§1)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · unconditional-observation-refresh · E4
- practice: at a fixed point after 25% of the environment actions, replace the observation with the one from two actions earlier (stale) or leave it clean, then branch the run from the identical state into one continuation with an extra look action (refresh) and one without, at delays 0, 1, 2 and 4 actions (§3.2, §5.1).
- claim: "recovery value is not a fixed property of the refresh operation"; it depends on the observation, the state and the timing (§5.2).
- evidence: 93 tasks whose unperturbed run succeeds, delay 0: refresh changes success by −6.45 pp on clean observations (95% CI [−11.83, −2.15]) and +7.53 pp on two-step stale ones (CI [−3.23, 18.28]); difference 13.98 (CI [2.15, 24.73]). Stale-minus-clean difference 18.28, 15.05 and 13.98 at delays 1, 2 and 4 (§5.2). Independent 89-task cohort: differences 8.99 to 14.61; at delay 2 +10.11 stale and −4.49 clean (§5.2). At delay 1: stale 15 rescues and 4 harms, clean 6 successes turned into failures (§5.2, Figure 6).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4, because both branches share state, history, model and decoding, so the only difference is the refresh. The stale-observation gain is not significant by itself (CI includes 0). In this cohort the no-refresh clean run succeeds by construction, so refresh can only harm there (§5.2). Authors injected the errors; one agent, one benchmark, not a coding task.

### F2 · practice · unconditional-observation-refresh · E4
- practice: as F1, on every prefix-feasible task including those whose unperturbed run failed (§5.2).
- claim: the effect is more favourable under stale than clean observations in both complete cohorts, and the same average can hide many rescues and many harms (§3.3, §5.2).
- evidence: primary cohort, 106 tasks: −3.77 pp clean (CI [−9.43, 0.94]) and +6.60 pp stale (CI [−2.83, 16.04]); difference 10.38 (CI [0.00, 20.75]). Independent cohort, 100 tasks: 0.00 clean and +4.00 stale; difference 4.00 (CI [−5.00, 14.00]). Counts in the primary cohort: clean 2 rescues and 6 harms, stale 17 rescues and 10 harms (§5.2).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the same practice as F1 on a wider set, kept apart because the interval of the difference touches 0.00 in the primary cohort and contains 0 in the independent one. Net +6.60 hides 10 harms next to 17 rescues, which is the paper's point about averages.

### F3 · practice · content-ablated-refresh · E4
- practice: issue the same environment query as a refresh but hide the returned content from the agent (§5.3).
- claim: the returned observation is not required for most shared rescues; refresh has two components, the query and the shown content (§5.3).
- evidence: 67-task mechanism cohort, two-step stale, delay 4: full refresh +13.43 pp (CI [4.48, 22.39]), content-ablated +10.45 pp (CI [1.49, 20.90]) (§5.3). Full refresh rescues ten tasks and content-ablated rescues eight of them (§5.3). On 65 matched tasks: full +13.85 (CI [4.62, 23.08]), content-ablated +10.77 (CI [1.54, 20.00]), full minus content-ablated +3.08 (CI [−4.62, 10.77]) (Table 2). Clean, delay 0: full −8.96, content-ablated −5.97 (§5.3).
- effect: helps
- reader: helps against no recovery on stale observations; against full refresh it is not distinguishable (CI includes 0). Why a refresh without content rescues is not explained in the parts read; a plausible reading is the extra turn or the changed context, which the paper does not test. Ten rescues is a small count.

### F4 · practice · replan-without-new-observation · E4
- practice: ask the agent to reconsider its next actions from the context it already has, without querying the environment (§3.1, §5.3).
- claim: replanning alone does not supply the benefit that refresh does (§5.3, Figure 3).
- evidence: two-step stale, delay 4: +1.49 pp (CI [−8.96, 11.98]); clean, delay 0: −14.93 pp, against −8.96 for full refresh (§5.3). No interval is given in the text for the clean value.
- effect: none
- reader: no gain on stale observations; on clean ones it is the largest drop of the three variants, so `none` records the stale-observation result only. Same mechanism cohort, 67 tasks.

### F5 · practice · outcome-predicting-recovery-router · E4
- practice: three l2-regularised logistic models (C = 0.1) predict the chance of an erroneous observation, success without recovery and success after an immediate refresh, from signals available before the decision (repetition of actions and reasoning, no-ops, observation novelty, action-observation consistency, task progress, context budget). Refresh at most once, at the first of delays 1, 2 and 4 where all thresholds are met; clean-trajectory harm limit 0.02, harm weight 2; thresholds fitted on out-of-fold predictions of 93 tasks, then fixed (§4, §5.1).
- claim: selective recovery gives a gain on new tasks and never touches clean trajectories (abstract, §5.4).
- evidence: 75 held-out tasks, 300 episodes: never refresh 70.33%, router 73.33%, +3.00 pp (CI [0.67, 5.67]), 11 rescues and 2 harms, sign-flip test p = 0.034. Table 1: clean 81.33% both, 0 rescues 0 harms; one-step stale 68.00% to 69.33%; two-step stale 60.00% to 69.33%, +9.33 pp (CI [1.33, 17.33]), 9 rescues and 2 harms; missing 72.00% to 73.33%. Intervenes in 16.3% of episodes and in 0 of 75 clean ones (§5.4).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 as an A/B on equal episodes against never refreshing. The authors designed, tuned and evaluated their own router, so `independent: false`. The held-out set is 3 perturbed conditions out of 4, and the perturbations are the same kinds used to fit; the gain per perturbed episode is 9 of 225 = 4.00 pp (reader's arithmetic). Counts are small: 11 rescues. No cost of the extra refresh, in tokens or time, is reported.

### F6 · practice · outcome-prediction-over-anomaly-detection · E4
- practice: decide to refresh from predicted success with and without refresh, in place of refreshing whenever an error detector fires; the baselines use the same error detector, one with a threshold chosen on the same objective and one with its training-time intervention rate matched to the router's (§5.1, §5.4).
- claim: the router "considers the predicted outcome of the intervention, not only whether the current observation appears unusual", which is why anomaly detection is insufficient (§5.4, Discussion).
- evidence: sequential anomaly baseline intervenes in 12.7% of episodes, +1.67 pp (CI [0.33, 3.33]), 6 rescues and 1 harm; rate-matched baseline 15.7%, +2.00 pp (CI [0.67, 3.67]), 6 rescues and 0 harms. Router margins over them: 1.33 pp (CI [−0.33, 3.33]) and 1.00 pp (CI [−0.67, 2.67]) (§5.4).
- effect: none
- reader: the router's point estimate is highest, 11 rescues against 6, but both margins have intervals that contain 0, so no difference is shown. The paper states only that the router "has the highest point estimate". Both baselines also gain and never harm a clean trajectory in the counts given.

### F7 · observation · router-gain-by-observation-staleness · E3
- practice: the router's result split by the observation condition of the 75 held-out tasks (Table 1, §5.4).
- claim: it "concentrates recovery where the observed benefit is largest" (§5.4).
- evidence: two-step stale: router intervenes in 31 of 75 episodes, +9.33 pp, 9 rescues and 2 harms. One-step stale and missing observations: +1.33 pp each, 1 rescue and 0 harms each; the router intervenes in 1 of 75 missing-observation episodes (§5.4, Table 1).
- effect: helps
- reader: a subgroup split of the F5 result, with no control for why the router refreshes more on two-step stale; the staleness severity is assigned by the authors. Rescue counts are 9, 1 and 1. The intervention count for one-step stale is not stated.

## Limits stated by the authors

- The tasks are the prefix-feasible ones that were available, "not the full ALFWorld distribution" (§5.1).
- The rescue and harm scores are not rescue and harm probabilities, since marginal success probabilities do not identify them (§4.2).
- Attribution of the difference to recovery holds "for the states studied here" (§3.2).
- The paper has no limitations section.

## Reader's notes

- Not a coding task. ALFWorld is a household text game, the agent is a 14B model, the harness a fixed ReAct loop, and refresh is a `look` action. Transfer to a coding agent re-reading a file or re-running a command is the reader's analogy, not tested.
- Decoding is greedy and replay is deterministic, so there is no sampling variance within a branch; variation comes from the task set. All CIs and the sign-flip test are at task level.
- The router is fitted on 93 tasks that all succeed unperturbed (the factual-success cohort), so its picture of success and failure under refresh rests on those tasks (§5.1).
- The overall 16.3% intervention rate is 49 episodes of 300 (reader's arithmetic); with 31 on two-step stale and 1 on missing, about 17 are on one-step stale, which the paper does not state.
- Harm to clean trajectories is limited to 0 of 75 on the held-out set. The training-time target was 0.02, so the held-out clean count says little about the rate in other tasks.
- Cost of refresh (an extra environment call, extra context) and of running the three models is not reported.

## Discrepancies

- §5.2 gives the stale-minus-clean difference as 13.98 at delay 0 and again 13.98 at delay 4 (13 of 93 tasks); the repeat may be a coincidence or a copy.
- §5.3 reports effects on 67 tasks (+13.43 and +10.45) and Table 2 on 65 tasks (+13.85 and +10.77); the paper explains this (two tasks end before recovery) and says the net gains of 9 and 7 tasks are unchanged (Appendix A).
