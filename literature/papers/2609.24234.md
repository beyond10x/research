## Question

How do developers organise supervision over a task delegated to AI coding agents (§1)? The paper
builds a seven-stage framework from 19 observed developers and applies it to Reddit discussions to find
supervisory demands and the strategies developers use against them (§1).

## Findings

### F1 · practice · detailed-plan-before-delegation · E1
- practice: write a plan with goals, requirements, constraints, success criteria and approach before the agent runs, and spend more effort there than later (§5, §7.2.1).
- claim: developers described well-structured plans as letting them move from Monitor to Wait and as giving fewer repeated Teach cycles (§7.2.1).
- evidence: quotes of single commenters, no counts. Agents run "for days without interruption and come back to clean and efficient implementation of code"; with a tight architecture and testing plan the "whole thing can run in literally one pass, no looping needed" (§7.2.1). One of 19 participants: "the planning itself was the teaching" (P3, §5).
- effect: helps
- reader: grade E1 because the evidence is a few quoted statements by developers about their own runs; no run was compared with or without a plan, and the 102 threads give no count of how many say this. The authors say the effect of effort in Plan on later correction is still to be tested (§8.3).

### F2 · practice · agent-critique-of-plan-before-execution · E1
- practice: put the plan through the agent or a second model before delegating: stress-test a drafted plan, let a planning skill interview the developer, have two models write and merge plans, or have one model plan and another review in a loop until agreement (§7.2.1).
- claim: involving agents in building the plan exposes assumptions, ambiguities and missing details before execution (§7.2.1).
- evidence: cases, no counts. "put it through the wringer with AI, stress testing it"; "/grill-me" then "answer questions until the skill reaches full understanding"; Claude prepares the plan, "Codex does the review, and critique is messaged back to Claude", "they loop till agreement" (§7.2.1). P18 refines the plan with AI until satisfied (§5).
- effect: helps
- reader: E1, the cases are described, not measured; no quality of plan or downstream outcome is reported. The effect is the developers' stated purpose.

### F3 · practice · explicit-scope-boundaries-in-plan · E1
- practice: state non-goals next to goals, name forbidden actions ("do not start x", "do not rewrite y", "preserve z"), and give the agent a change contract of allowed files, expected behaviour and what it must not touch (§7.2.1).
- claim: explicit boundaries clarify the intended scope of the work (§7.2.1).
- evidence: quotes from individual commenters; no counts and no outcome (§7.2.1). Pairs with the observed scope expansion in F11.
- effect: helps
- reader: E1; whether agents then stay in scope is not reported. The same quotes record that the agent expands scope on its own (F11).

### F4 · practice · small-units-with-definition-of-done · E1
- practice: split planned work into smaller execution units than one would do by hand and give each an explicit end condition, for example "done = this test passes and nothing outside file X changes" (§7.2.1).
- claim: smaller units brought "a lot more success" and "higher quality"; the end condition marks anything else as a "rabbit hole" (§7.2.1).
- evidence: two single-commenter quotes (§7.2.1). No counts.
- effect: helps
- reader: E1; the success claim is one developer's impression, with no comparison in the paper.

### F5 · practice · cross-model-review-of-draft · E1
- practice: review agent output with models from other providers, so that each reviewer has different biases (§7.2.2).
- claim: one developer used "multiple models from different providers to have them all audit each other's work", each with "its own biases and blindspots" (§7.2.2).
- evidence: one quoted developer; no measurement (§7.2.2).
- effect: helps
- reader: E1, one case reported in the commenter's words. Controlled evidence on separate reviewers exists elsewhere in the distillation and is mixed; this paper adds none.

### F6 · practice · focused-review-passes · E1
- practice: run separate review passes by concern ("one pass for security/data loss, one for test gaps, one for overengineering") instead of one review covering everything (§7.2.2).
- claim: "when one review tries to cover everything, it tends to produce generic advice" (§7.2.2).
- evidence: one quoted developer; no measurement (§7.2.2).
- effect: helps
- reader: E1; no comparison of review quality between the two set-ups is given.

### F7 · practice · enforce-conventions-in-tooling · E1
- practice: put supervisory actions in hooks instead of written guidance: a stop hook that makes the agent read the system patterns of an area first, and a PostToolUse hook that runs "the narrowest relevant test right after an edit" and feeds failures back at once (§7.2.2).
- claim: written guidance can be "read, weighed against everything else, and quietly deprioritised once context fills up", whereas "a hook is code; it fires every time whether the model thinks it's relevant or not"; the test hook tells the agent it broke something "on the same turn, before it stacks three more edits on a broken base" (§7.2.2).
- evidence: quotes from individual developers; no rate of compliance or of defects (§7.2.2).
- effect: helps
- reader: E1; the claim that prose is deprioritised is stated, not measured here.

### F8 · practice · human-plus-agent-review · E1
- practice: let agents review first (obvious mistakes, failed validations, deviations from the specification), then the developer reviews what remains (§7.2.2).
- claim: "agents and humans catch different types of errors"; the human reviews "something that's already been checked" (§7.2.2).
- evidence: quotes, no counts and no error data (§7.2.2). Review has the largest share of coded quotes in the 30-thread sample, 366 of 1,114 (§6.2).
- effect: helps
- reader: E1; "different types of errors" is a stated impression. In the same section the developers also keep a role for themselves, so the claim is about division of work, not about the agent review replacing the human one.

### F9 · practice · accepted-review-comments-as-instruction-rules · E1
- practice: turn a repeated correction into a standing asset: a hook, a static linter before commit, a MISTAKES.md file plus a rule in CLAUDE.md to record mistakes, or a milestone retrospective that edits skill files (§7.2.3).
- claim: "if I catch myself repeating the same instruction to the agent, that's not a prompting problem, that's a missing hook"; retrospectives give "compounding effects, essentially preventing the same kind of mistakes from happening in the future" (§7.2.3).
- evidence: quotes from individual developers; no recurrence counts (§7.2.3). Update Assets is optional and not done after every task (§5); it has 230 of the 1,114 coded quotes in the 30-thread sample (§6.2). P10 and P14 update AGENTS.md or documents occasionally, when improvements repeat (Fig. 4).
- effect: helps
- reader: E1; the preventive effect is a developer's claim. Closest quantitative evidence is in the distillation under this slug.

### F10 · practice · manual-fix-for-small-or-repeated-corrections · E1
- practice: make the correction by hand when it is simple, quicker than a prompt, or when prompting again would not pay, and code the risky or architectural parts by hand to keep one's own picture of the system (§5, §7.2.2).
- claim: "surgical edits for things that would take longer to type into a prompt than to just fix myself"; repeated prompting is "mentally much less taxing" to replace with manual coding; rewriting a risky function periodically keeps "the map in my head" (§7.2.2).
- evidence: quotes (§7.2.2, §7.1.3) and P6's "it was faster for me" (§5). Manual Fix has 40 of the 1,114 coded quotes in the 30-thread sample, the least of the seven stages (§6.2).
- effect: helps
- reader: E1; the claim is about developer effort and understanding, and neither is measured. The paper gives no size below which manual fixing is quicker.

### F11 · observation · unrequested-scope-expansion · E1
- practice: none applied. Developers report that agents change the plan's criteria, make unintended assumptions or build beyond the task, and that repeated correction can add drift (§7.1.1).
- claim: keeping agent work aligned with developer intent is a demand across Monitor, Review and Teach (§7.1.1).
- evidence: quotes; no rates. "doesn't make up criteria or change criteria as it goes"; review checks "did the agent actually build what was in the plan instead of sneaking in extra work"; "scope drift over long sessions" where the agent "starts building on its own previous assumptions" (§7.1.1).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E1, a handful of commenters' statements out of 102 threads, so no prevalence. The threads were chosen by an LLM for the number of supervision excerpts (§6.1), which favours threads about problems.

### F12 · observation · quality-defects-beyond-working-code · E1
- practice: none applied. Developers report that agent code that does what was asked can still fall short: one writes that agents produce "lazy, sloppy, repetitive, and overly defensive code—even with solid guardrails and tests" (§7.1.2).
- claim: enforcing quality standards beyond task completion is a separate demand, handled in Review by checks for duplicate and dead code, and carried into Teach or Manual Fix (§7.1.2).
- evidence: quotes, no counts or code measures (§7.1.2).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E1; a standard-of-quality complaint, with no measure of how often it occurs. It matches the distillation's functional-pass-hides-review-constraint-violations, which is measured elsewhere.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Study 1 captured only a window of about 40 minutes of work per participant; real projects span longer periods and sessions, and the workflow diagrams add retrospective reflection (§9).
- Participants were observed and thinking aloud, which may have changed how they worked; introducing Sheridan's framework may have anchored how they drew their stages (§9).
- The paper gives no evidence on which supervisory practices work: effects of Plan effort on later correction, or of delegated Review on code quality, are named as future tests (§8.3).
- Whether the framework fits work outside software development is open and would need adapting (§8.1).

## Reader's notes

- Every effect above is what developers say about their own practice. No finding compares outcomes with and without the practice, so no finding exceeds E1. Counts are given only for the corpus (41,757 posts to 102 threads) and for the coded quotes by stage.
- Study 2 selection: 41,757 posts, 11,471 after dropping deleted, comment-less and some flair classes, 5,041 after an LLM relevance screen (gpt-5.6-luna), 4,390 threads with at least one stage excerpt, the top 100 by excerpt count with ties made 103, minus one promotional thread (§6.1). Ranking by number of tagged excerpts picks threads dense in supervision talk, not a random sample.
- Coding: 30 threads (29.4%) coded by three authors with the LLM's tags visible and open to revision; the other 72 threads by two authors (§6.2). Agreement between coders is not reported in the parts read.
- The 1,114 quotes by stage cover only the 30-thread sample, not the 102 threads (§6.2). The shares (Review 366, Update Assets 230, Plan 204, Teach 127, Wait 74, Monitor 73, Manual Fix 40) are coded excerpts, not time or effort spent.
- Reddit commenters in agent subreddits describe set-ups they chose to post about; success stories and complaints are over-represented. `r/ExperiencedDevs` gives 6 of the 102 threads (§6.1).
- The 19 participants brought their own tasks of about 40 minutes (§4.2), many on side or research projects (Table 1); the workflows may differ on long-running or team work.
- The Wait stage (developer runs other agents or tasks while the agent executes, §5) is the paper's description of parallel supervision; no outcome on parallel work is reported, so it is not a finding here.
- Finding F8 and F9 repeat practices that other papers in the distillation measure; this paper supplies the developer-side description.
- Not fitted to a finding: the framework's seven stages and loops, a conceptual result with no direction (§5); the split of coded quotes by stage; the observation that Review is a separate stage from Monitor.
