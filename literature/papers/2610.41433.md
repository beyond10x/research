## Question

When a repair agent fails a task and is retried, what does a record of the failed attempt change in
the retry: its pass rate, its cost, where it edits? Does a model-written lesson beat the transcript it
summarises, and how much of any effect is only the notice that the attempt failed (§I)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · failure-transcript-in-retry · E4
- practice: append the truncated transcript of the failed attempt (each message cut to 2,000 characters, final test output to 6,000) plus a notice that the attempt failed and the workspace was reset, to the retry prompt; the control is a plain retry with the first-attempt prompt (§III.C). Six first attempts per task, every failure retried once under each condition from the original workspace.
- claim: every memory condition gained over a plain retry in point estimate, none significantly after Holm correction; the data "cannot tell us whether failure memory raises the pass rate of a retry" (abstract, §V).
- evidence: M1, 181 failures from 52 tasks, plain retry 29.8% resolved; HISTORY 39.2%, +9.4 points, 95% CI [-0.5, +18.9], Holm p 0.24; task-weighted +11.4 (Table II). The first 46 tasks showed +18.6 ([+9.0, +29.9], Holm p 0.007, nominal) and the remaining 29 tasks -5.9 to +4.4 (§IV.B, Table III); the three execution batches gave +13.6, +31.2 and -5.9 (Table III). M2, gpt-5.4, 85 failures, plain retry 43.5%: HISTORY +10.6 [-8.9, +29.3] (Table VI). Cost per failure: plain retry about $0.021, HISTORY about $0.004 more (§IV.B); for gpt-5.4 $0.084 against $0.095 (§IV.D).
- effect: none
- reader: an ablation on equal failures with paired retries, so E4. No contrast is significant, and the interval runs from a small harm to about 19 points; "none" records "not distinguished", not "no effect". The interim look at 46 tasks was decided on after the result was seen, and the batches differ in plain-retry rate (17%, 28%, 46%). The memory arms differ from NONE by two things, the content and a header that tells the agent the attempt failed.

### F2 · practice · failure-transcript-in-retry · E4
- practice: as F1, compared with a retry that gets only the notice that the previous attempt failed and the workspace was reset (NOTICE, M3). Every M1 and M2 failure received four new retries (NONE, NOTICE, HISTORY, REFLECT) together in one session (§III.F).
- claim: "the transcript may carry more than a failure notice"; the transcript exceeded the notice in point estimate for both models, significantly only on resolution for the smaller model, in a partial run (abstract, §IV.E).
- evidence: HISTORY - NOTICE on resolution: gpt-5.4-mini, 156 failures, +12.2 points ([+4.2, +20.0], Holm p 0.033, the only one of 12 predeclared contrasts classed positive); gpt-5.4, 85 failures, +16.5 ([+0.0, +33.0], not distinguished). Resolved share NONE / NOTICE / HISTORY / REFLECT: 28 / 31 / 43 / 37 (mini) and 48 / 41 / 58 / 47 (gpt-5.4) (Table VII). On modifying a Python file HISTORY - NOTICE was +12.8 and +15.3, Holm p 0.07 and 0.36 (§IV.E).
- effect: helps
- reader: one significant contrast of twelve, on a partial run (46 of 52 tasks) and a different retry sample than M1; the gpt-5.4 interval touches zero. The paper itself says the data "do not establish the size of the transcript's own contribution". Graded E4 because NOTICE is a control on the same failures; the effect is one the authors predeclared only as a family of contrasts.

### F3 · practice · model-written-failure-lesson · E4
- practice: the same model, with the same sampling settings, reads the task statement and the same truncated failure record as HISTORY and fills a fixed schema (suspected files, suspected functions, root cause, what failed, next steps); that lesson replaces the transcript in the retry prompt (REFLECT) (§III.C).
- claim: "a model-written lesson showed no advantage over the transcript it summarises", and it costs an extra model call (abstract).
- evidence: REFLECT - HISTORY on resolution -1.7 points (95% CI [-8.7, +4.9]) for gpt-5.4-mini and -3.5 ([-14.1, +8.2]) for gpt-5.4 (Table VI). REFLECT - NONE +7.2 [-2.3, +17.4], Holm p 0.33 (Table II). Added cost per retry over a plain retry: HISTORY about $0.004, REFLECT $0.009, BOTH $0.008; REFLECT and BOTH took about 21 to 23 seconds longer (§IV.B). gpt-5.4: HISTORY $0.095 and REFLECT $0.115 per failure (§IV.D).
- effect: none
- reader: the contrast is designed to isolate summarisation, since both arms see the same record, so E4. The interval allows a lesson from about nine points worse to five points better. The lesson writer is the small model at low reasoning effort, and the authors did not test a stronger writer. On the post hoc full-suite outcome the lesson was slightly ahead (F9).

### F4 · practice · extracted-failure-signal-digest · E4
- practice: append a deterministic digest of the failed attempt, not a transcript: final status, failing tests, up to 30 assertion or exception lines (3,000 characters) and the files the attempt modified (SIGNAL); BOTH adds the lesson and the digest together (§III.C).
- claim: neither the digest nor the combination was significantly better than a plain retry, and neither distinguished itself from the lesson (§IV.B).
- evidence: SIGNAL - NONE +5.5 points [-1.2, +12.8], Holm p 0.33; BOTH - NONE +9.9 [+1.5, +19.1], Holm p 0.17, the interval excludes zero before adjustment; REFLECT vs SIGNAL +1.7 [-5.7, +9.2]; BOTH vs REFLECT +2.2 [-3.9, +8.7] (Table II, §IV.B). M1 only; M2 had no digest condition.
- effect: none
- reader: same design as F1. The digest is the authors' own extraction rule and one rule only. BOTH is the only memory condition whose unadjusted interval excludes zero, which the authors decline to read as a benefit because the intervals are not adjusted for multiplicity.

### F5 · practice · failure-notice-without-content · E4
- practice: tell the retry only that the previous attempt did not pass the selected tests and that the workspace has been reset, with no record of what happened (NOTICE), compared with a plain retry (§III.F).
- claim: the notice alone did not significantly change the retry; the predeclared hypothesis N1, that it reduces retries without an edit, is "not supported for either model" (§IV.E).
- evidence: modifying a Python file, NOTICE - NONE +1.9 points [-6.3, +9.9] (mini, 156 failures) and +5.9 [-7.8, +19.5] (gpt-5.4, 85 failures). Resolved, NOTICE - NONE +3.2 [-5.0, +11.9] and -7.1 [-19.7, +5.2] (Table VII).
- effect: none
- reader: E4, a control on the same failures. The gpt-5.4 interval cannot exclude a large notice effect (the authors say so), and the mini interval on editing lies within +-10 points. The mini part is partial (156 of 181) because of an infrastructure error.

### F6 · practice · failure-memory-in-retry · E4
- practice: any of the memory conditions of F1, F3 and F4; the outcome is whether the retry modifies at least one Python file (test files are read-only) (§IV.C).
- claim: "with memory, fewer retries end without an edit"; fewer retries declared the task done without changing anything (§IV.C, §V).
- evidence: M1, share of retries that edited a source file: NONE 69.1%; HISTORY 86.2% (+17.1 points, [+7.9, +26.1]); REFLECT 82.2% (+13.3, [+6.3, +20.6]); SIGNAL 79.0% (+9.9, [+2.0, +18.0]); BOTH 78.5% (+9.4, [+1.2, +17.7]) (Table IV). A plain retry edited no source file in 30.9% of failures (56 of 181) and the transcript retry in 13.8% (25); 22 plain retries declared the task done without a change against 4 with the transcript (§IV.C). M2, predeclared H1: HISTORY +14.1 [+5.1, +24.1], Holm p 0.033; REFLECT +5.9 [-2.4, +16.4], Holm p 0.37, so H1 is not supported (§IV.D). NOTICE - NONE on editing +1.9 and +5.9, not significant (F5).
- effect: helps
- reader: the M1 analysis was added after the data were seen; M2 tested it for gpt-5.4 on the same tasks, and only the transcript contrast held. Editing is an intermediate outcome; retries that left the fix files untouched resolved in 1 of 215 across conditions (Table IV, §IV.C), which is why it matters, but it is not the pass rate. The larger model left fewer plain retries without an edit (17.6% against 30.9%), which leaves less room for memory.

### F7 · observation · retry-edit-location-with-memory · E3
- practice: the retries that edited a source file, in each condition, and which file they edited (§IV.C). Nobody assigned this subset; it is conditioned on a post-treatment variable.
- claim: "memory did not improve where the retry edited"; 94% to 98% of editing retries already edited a file the reference fix changes, and the pass rate among them was only slightly and imprecisely higher with memory (§IV.C).
- evidence: fix file among editing retries NONE 97.6%, HISTORY 98.1%, REFLECT 95.9%, SIGNAL 96.5%, BOTH 94.4% (Table IV). Resolved among retries that edited a fix file: NONE 44.3% (122), HISTORY 46.4% (153), REFLECT 47.2% (142), SIGNAL 46.4% (138), BOTH 53.0% (134); HISTORY +2.1 [-9.5, +13.5], BOTH +8.7 [-2.4, +19.9] (Table IV, §IV.C). The reference fix changes one source file in 45 of the 52 tasks with failures (§IV.C). For HISTORY 0.845 x 0.464 = 0.39 against 0.674 x 0.443 = 0.30 (§IV.C). M2: 94% to 95% fix file; pass among editing 56.1 / 59.7 / 60.6% (Table VI).
- effect: none
- reader: E3, unpaired subsets of a post-treatment variable, descriptive by the authors' own statement. A ceiling on file-level hits (the fix is a single file in most tasks) makes this measure uninformative about localisation.

### F8 · observation · lesson-names-fix-location · E3
- practice: the files and functions named in the model-written lessons, matched against the files and functions the reference fix changed, and against two deterministic baselines: the locations the failed attempt itself edited, and the source frames of its final traceback (§III.D, §IV.F).
- claim: "lessons usually name the right file"; when the failed attempt had edited files, lessons rarely improved on it, and their gain comes from failures in which the attempt edited nothing (§IV.F).
- evidence: 181 failures, Table VIII. Lesson (any listed) file hit 92.8%, first-listed 87.3%; function hit 75.1% (first-listed 55.6%) over the 169 failures whose fix changes a function. Attempt's own edits 52.5% file and 40.2% function; traceback frames 22.7% and 13.0%. Of the 181 failed attempts, 81 edited no source file. Where the attempt edited files (n = 100): lesson 96% against attempt 95% (file), 2.3 against 1.0 listed files, precision 0.46 against 0.94; 72 of the lessons' 73 additional correct files came from failures with no edit (Fig. 3, §IV.F). At function level, 83.5% against 74.7% (n = 91), no advantage for the first-listed function (61.5% against 62.6%).
- effect: helps
- reader: E3, a comparison with deterministic baselines that were not matched on what they could see (the attempt's edits are zero by definition in 81 cases). The comparison is scored by fixed matching rules against one reference fix, which is one valid solution among others, and the file-level hit is near ceiling outside black (99.2%). The authors did not assess whether the diagnoses are correct.

### F9 · practice · test-output-of-failed-attempt-in-retry · E3
- practice: retries that saw the previous attempt's test results (the final output in HISTORY, an extracted digest in SIGNAL and BOTH) against retries that did not; the check is post hoc, running every resolved retry and every resolved first attempt of tasks with failures against the full test suite of its project (§IV.C).
- claim: retries that saw the previous attempt's test results "more often failed other tests that the reference fix passes", which shrinks their apparent gains; the authors call it an association and a hypothesis (abstract, §IV.C).
- evidence: resolved retries that broke a test that passed in both reference runs: NONE 3 of 54, REFLECT 3 of 67, HISTORY 11 of 71, SIGNAL 9 of 64, BOTH 16 of 72, spread over 7 to 10 tasks per condition. Suite-clean share of failures: 28.2, 35.6, 33.1, 30.4 and 30.9%; difference from NONE +5.0 [-4.3, +14.3] (HISTORY), +7.2 (REFLECT), +2.2 (SIGNAL), +2.8 (BOTH), all intervals include zero (Table V). Ignoring black's self-formatting checks and tqdm's timing tests: +6.1, +6.7, +3.9, +4.4 (Table V). Not run for M2 (§IV.D).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E3 because the denominators are post-treatment subsets (resolved retries), the counts are small, and the analysis and one sensitivity outcome were chosen after seeing the data. The baseline is the fixed version, not the buggy one, so a failing test is not necessarily a regression the patch introduced. Note that the primary Table II gain of HISTORY (+9.4) falls to +5.0 here, while REFLECT stays at +7.2.

### F10 · observation · retry-outcome-clustering-by-failure · E2
- practice: how retry outcomes are distributed across failures, tasks and conditions (§IV.C).
- claim: "outcomes varied far more between failures than between conditions"; the failure matters more than the memory (§I, §IV.C).
- evidence: 181 failures, each retried under all five conditions: 63 resolved under no condition, 17 under all five, 101 split. Intraclass correlation within a failure 0.40 (0.37 within a task); condition accounts for 0.6% of the outcome variance (§IV.C). Post hoc design calculation: a 7-point gain with 80% power would need about 106 to 210 tasks with failures (150 to 299 at alpha = 0.0125); a 10-point gain 52 to 103 (74 to 146) (§V).
- effect: none
- reader: E2, numbers with no comparison. The 0.6% share is small partly because the outcome is binary with condition means between 30% and 40%, as the authors say, so the ICC carries the point. The sizing figures assume the observed standard errors carry over to a larger task sample.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Two models of one family, one harness, one prompt wording per condition, no weight updates; M2 has three conditions and reuses the tasks that produced its hypothesis (§VI).
- The harness is tight: eight responses, low reasoning effort, 3,000 output tokens per response; 131 first attempts used all eight responses, so some failures are budget exhaustion and not wrong fixes (§VI).
- Pure-Python repositories without native extensions and with a linked issue; black contributes 62 of the 181 failures (§VI).
- Public BugsInPy fixes may be in training data (§VI).
- The reference fix is one valid solution; bug-revealing tests and the full-suite check are incomplete checks, and passing is not proof of a correct fix (§VI).
- Truncating the failure record may disadvantage HISTORY and REFLECT on long failures; the lesson was written by the same small model (§VI, §V).
- M1 was interrupted three times, M3's mini part is partial (156 of 181), and M3's gpt-5.4 part was resumed twice, against its own protocol; the paper lists these (§VI).
- The correctness of the lessons' diagnoses was not assessed (§IV.F).
- The study is exploratory; post hoc analyses are marked and their intervals are not adjusted for multiplicity (§III.D, §IV.C).

## Reader's notes

- The study authors designed the harness, the five conditions, the truncation limits and the SIGNAL digest rule, and evaluate those; `independent: true` holds because they evaluate generic retry practices, not a product of theirs, but all conditions are their own implementations.
- Every memory condition begins with a header that NONE lacks. M3 tests the header, but only for 156 of 181 failures with the mini model and without a SIGNAL arm, so F1, F3 and F4 contrasts with NONE confound content and header.
- The decision to complete the planned 75 tasks after an interim result of +18.6 is reported honestly, and the full-set result (+9.4) is what the paper uses; the interim figure is the case where a stopped study would have reported a significant gain.
- The scoring rule is the bug-revealing tests only, plus a one-time full-suite check on resolved attempts of M1. Conventions, readability and review outcomes are not scored.
- The lessons and the transcripts are built from a record truncated to 2,000 characters per message, with a first-attempt prompt of up to 32,000 bytes; the agent never sees a longer record, so the results may not transfer to untruncated retries.

## Discrepancies

- §V says a confirmatory study of a 7-point effect "would need roughly two to six times the present task set". The same paragraph gives 106 to 210 tasks with failures (150 to 299 at alpha = 0.0125). Against the 75 tasks that is 1.4 to 4.0 times; against the 52 tasks with failures it is 2.0 to 5.7 times (reader's arithmetic). The paper does not say which base it uses.
- The abstract says every memory condition gained "+5.5 to +9.9 points over 29.8%" and "none significantly"; Table II shows the BOTH interval [+1.5, +19.1] excluding zero before Holm adjustment. The text explains this, so it is a wording point more than a contradiction.
- §III.F says every M1 and M2 failure received four new retries; Table VII covers 156 of the 181 mini failures. The paper states the partial run (§IV.E, §VI).
- M3's protocol excluded resumption, and the gpt-5.4 part was resumed twice; §VI says the second resumption "breaks the first addendum's one resumption rule".
