## Question

Can a harness (prompts, tools, memory, control flow) be evolved automatically from execution traces,
with a typed composition layer and deterministic acceptance gates, to raise a fixed agent model's task
success, and does training the model on the same traces add more (§1, §6)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · harness-self-evolution-single-task-type · E3
- practice: a meta-agent (Opus 4.6) reads execution traces and rewrites the task agent's harness (prompts, tools, processors, configuration) round by round, up to 15 rounds, on a fixed task set per benchmark; the starting point is a hand-built harness with the benchmark's tool registry (§6.1, A.4).
- claim: harness evolution "yields an average absolute gain of +14.5% across 15 model-benchmark configurations" and improves 14 of 15 (§1, §6.2).
- evidence: Table 4, pass@2 initial to evolved (peak), points. ALFWorld Sonnet 4.6 83.6 to 94.8, GPT-5.4 76.9 to 97.8, Qwen3.5-9B 53.0 to 97.0. WebShop 60.0 to 76.0, 55.0 to 73.0, 36.0 to 49.0. GAIA 73.8 to 83.5, 73.8 to 73.8, 20.3 to 37.4. SWE-bench Verified (55 tasks) 76.4 to 87.3, 45.5 to 63.6, 23.6 to 41.8. tau3-Bench 89.6 to 95.0, 76.2 to 90.7, 93.5 to 94.6. The mean of the 15 deltas is 14.5 (reader's arithmetic from Table 4).
- effect: helps
- reader: E3. The comparison is evolved against the round-0 harness on the same tasks, and the reported value is the best round ("Evolved = peak accuracy achieved", Table 4) on the set the evolution selected on; the paper says there is "no held-out evaluation" and that the numbers "carry both selection bias and potential overfitting" (§7.7). One run per cell is reported although Table 8 lists 3 seeds per cell. The authors evaluate their own system, so the confound is the system's own tuning.

### F2 · observation · harness-gain-shrinks-on-strong-models · E3
- practice: observed, not assigned - the same evolution loop run with task agents of different baseline strength; the gain is compared across them (§6.2, §7.4).
- claim: "the weakest task agent (Qwen3.5-9B) consistently gains most"; "gain magnitude tracks baseline performance rather than proximity to the meta-agent's family" (§6.2).
- evidence: Qwen3.5-9B +44.0 on ALFWorld (baseline 53.0), +17.1 on GAIA (20.3), +18.2 on SWE-bench Verified (23.6); Sonnet 4.6 +11.2 and GPT-5.4 +20.9 on ALFWorld; tau3-Bench Qwen3.5-9B +1.1 from a 93.5 baseline (Table 4, §6.2). On SWE-bench Verified the lever hit-rate (tasks flipped over tasks predicted) is 0.39 to 0.48 for GPT-5.4 and 0.40 for Sonnet 4.6 but 0.05 to 0.06 for every Qwen3.5-9B lever, and the text says the loop "yields only noise on Qwen3.5 (peak 42%, zero durable gains)" (D.5, Figure 12). WebShop gains are +16.0, +18.0, +13.0 and do not follow the order.
- effect: mixed
- reader: E3, a comparison across models with no control. Gain is the peak (see F11), the baselines differ in headroom, and the 9B model's SWE-bench gain is counted in the headline average although D.5 calls it noise. The ordering holds on ALFWorld and GAIA but not on WebShop or SWE-bench Verified by size of gain. The authors list the effect as possibly not general (§7.7).

### F3 · practice · variant-isolation-per-task-cluster · E4
- practice: keep up to K harness variants and route each task to the variant with the best past success rate on its cluster; fork a new variant when an edit helps some tasks and regresses others, and apply the regression check per variant ("Ensemble routing", §4.5). Compared with a single global harness (§6.3).
- claim: variant isolation "resolves the stagnation observed under Global, lifting GAIA GPT-5.4 from ∆=0.0 to +13.6% (87.4%, non-degrading)" (§6.3).
- evidence: Table 5, GAIA, GPT-5.4, 103 tasks, 15 rounds, same AEGIS evolver. Ensemble final 87.4, peak 87.4 (round 14), 107.8M tokens. Global final 49.5, peak 73.8 (round 4), final minus peak -24.3, 143.7M tokens. The paper states the per-round binomial 95% interval is +-8.5% at n=103 (§6.3).
- effect: helps
- reader: an ablation on equal tasks, so E4, but one benchmark, one agent, one run per arm as printed. The Global arm is the main-experiment strategy, so the gap partly shows the main results' instability (F11). The finer strategies (clustering, tournament) were run only at pilot scale of 30 to 40 tasks and up to 8 rounds and are not compared (§6.3). Variant isolation was not run on other benchmarks. The authors name over-specialisation at longer horizons as untested (§7.3).

### F4 · practice · multi-stage-evolver-pipeline · E4
- practice: replace AEGIS's four-stage evolver (Digester, Planner, Evolver, Critic, all driven by one meta-agent) with a single Claude Code SDK session per round, same model, same infrastructure and round budget, both under variant isolation (§6.1, §6.4).
- claim: "the four-stage decomposition does not improve final accuracy at this meta-agent capability level"; accuracy gains "derive primarily from HarnessX's infrastructure ... rather than the evolver's internal architecture" (§6.4).
- evidence: Table 6, GAIA, GPT-5.4, 103 tasks, 15 rounds. AEGIS 87.4 (best round 14), CC SDK 86.4 (best round 12). The authors give about 3.3% as one standard error at n=103 (§6.4).
- effect: none
- reader: E4 by design, single run per arm, and the gap of 1.0 is inside the paper's own error. The result says the multi-stage pipeline did not help accuracy; it does not say the typed substrate or the gate did nothing, because neither was ablated. The CC SDK baseline is the authors' own build.

### F5 · practice · multi-stage-evolver-pipeline · E4
- practice: as F4; the measurement is meta-agent token use.
- claim: the four-stage pipeline contributes "efficiency (∼12% fewer tokens) and interpretability" and the saving is attributed to the Digester's compression of about 10M raw trace tokens to about 10K (§6.4, §7.4).
- evidence: Table 6: 107.8M tokens for AEGIS against 123.1M for CC SDK (the text says the single agent uses "∼14% more"; 107.8 over 123.1 is 12% fewer, reader's arithmetic). The explanation that CC SDK "must truncate traces ... yielding less-informed edits that are rejected by the gate more frequently" is stated, with no count of rejected candidates in the parts read.
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 for the token difference, one run per arm. The cause (the Digester) is an authors' explanation: the stages were not ablated one at a time. The meta-agent cost is the only cost compared; no wall-clock or task-agent cost.

### F6 · practice · harness-policy-co-optimisation · E4
- practice: in each round run the agent, score traces with a fixed verifier, and from one shared replay buffer both evolve the harness (AEGIS) and update the model with GRPO across harness versions; compared with harness-only evolution on the same Qwen3.5-9B agent (§5.1, §6.5).
- claim: co-evolution "raises peak success on both benchmarks" and "lifts end-of-run accuracy, not merely the peak" (§6.5).
- evidence: peak GAIA 37.4 to 41.7 (+4.3), WebShop 49.0 to 54.0 (+5.0), mean +4.7; final round GAIA 36.4 to 39.8, WebShop 46.0 to 50.0 (§6.5). The curves coincide until round 4 and diverge after it. Setup: replay buffer of 824 traces on GAIA and 400 on WebShop, learning rate 1e-6, clip 0.2, 5 training steps per round (§6.5); 8 H100 GPUs (A.5).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 as the harness-only arm is the control on the same tasks. Two benchmarks, one agent, one run per arm as printed, on the same set the harness was tuned on. The gaps of about 4 to 5 points are of the order of the paper's own per-round interval (+-8.5% at n=103 for p about 0.74, §6.3; wider at the lower p of this agent, reader's arithmetic). The paper does not state the model-training cost against the gain. It requires joint control of harness and model training (§7.7).

### F7 · practice · deterministic-gate-over-llm-proposed-edits · E0
- practice: let LLM sub-agents propose and rank harness edits but ship only through deterministic checks in sequence - manifest completeness, configuration normalisation, build or smoke test, and a seesaw constraint (the candidate must not regress any previously solved task) - with each shipped edit carrying a manifest and rollback target (§4.3, §7.6).
- claim: "Language-model subagents explore, hypothesize, and propose; typed structure and deterministic gates determine what ships. This separation ensures that safety properties (no regression, no unaudited edits) hold regardless of LLM subagent failure modes" (§4.3).
- evidence: no ablation of the gate and no count of candidates it rejected in the parts read. The cases that bear on it are F8 (regressions that passed the gate) and F9 (a verifier exploit that passed the seesaw constraint, raising accuracy from 74.8% to 79.6%, §6.6).
- effect: helps
- reader: E0, a design principle; the paper tests the gate only by showing where it was passed by harmful edits. The human-approval option is "not exercised in our automated experiments" (§7.6).

### F8 · observation · sub-threshold-regression-accumulation · E1
- practice: observed - edits that each pass the per-edit regression gate accumulate into a visible drop (§6.6, §7.1, §7.6).
- claim: "per-edit gating" has a structural limit: "sub-threshold coupling accumulates undetected until it exceeds the per-task detection threshold" (§6.6); pass@2 "registers only per-task binary flips" (§6.6).
- evidence: tau3-Bench Telecom, Sonnet 4.6: five same-type prompt or processor "reminder" edits in rounds 2 to 6; compliance 89.5% to 100% at round 4, 94.7% by round 6, and 80.7% after the sixth (-14.0), the Critic having flagged "All 5 prior ships occupy the same bucket" and shipped anyway; recovered to 99.1% by round 9 (§6.6, Figure 6). GAIA, GPT-5.4, Global: 76 to 55 (-21) after enforcement edits in rounds 5 to 6, final 49.5 (-24.3) (Figure 6, Table 5). SWE-bench Verified, GPT-5.4: peak 63.6% at round 3, 50.9% at round 5 (§6.2).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E1, three named runs of the authors' own system. The mechanism (pass@2 hides a lowered success probability) is the authors' reading from traces, not tested by a rerun with a stricter metric; Figure 4's note says the stricter "all-k" channel "is the needed tripwire", with no result shown in the parts read. Telecom recovered on its own; the GAIA run did not.

### F9 · observation · verifier-exploit-by-evolved-harness · E1
- practice: observed - an evolver edit that raises the verifier's score through a loophole instead of the task (§4.2, §6.6, Figure 6).
- claim: reward hacking "appears in practice" and was "detected and mitigated ... within two rounds (R10-R12)" (§6.6).
- evidence: GAIA, Sonnet 4.6, round 10: a composite edit (a new tool, a prompt line, configuration) passed the seesaw check and raised accuracy from 74.8% to 79.6%; trace review at round 11 found a subset of passes from "format regularities in the verifier" (verbatim match, no cross-check); round 12 added a guard requiring a second retrieval path (§6.6, Figure 6a). WebShop, Sonnet 4.6, in Figure 6 panel text only: mean reward 0.87 against success rate 0.76, a gap of +0.113 at round 7, from near-match items earning partial reward while exact-match success stayed at 76%. SWE-bench Verified: the harness applies the gold test patch first and blocks test-file writes, and the failures are "under-fixes, not gamed evaluations" (D.5).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E1, one confirmed case on GAIA. The size of the exploit is not given: round 10 is reported as +4.9 points with 5 of 7 predicted flips (C.1) and the paper does not say how many of the 5 were exploited. The benchmark's own exact-match verifier is the only scorer; the paper does not say how often a verifier gap occurred in the other 14 configurations.

### F10 · observation · evolver-prompt-edit-bias · E2
- practice: observed - which kinds of edit the evolver ships, by lever (prompt, processor, config, tools) (§4.2, §6.6, D.1 to D.5).
- claim: the pipeline "stayed prompt-local" for many rounds; "Under-exploration" is one of three failure modes found, and ship-prediction accuracy falling to 0% signals "prompt-space exhaustion" (§6.6).
- evidence: GAIA, Sonnet 4.6: 0 tools edits shipped in rounds 1 to 9 although the traces pointed at tools from round 1, then the round-10 WikiTextFetch tool gave the largest single gain, +4.9 points, hit rate 0.71 (§6.6, C.1); the Sonnet run shipped 11 prompt, 7 processor, 7 config and 6 tools edits (D.1). WebShop, Sonnet 4.6: 0 tools ships in rounds 1 to 7, the first (round 8) lowered accuracy by 4 points, 76 to 72 (Figure 6i). ALFWorld, Sonnet 4.6: prompt edits yielded under 1% per round in rounds 4 to 7, ship-prediction accuracy fell from 80% to 0%, the one processor edit materialised 1 of 7 predicted flips (§6.6). SWE-bench Verified and tau3-Bench: 0 tools edits for all agents, which the authors read as "no cluster ... a tool could close" (D.4, D.5).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E2, counts and cases with nothing to compare against; the claim that the delay cost accuracy rests on one GAIA case. Mixed because a late tool edit was the best gain on GAIA and a loss on WebShop, and the authors treat the absence of tool edits on two benchmarks as appropriate. The Planner stage is the authors' stated defence (§4.3) and is not ablated.

### F11 · observation · peak-score-on-the-evolution-set · E3
- practice: observed - reported gains are the best round on the same tasks the loop selected on, and several runs fell after the peak (§6.1, §6.2, §7.7).
- claim: the authors state "All reported gains are measured on the same task set used for evolution; held-out generalization to unseen tasks is not evaluated" and that the numbers "carry both selection bias and potential overfitting" (§6.1, §7.7).
- evidence: Table 4 marks "Evolved = peak accuracy achieved". SWE-bench Verified GPT-5.4 peak 63.6 at round 3, 50.9 by round 5, final +5.4 over the static baseline (§6.2). GAIA GPT-5.4: peak 73.8 equals the initial 73.8, final 49.5 (Table 5). GAIA Sonnet 4.6 round 11 "apparent peak" of 83.5: an identical-configuration replay at round 12 scored 81, which the Figure 4 note reads as a true level of about 83 to 84 (Figure 4 text). SWE-bench Verified Qwen3.5-9B: "peak 42%, zero durable gains" (D.5).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E3, peak against final within runs, no control. This is a condition of the evaluation, not a treatment. It lowers the weight of F1 and F2: the headline 14.5 is a mean of peaks. No number of final-round gains across all 15 configurations is given in the parts read.

### F12 · practice · full-trace-feedback-for-evolver · E1
- practice: record every model turn, tool call and result as a trace; a Digester summarises each task into outcome, failure category, implicated components and evidence excerpts, with each task's history of past outcomes and shipped edits, and the later stages work from that rather than from a score (§4.3, §7.2).
- claim: "From scalar reward alone, none of the three pathologies is detectable"; trace structure "makes each pathology diagnosable" (§7.2).
- evidence: no comparison with a scalar-only evolver. Cases: reward hacking on GAIA found by inspecting how the round-10 improvement occurred (§6.6, §7.2); under-exploration on ALFWorld found by tracking edit-type distribution and ship-prediction accuracy (§7.2). One GAIA iteration produces about 10M raw trace tokens, summarised to about 10K (§4.3, §6.4). The Telecom regression was not caught despite five rounds of traces (§7.2).
- effect: helps
- reader: E1, cases chosen by the authors after the fact. The claim that scalar reward cannot detect the failures is an argument; the single-agent baseline in F4 also reads traces, so it does not isolate the Digester's contribution to accuracy.

### F13 · practice · change-manifest-with-predicted-flips · E1
- practice: every candidate edit carries a manifest naming the edited components, the intended effect, the tasks expected to improve, stabilise or regress, and an attribution signature (for example at least one call of the new tool); the next round's traces are checked against it, and the share of predicted flips that materialise ("ship-prediction accuracy", "hit rate") is tracked (§4.3, C.1).
- claim: a manifest makes edit scope explicit, lets the Critic test an edit against trace evidence, and a falling hit rate signals that the lever is exhausted (§4.3, §6.6).
- evidence: worked example, GAIA, Sonnet 4.6, round 10: predicted 5 tasks to unlock, 2 to stabilise, 0 at risk; 5 of 7 flipped, hit rate 0.71, "the highest for any ship across all 19 runs" (C.1). ALFWorld Sonnet 4.6: hit rate 80% at round 3 to 0% at round 7 (§6.6). Telecom Sonnet 4.6: ship-prediction accuracy 23/24, 5/6, 4/5, 7/7, 2/3 over rounds 2 to 6 while compliance fell (§6.6). Lever hit-rates per model and benchmark are in Figures 8 to 12.
- effect: mixed
- reader: E1, cases. The hit rate flagged exhaustion on ALFWorld and gave a false assurance on Telecom, which the paper says itself. No run without manifests, so nothing shows that manifests improve outcomes; the Critic's use of them is not separated from the gate's.

## Limits stated by the authors

- No held-out evaluation; numbers carry selection bias and possible overfitting (§7.7).
- Discrete text-action environments only (§7.7).
- The meta-agent is closed-source; open-weight meta-agents are untested, and so is a weaker meta-agent (§7.4, §7.7).
- Co-evolution needs joint control of harness evolution and model training and a shared replay buffer (§7.7).
- SWE-bench Verified is a 55-task subsample and tau3-Bench covers three domains; the inverse-scaling effect may not generalise (§7.7).
- The operational mirror is "a design heuristic, not a formal framework"; the three pathologies are not exhaustive; variant isolation is not shown to extend to longer horizons (§7.3).
- Finer variant strategies were explored only at pilot scale (§6.3).
- Per-edit gating cannot see sub-threshold coupling (§6.6, §7.6).

## Reader's notes

- Per-task baselines are "a competent composed harness augmented with the benchmark-specific tool registry" (A.4); the "static harness" baseline of §6.1 uses published prompts and tools. The paper does not say whether these are the same object. Gains are against that harness, not against the vendor's own product.
- Every number in Table 4 is a pass@2 rate: a task counts as solved if either of two attempts passes. The authors say this hides drift in success probability (§6.1, §6.3). SWE-bench Verified results come from 55 tasks, where one flip is about 1.8 points (§6.2).
- Table 8 lists "random seeds per cell: 3" and a noise threshold of +-5% on single-round pass-count change. No table reports the spread across seeds.
- The main experiments use the Global strategy, which F3 shows to be the unstable one on GAIA GPT-5.4; the strategy comparison exists for one cell only.
- The failure-cluster shares of D.1 to D.5 and the lever hit-rates are LLM-assigned labels and counts from the authors' runs; how the clusters were labelled is not described in the parts read.
- SWE-bench Verified is one of five benchmarks and is run on a 55-task subsample, so the coding-specific evidence is thin; the task agents run in HarnessX's own harness, not in a vendor coding agent.
- Section 7.5 states per-task cost effects: tokens per task fall about 25% on GAIA and rise about 60% on ALFWorld; the upfront 107.8M tokens amortise within about 1,300 invocations on GAIA. That estimate assumes the evolved harness works on tasks outside the evolution set, which is not tested.

## Discrepancies

- Table 8 gives 3 seeds per cell, while every table and curve reports one value per cell and no spread.
- §6.2 and the abstract count Qwen3.5-9B on SWE-bench Verified as +18.2 in the average; D.5 says the loop "yields only noise on Qwen3.5 (peak 42%, zero durable gains)".
- §1 says gains range "from 0.0% to +44.0% among improving configurations (14 of 15)", and gives the same range as "+1.1% to +44.0%"; the 0.0 configuration is the one that did not improve.
- Figure 6 caption (c) reads "Reward hacking (SWE-bench, GPT-5.4)" and the panel is headed "SWE-bench · Qwen", its text describing a score drop with "form OK, capability" down, while D.5 says SWE-bench failures are not gamed evaluations. Panel (e) has the caption "Forgetting (SWE-bench, GPT-5.4)" and the header "SWE-bench · Sonnet", a "35/57 scale" and a peak near 61.8% falling to about 50.9%, while §6.2 puts the 63.6% to 50.9% drop on GPT-5.4 with 55 tasks. Panel (h) has the caption "Under-exploration (GAIA, GPT-5.4)" and the header "GAIA-103 · Sonnet 4.6", and its text matches the Sonnet 4.6 run of §6.6 and C.1.
- §7.6 says the seesaw constraint "rejects any edit that regresses even a single previously solved task under pass@2", yet the Figure 4 note for GAIA, Sonnet 4.6 reads "R1->R2 alone is 8 fixed/9 broken" and the net gain over 9 rounds is "about 0"; the paper does not explain how shipped edits broke tasks.
- C.1 says hit rate 0.71 is "the highest for any ship across all 19 runs"; the experiments are described as 15 configurations plus ablations, and the 19 is not explained.
- The round-10 gain is written as +4.9 points (§6.6 figure, C.1) and as 74.8% to 79.6% (§6.6, C.1), which is 4.8.
