## Question

When a coding-agent harness promises guidance before the first source edit, how often does the
guidance arrive before the first file-content change, given which requests the gate sees and
which plans are actually delivered (Abstract, §1, §3)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · editor-only-guidance-gate · E4
- practice: hold only structured editor requests (`replace_text`, `write_file`), obtain a plan from the same model and insert it before the held request is replaced; run on 39 prescribed single actions with a placeholder plan, each in a fresh workspace (§4.3, §5.7, Appendix A).
- claim: "Editor-only guidance reaches 3 of 24 chosen package-source changes" (§6).
- evidence: Table 8, editor requests only: TP 3, FP 6, FN 21, TN 9, 9 artifacts (§5.7). The suite contains 24 package-source changes and 15 nonchanges (§5.6); 30 of the 39 actions are shell actions, 21 of them actual changes (§5.6, Table 7).
- effect: hurts
- reader: same 39 actions under each policy, so E4 for the coverage claim. The 39 cells are chosen to include hard write forms and are "not sampled from a population of agent actions" (§4.3); 21 of 24 changes are shell forms by design (30 shell actions, §5.6). The count says what the gate can see, not how often agents write through the shell. The author wrote the gates, so `independent: false`.

### F2 · practice · literal-command-marker-gate · E4
- practice: hold editor requests plus any shell request whose command text contains one of nine fixed literal mutation markers taken from SI2CA's public rule (§4.3, Appendix A).
- claim: the literal rule reaches more changes than editor-only but cannot see writes done "by an indirectly invoked helper when no marker appears" (Appendix A).
- evidence: Table 8, editor or literal command marker: TP 9, FP 9, FN 15, TN 6, 18 artifacts, on the same 39 cells as F1 (§5.7).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 on equal constructed cells. Better coverage (9 of 24 against 3 of 24) comes with 9 guided actions that had no immediate source change against 6. The nine markers are the author's local copy, not SI2CA's gate itself (§4.3).

### F3 · practice · all-request-guidance-gate · E4
- practice: hold the first non-finish request of any kind, whatever tool it uses (§4.3, Appendix A).
- claim: an all-request gate "reaches all 24 and also delivers guidance on all 15 actions without an immediate package-source change" (§6).
- evidence: Table 8, every tool request: TP 24, FP 15, FN 0, TN 0, 39 artifacts (§5.7, Figure 5).
- effect: mixed
- reader: coverage is full by construction when every request is held. The 15 guided nonchanges are the cost in this suite; the author says this "does not say those plans would be wasted during a longer repair" (§5.7), since a plan before a read can change later edits.

### F4 · practice · all-request-guidance-gate · E4
- practice: the same all-request gate against the editor-only, literal-marker and shell-capability gates in live local runs of a 7B agent: 30 test tasks, 2 temperature-zero seeds, 60 arms per policy (§4.3, §5.10).
- claim: "selective policies deliver 6-8 plans per 60 arms, while all-request delivers 55" (§6).
- evidence: Table 11, delivered plans: Editor 7, Literal 6, Capability 8, All 55 of 60 arms. Plan delivered with no observed Python delta: 4, 2, 4 and 53. Planner output tokens 1,540, 1,320, 1,528 and 8,980; successful-call seconds 122.4, 114.4, 113.4 and 794.5. Changed arms 3, 4, 4 and 2. In the 23-task development cohort every policy delivered 23 plans and the Planner output tokens were 3,479, 3,479, 3,479 and 3,446, because 22 of 23 tasks first reached an editor request under every policy (Table 10, §6).
- effect: hurts
- reader: A/B on equal tasks, so E4. Tokens and seconds count Planner calls only, "not total agent cost" (Table 11 caption), and the plan was never shown to be unnecessary. The 7B at temperature 0 produced near-identical seeds (F13), so n is closer to 30 than 60. Qualifies the 14B result in F5.

### F5 · practice · all-request-guidance-gate · E4
- practice: compare the share of arms that change Python source under each gate, in three cohorts (§5.9, §5.10, §5.11).
- claim: "The all-request gate produces more source-changing arms in this selected cohort", but "neither a correctness gain nor a general gate ranking" (§6).
- evidence: changed arms. Development cohort, 23 tasks per policy: Editor 6, Literal 6, Capability 6, All 5 (Table 10). Follow-up, 60 arms per policy: 3, 4, 4, 2 (Table 11). 14B cohort, 38 arms per policy: editor-only 5 and all-request 10; task-cluster bootstrap difference 0.132, 95% percentile interval 0.026 to 0.263 (§5.11).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the sign changes with the cohort: no difference at 7B in the first cohort, a lower count for all-request in the follow-up, a higher count for all-request at 14B. A "change" is any content delta, not a repair (F13). The 14B model and task frame were chosen after pilots, "this adaptive choice may favor the selected task frame" (§4.3). Flask supplies no change, so the bootstrap has no within-repository variation for it. The 7B and 14B cohorts are not pooled.

### F6 · practice · plan-before-first-edit · E2
- practice: hold the first eligible request, call the Planner once, insert its plan into the Executor's conversation, and let the Executor choose a new action; compare plan insertion with the first observed Python-content delta from before/after hashes (§4.3, Figure 2).
- claim: "Guidance precedes every observed first change" (Abstract); the audit "makes the timing claim testable within a stated source scope".
- evidence: development cohort, plans preceded the first change in all 23 mutation-bearing arms (§5.9). Follow-up, every first delta followed a delivered plan in 13 of 13 changed arms, from three tasks in two repositories (§5.10). 14B cohort, 15 of 15 changed arms, from seven tasks in three repositories (§5.11). In all three cohorts every first change followed `replace_text`.
- effect: helps
- reader: a count with nothing to compare against, so E2, and the outcome is timing only. Every gate holds the editor request and every first change used the editor, so the ordering follows from the design; no live run exercised a shell-first write (§5.10, §5.11). No repair outcome is graded and the abstract says the data "do not show that planning improves repair correctness". The Planner is the same small local model as the Executor, and plan content is not examined.

### F7 · practice · plan-before-first-edit · E3
- practice: assign tasks to the PAUSE condition, which pauses at an editor request and inserts a plan, against a baseline run of the same task; the author re-derives the counts from the released trajectories of the PAUSE paper (§4.1, §5.1).
- claim: the released arm-level comparisons are positive but "cannot be read as per-trajectory treatment effects because the task-matched arms are independent runs" (§5.1).
- evidence: baseline to PAUSE solved tasks: 251 to 325 (SWE-agent Verified, 500 tasks), 85 to 129 (SWE-agent Multilingual, 300), 185 to 215 (Pi Verified, 500), 227 to 311 (OpenHands Verified, 500) (§5.1, Table 2). Pi triggered in 261 of 500 runs against 465 of 500, 289 of 300 and 486 of 500 for the others (F11).
- effect: helps
- reader: this paper only reproduces the counts, which come from the PAUSE authors' own evaluation of their system; the reader grades the PAUSE result as reported, not this paper's work. Between-group comparison with no control that fixes the pre-edit state. The Pi gain of 30 tasks was reached with delivery in about half the runs (F11), so the harness does not identify what delivery caused.

### F8 · practice · literal-command-marker-gate · E3
- practice: count "mutation-like" commands by literal substring match, the rule in SI2CA's released code, and use the count to decide when to branch or hold (§2.3, §5.5, §5.6).
- claim: the lexical counter measures command forms, not file changes: "These constructed cases directly validate that the gate measures mutation-like command forms, not file-content changes" (§5.5).
- evidence: on 30 shell actions with known bytes the SI2CA marker finds 6 of 21 actual source changes and marks 3 of 9 nonchanges; the 15 misses are five shell forms repeated over three repositories, the 3 false positives are same-content `tee` rewrites; an exit code 7 still changed the file (§5.6, Table 7). On SI2CA's released standard-arm trajectories 473 of 10,240 runs with a complete existing-Python diff contain a marker-negative `cp`/`mv`, `python3 -c` or `perl -i` candidate with exit code 0; in the discovered-strategy arm 347 of 10,218 runs, 82 of them inside the active window (Table 6, §5.5). Of 114 command-level active-window candidates, 10 in 10 repositories have identical reads before and after that return different source text; all 10 are `python3 -c` writers, and the marker tuple lists `python -c` but not `python3 -c` (§5.5).
- effect: hurts
- reader: the constructed cells show the detector's behaviour on chosen forms; the author says they do not give "precision or recall in agent populations". The trace counts are candidates, not confirmed writes (exit code 0 does not prove a delta). The two SI2CA arms are different generated trajectories, so 473 against 347 is not a gate effect. The 10 changed-read cases are a strict subset with unknown recall.

### F9 · practice · per-action-source-hash-observer · E3
- practice: hash every regular `.py` file in the working repository before and after each executed action, take the first nonempty difference as the first durable source change, and replay the recorded actions from the base commit without model calls (§4.3, Appendix B).
- claim: guidance timing is testable only with such state observation; command text, final diffs and exit codes cannot place the first mutation (§4.2, §6.1).
- evidence: the observer agrees with a direct target-byte comparison in all 39 constructed cells (24 changes, 15 nonchanges) (§5.6). Replay: 92 of 92 development arms matched; 236 of 240 follow-up arms reproduce exact output text (4 pip retry outputs differ, order, exit codes and hashes match); 75 of 76 14B arms byte-exact, the 76th differs in an ephemeral checkout path (§5.9, §4.3, Appendix B). The released PAUSE and SI2CA traces lack per-turn file state and "cannot identify exact first mutations" (Abstract).
- effect: helps
- reader: validated against a ground truth the same author built, on 39 chosen cells, so E3. The observer misses a write reverted within one action, non-Python files and symlinks (Appendix B). Replay checks recorded actions and hashes, not that the model generated them (Appendix B). The author's checklist for a reusable experiment (policy version, path set, ordered tool-call IDs, workspace state before and after each action) is in §6.1.

### F10 · practice · shell-capability-guidance-gate · E3
- practice: treat the general shell tool as writable and hold every shell request, even a read-only one (§4.3, Appendix A).
- claim: with one general shell, capability-based selectivity "collapses to all-request gating for these intervals" (§5.8).
- evidence: on 19 selected writer commands in 17 runs from PAUSE Pi and SI2CA traces plus their 38 flanking read slots: editor-only held 0 and 0; editor or literal marker 8 and 0; editor or any shell 19 and 38; every request 19 and 38 (Table 9). Live follow-up: Capability attempted 10 held requests against 8 for Editor and delivered 8 plans against 7 (Table 11); two `pytest --fixtures` inspections were held by it, and none of those arms changed source (§5.10, §6).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the 19 intervals are selected positive witnesses, not a sample, and eligibility is evaluated offline on commands already run: "we did not insert guidance into the publisher runs". The live difference is 2 held requests. Catches every shell write, and every shell read as well.

### F11 · practice · path-scoped-editor-trigger · E3
- practice: trigger planning at an editor request only when its target path lies in a harness-specific source-directory list (PAUSE's design, as described in the released dataset card) (§4.1, §5.3).
- claim: delivery is incomplete, and the trigger scope can miss successful source edits on paths outside the list; the released cases "do not support a broken editor hook claim" (§5.3).
- evidence: `paused=true` in 465 of 500 SWE-agent Verified, 289 of 300 Multilingual, 261 of 500 Pi Verified and 486 of 500 OpenHands Verified runs; a stored plan in 465, 288, 260, 486 (Table 2). Across 500 shared Verified task IDs, SWE-agent and OpenHands both trigger in 452, SWE-agent only in 13, OpenHands only in 34, neither in 1 (§5.1). Of the 34 OpenHands-only tasks, 22 SWE-agent no-trigger runs have a successful direct-editor edit of an existing Python file; 19 are pytest-dev (all 19 pytest-dev SWE-agent runs), 2 Matplotlib, 1 Flask (§5.1, §5.3). An inactive prefix list in the PAUSE TeX source excludes `src/_pytest/`, `lib/mpl_toolkits/` and `src/flask/` and fits all 465 SWE-agent trigger paths and all 260 linked Pi editor targets (§5.3).
- effect: mixed
- reader: counts from other authors' runs with different harnesses, tools and path rules, no control; the author says the Pi contrast "does not identify a harness effect". The list is "not authenticated as the live harness configuration", so the path explanation is a fit, not a finding. The 22 witnesses are 3 repository-level examples. Delivery is high in three cohorts (93% to 97%) and 52% in Pi (reader's arithmetic from Table 2).

### F12 · observation · editor-failure-shell-fallback · E2
- practice: observed in 239 Pi runs with no recorded PAUSE trigger: the agent's structured editor call returns an error and the agent then writes the same file through the shell (§5.2, Table 3).
- claim: the sequence "is compatible with editor failure and fallback to shell", a route outside an editor-request hook (§5.2).
- evidence: 213 of 239 runs have a final diff and 136 modify an existing Python file. In 128 of the 136 a direct editor request on the modified path returns an error, 125 with `edits.0: must be object`. In 90 an errored editor call precedes a same-path shell-write candidate, 76 with a later candidate reporting no error (Table 3). Strict check: 9 clean shell-write commands in 7 runs sit between identical reads that return different source text, all with an earlier errored editor request, 3 runs in django and 4 in sympy (§5.2). Of 211 clean same-path write candidates, only 9 meet the strict pattern. The 90 are spread over nine repository groups, django 40 and sympy 21 (§5.2).
- effect: hurts
- reader: observed on final diffs and command text; exact first mutations are not established, and the 202 other candidates are "not observed no-ops". The path may not have met PAUSE's hidden package-directory rule. Repository clusters, "not 90 independent estimates". The error is a tool-call schema error, so the finding is about a tool interface, not about agents in general.

### F13 · observation · source-change-as-repair-proxy · E2
- practice: observed in the arms that changed source after a delivered plan: what the changed files do under local tests (§5.10, §5.11, Table 13).
- claim: "Source change also proved a weak repair proxy" (§5.11); "The local studies show when guidance reached constrained agents, not whether planning improved repair quality" (§6.2).
- evidence: 14B cohort, 15 changed arms (13 distinct final patches): 7 pass target and tested regressions (3 with the full listed regression set, 4 with a partial set), 5 fail a target, 1 passes the target and fails a regression, 2 cannot collect because the edited module is invalid (Table 13, §5.11). 4 changed arms have a Python file that fails compile; 4 replace a file with less than half its line count; one pytest arm shrinks a 662-line module to 8 lines and still compiles. 7B follow-up, 13 changed arms: 6 Requests-2148 arms edit `requests/adapters.py` while the reference patch names `requests/models.py` and fail the selected FAIL_TO_PASS test; all 5 pytest-8365 arms insert a call to an undefined `sanitize_username` and an isolated call raises `NameError` (§5.10).
- effect: mixed
- reader: no official SWE-bench grader was run; the local environment "neither reproduces the official environment nor establishes a gate effect on repair quality" (§5.11). Tests were run post hoc on changed arms only, so the other 61 arms are ungraded. The observation does not compare gates. It bounds what F5 and F6 mean: an early plan preceded changes that were wrong in about half the graded cases.

## Limits stated by the authors

- All first source changes in the live cohorts used a structured editor, so shell-first writes in live agent runs are unmeasured; the studies miss the prespecified 20-arm target (Abstract, §6.2, §7).
- No complete repair outcomes were graded and no official SWE-bench evaluation was run; the studies cannot say whether planning improved repair (Abstract, Appendix C).
- Source changes come from few tasks and few repositories: 6 tasks in 4 repositories (development), 3 tasks in 2 (follow-up), 7 tasks in 3 (14B) (§6.2).
- The inactive PAUSE path list is not the authenticated live configuration, so case-level eligibility is not established (§6.2).
- The two 20-case samples were inspected in one preliminary, tool-assisted pass with no independent human coding; no recall or inter-rater agreement is claimed (§4.3, §6.2).
- The 39 cells are constructed and the strict witness intervals are selected positives; neither gives field precision or recall (§6.2).
- The observer misses transient writes reverted inside one action, symlinks, non-Python source and concurrent writers (§6.2, Appendix B).
- The 14B model was chosen after pilots and the cohort is "not a random held-out sample of coding tasks" (§6.2).
- A journal submission "still needs outcome-aware evidence, independent human coding and scientific review, and a public reproduction package" (§6.2).

## Reader's notes

- The author writes the gates, the Planner and the observer, and then evaluates them on constructed and chosen cases; all E4 findings measure what the author's own gates do on equal inputs. `independent: false`.
- The Planner and the Executor are the same 7B or 14B model; whether a plan helps was not studied. Plan content is not examined at all, only its position in the trace.
- Single author, private artifacts, and OpenAI ChatGPT and Codex used for analysis code, drafting and the preliminary trace review (§4.3, AI Declaration). The numbers in the text could not be re-run.
- The task-paired comparisons of the PAUSE data are descriptive only (§4.5). In the no-trigger subsets the baseline and PAUSE-assigned outcomes disagree on 24 of 299 pairs, 15 baseline-only solved and 9 PAUSE-only (reader's arithmetic from Table 5); the paper reports this as run-to-run noise, with no claimed direction. Not graded as a finding.
- The SWE-rebench figure (144, 158 and 158 of 1,110 within-task run pairs disagree on the external grade for three agents, §5.4) is cited from prior work as context, not measured here.
- Temperature 0 with two seeds gave both first model responses in 118 of 118 available seed pairs identical, and executed action sequences matched in 108 of 120 pairs (§5.10); at 0.7, 12 of 38 repeat pairs began with identical text and 1 of 38 had the same action sequence (§5.11). The model differs between the two settings (7B and 14B), so seed diversity is confounded with model size. Not graded as a finding.
- The advisory instruction to inspect the checkout before editing was followed on the first action in 208 of 240 follow-up arms (§5.10); a 7B model with a JSON action schema, no comparison without the instruction.

## Discrepancies

- The abstract and §5.11 say 4 of the 7 passing 14B arms use partial regression lists (3 full, 4 partial); §6 says "partial regression coverage in two of those passes".
