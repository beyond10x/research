## Question

Which mechanisms form the harness through which teams govern coding agents, with specifications as
the central artifact (RQ1), and which human–agent interaction patterns work and how do human roles
change (RQ2) (§1, §3.1)? The paper proposes a framework and states that it is "a set of falsifiable
hypotheses", not a validated method (§10).

## Findings

### F1 · observation · individual-gain-without-team-gain · E3
- practice: observed in industry reports the paper cites, not run by the authors: teams with higher GenAI adoption against lower, on individual output and on team-level delivery (§1).
- claim: individual productivity rises while team throughput, review capacity and stability degrade; "the limiting factor is not model capability alone" but the capacity of review, verification and integration (§1, Abstract).
- evidence: Faros AI report, more than 10,000 developers in 1,255 teams: +21% individual task completion, +98% merged pull requests, +91% PR review time, +9% defects per developer (§1). DORA 2024: higher AI adoption associated with reduced delivery performance and instability; DORA 2025: the association with throughput reversed, instability persisted (§1). The authors add an Amdahl's-law argument (§1).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E3 because the numbers compare adoption levels with no control, and they are second-hand: the paper reports them from the cited reports ([14], [18], [19]) and does not describe how they were measured. The METR trial it also cites (experienced developers 19% longer while believing about 20% faster, §1) is a controlled study, graded in 2507.09089 and not here. Mixed because output went up and review time and defects went up. Association over adoption levels, not an effect of any practice.

### F2 · observation · refactoring-share-decline-and-duplication · E3
- practice: observed in an industry report the paper cites: code changed between 2020 and 2024, split by year (§1).
- claim: the degradation shows "not only in how fast teams deliver, but also in the maintainability of what they deliver" (§1).
- evidence: across 211 million changed lines authored between 2020 and 2024 the share associated with refactoring fell from 25% to below 10%, and duplicated code blocks increased eightfold ([17], §1).
- effect: hurts
- reader: a before and after over years with no control for what else changed; second-hand; the paper does not say which lines were AI-authored. The title of the cited report, as listed in the references, says "4x Growth in Code Clones" (see Discrepancies).

### F3 · practice · system-and-feature-specification-both-loaded · E0
- practice: keep two levels of written specification and load both into the agent's context: a system specification (architecture, conventions, normative rules; kept in a rule file such as `AGENTS.md` or `CLAUDE.md`, loaded at every session start) and a per-task feature specification (what is built now, acceptance criteria; archived after delivery) (§4.1).
- claim: "When only the feature specification is loaded, the agent produces code that is functionally correct but architecturally inconsistent; ... when only the system specification is loaded, the agent cannot ground the current task" (§4.1). Mixing the two causes rigidity or architectural drift (§4.1).
- evidence: none; the claim is argued. A worked example for context engineering is a constructed e-commerce case in which a UTC timestamp rule, moved from a wiki into the system specification, "prevents the entire class of defect, for every future feature, at the cost of one line" (§5.3).
- effect: helps
- reader: E0. The worked examples are "illustrative and academic constructions of the authors, not reports of specific industrial systems" (§5), so they are not cases. This restates the instruction-file practice of 2602.11988 F3 with a feature-level counterpart; no data in this paper on either.

### F4 · practice · standing-norms-in-always-loaded-file-not-skill · E0
- practice: put standing conventions and prohibitions in the always-loaded rule file and procedures ("how to do X") in on-demand skills, not the other way round (§4.1, §5.7).
- claim: "A team that places a standing convention in an on-demand skill will find the agent violating it in every session that does not happen to trigger the skill"; procedural content in the rule file makes the always-loaded context "unnecessarily large and harder to maintain" (§5.7, §4.1).
- evidence: none; argued from the load mechanism.
- effect: helps
- reader: E0. The mechanism is plausible and matches the skill-listing findings in this collection (2610.11647), but this paper tests nothing. No size at which the rule file becomes too large is given.

### F5 · practice · accepted-review-comments-as-instruction-rules · E0
- practice: when a reviewer applies the same correction repeatedly (the example: after the third repetition), turn it into a normative clause added to the rule file by reviewed pull request, pair it with a lint check whose violation message repeats the rule text, and let the agent draft the clause when it sees a human refactor its output the same way again and again (§5.7, §6.1 Pattern 4).
- claim: the agent "self-corrects before a human ever sees the code"; tacit norms the team never wrote down surface (§5.7).
- evidence: one constructed example, a service-layer exception convention (§5.7). No counts.
- effect: helps
- reader: E0 because the example is constructed. The practice is the paper's description of how a human turn (a repeated correction) becomes a standing rule, which bears on what human turns are replaceable; the paper gives no rate of repeated corrections or of lint-driven self-correction.

### F6 · practice · decision-store-read-and-written-per-session · E0
- practice: keep a persistent store of decisions, rationale, session summaries and discoveries, read at the start of every agent session and written at its end; it holds summaries not transcripts, is retrieved lazily by ranked search, and is neutral to agent and tool (§5.4).
- claim: it is "the key enabler of teamwork" (§5.2); it lets stateless sessions "behave as a team", and amortizes the token and time cost of re-explaining and re-deriving context (§5.4); the marginal cost of supervision "should decrease over time" (§8.1).
- evidence: one constructed two-day example (refund idempotency decision carried across a session, a tool and a sub-agent) (§5.4). No measurement. §8.1 calls the benefits "predictions of the framework, not yet validated findings".
- effect: helps
- reader: E0; constructed example and an explicit statement by the authors that the benefit is a prediction. Overlaps in kind with `external-work-ledger` and `user-approved-project-memory`.

### F7 · practice · scoped-consultation-request-recorded-against-spec · E0
- practice: the agent suspends when it meets a decision it cannot or should not resolve and raises a request with the question, the options, the specification clauses involved and the consequence of each option; the human answers, and the resolution is committed with the specification so the question is not asked twice (§5.8, §6.1 Pattern 2).
- claim: replaces continuous supervision, which "recreates the bottleneck"; "human attention is concentrated at the points of highest leverage"; consultation decides before the fact, review reads after it (§5.8).
- evidence: one constructed example, pagination strategy for a listing endpoint (§5.8). The request format is adopted from a proposal by Hassan et al., 2509.06216 (§5.8). The paper notes "an unanswered consultation blocks parallel agents" (§6.1).
- effect: helps
- reader: E0. This is the paper's account of which human turns are load-bearing: scoped decisions the agent surfaces. No response times, counts of consultations or defects avoided are given.

### F8 · practice · evidence-pack-in-place-of-code-reading · E0
- practice: define done as a structured evidence bundle, not "the PR is merged": executed acceptance tests mapped to specification clauses, CI check output, a short rationale, and links to the specification version and the rules in force; the reviewer audits whether the evidence discharges the contract (§5.9).
- claim: auditing evidence is "less cognitively demanding and more informative per minute spent" than reading unfamiliar code, and "makes the review bottleneck ... structurally addressable" (§5.9). The five criteria are functional completeness, verification soundness, maintainability, rationale and auditability, taken from Hassan et al. (§5.9).
- evidence: none beyond a constructed example (§5.9).
- effect: helps
- reader: E0. Whether reviewers catch defects in an evidence pack that they would catch in the diff is untested; the paper itself lists "evidence-pack completeness" as a construct still to be operationalised (RA5, §9).

### F9 · practice · autonomy-level-per-task-class · E0
- practice: set the autonomy level per class of work as an explicit, versioned, revisable decision, for example dependency patch updates fully autonomous, ordinary features autonomous with consultation and an evidence review at the end, and changes to the authentication module only with a human-approved plan and step-wise review (§5.10).
- claim: "an agent given full autonomy will sometimes deliver well and sometimes drift; an agent given none is a slow autocomplete"; calibration is revised from observed incidents (§5.10).
- evidence: one constructed example, including a quarterly recalibration (§5.10). The "ladder rather than a switch" idea is cited from Hassan et al. (§5.10).
- effect: helps
- reader: E0. No data on how classes were chosen or whether outcomes differ by level. Related to `bounded-observable-reversible-autonomy`, which has survey data in another paper.

### F10 · practice · separate-worktree-per-parallel-agent · E0
- practice: give each parallel agent its own linked git working tree, with its own index and branch, on one shared object store, instead of one shared working directory (§5.6).
- claim: agents are then isolated at file level, "conflicts move from active-work time, where they are silent and corrupting, to merge time, where standard tooling detects and surfaces them"; it is "the cornerstone for parallel agentic work during 2026" (§5.6). The authors state a limit: isolation covers code, not runtime; parallel agents still share ports, databases and external services unless separated (§5.6).
- evidence: none; argued. A constructed example has three agents implementing three idempotency designs in separate trees (§5.6).
- effect: helps
- reader: E0. In this collection 2611.00689 F1 compared a shared tree with separate clones and reports a mixed result; this paper's claim is not tested against it.

### F11 · practice · testable-acceptance-criteria-in-spec · E0
- practice: write specifications so that acceptance criteria are testable (Given–When–Then), constraints are checkable by a tool (ownership check in CI, API conformance against OpenAPI) and decisions are explicit; run the cycle proposal, apply (the agent derives artifacts without improvising and suspends to consult on ambiguity), archive (§4.1, §5.5).
- claim: "a constraint that no tool can enforce is a wish rather than a constraint"; "in our experience, a specification that is consumed by an agent and validated by tests stays alive, whereas one that is only read by humans decays" (§5.5). The Gherkin approach is said to be "already validated" by Zhang et al. (§5.5).
- evidence: no numbers in this paper. The cited validation (ACM TOSEM 2025, doi 10.1145/3702987) is not described beyond that sentence. A constructed refund example (§5.5). The author-experience statement is not a documented case.
- effect: helps
- reader: E0. The one external support is a cited study the paper does not report on, and "in our experience" is not a case.

### F12 · practice · fragmentary-adoption-of-spec-discipline · E0
- practice: adopt only part of the harness, for example writing feature specifications but neither encoding norms nor persisting decisions (§5.11).
- claim: the team "obtains a fraction of the benefit while paying most of the discipline cost"; this is "consistent with the empirical pattern that partial, ungoverned adoption can degrade team outcomes [18]" (§5.11).
- evidence: none for the claim; the cited DORA 2024 finding concerns AI adoption without governance, not partial adoption of a specification discipline (§1, §5.11). The authors list "whether partial adoption is in fact worse than none" as an open question (RA4, §9).
- effect: hurts
- reader: E0. The paper states the claim and, in §9, that it is untested. Exploratory work with unknown requirements may suit a lighter discipline, which the authors also say (§8.2).

## Limits stated by the authors

- The work is "a conceptual socio-technical framework rather than a validated engineering methodology"; its benefits and risks are "theoretical predictions" (§8.1, §10).
- The evidence base is mostly grey literature, not peer reviewed, and the key terms (agentic SE, vibe coding, harness, specification) are not agreed (§1, §8.3).
- Whether SDD mitigates the productivity paradox "remains an empirical question" (§8.3).
- The motivating evidence skews to web, SaaS and platform engineering; embedded, safety-critical and scientific software, and regulated domains, are untested (§8.3).
- Operational detail (artifact schemas, autonomy thresholds, the thickness of the technical harness) will date as models improve (§8.3).
- Worked examples are constructed, not industrial reports (§5).
- No adoption or maturity model is proposed because cross-organisational evidence is thin and largely self-reported (RA4, §9).

## Reader's notes

- The selection counts are 120 database hits and 94 included (reader's arithmetic from §3.4), but the paper does not say how many unique sources entered the synthesis after the 9 duplicates, and gives no counts for the grey literature. The extraction matrix is in the Zenodo package only.
- Table 4 links source clusters to constructs, not individual claims to sources. Which claim in §5 rests on which source cannot be checked from the paper. Nothing in the parts read says how many researchers coded the evidence or with what agreement; §8.3 says only that consensus was sought "in each iteration".
- The claim in §5.1 and §5.2 that the technical harness depreciates while the methodological one appreciates is argued, not measured. It cites one practitioner post ([27]).
- Every finding F3 to F12 describes a design the authors propose; all are graded E0 because the only illustrations are constructed. A reader looking for effect sizes finds only F1 and F2, both second-hand industry numbers.
- The terms of F1 conflate adoption of GenAI assistants and of agents; the cited reports predate most agent use, and the paper does not separate them.
- The paper uses the AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md rule file as the physical form of the system specification and says one file serves as specification, guide and norms (§5.11). That claim is a definition, not a result.

## Discrepancies

- §1 says duplicated code blocks "increased eightfold [17]"; the reference list gives [17] the title "AI Copilot Code Quality: 2025 Data Suggests 4x Growth in Code Clones". The paper does not reconcile the two.
- The Abstract says the analysis "draws predominantly on gray literature"; §3.4 reports counts only for four academic databases (94 included of 120) and none for grey sources.
- §1 says no peer-reviewed study has defined or measured the effects of SDD or the harness, while §5.5 says Zhang et al. "already validated" the Gherkin approach, a peer-reviewed study of a related practice; the paper does not say what that validation measured.
- §8.3 (Dependability) says the review is a process "that any researcher can replicate"; the grey-literature search terms, dates and source lists are not given in the text (§3.2 names source types and people).
