## Question

When an agent compacts its context for the question at hand, does the compacted text still serve
the questions that come later, and which compaction method serves them best (Abstract, §1)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · known-question-sentence-selection · E4
- practice: keep the sentences that gpt-6-luna judges necessary for the current ("known") question, with one Boolean per sentence, at 25% of the text; compare with random deletion at the same budget, on the known question (§2, §3).
- claim: selection for the known question beats random deletion on that question (§3).
- evidence: MultiRC, gpt-6-luna, 25%: known-question accuracy 77% after typed selection against 67% after random deletion and 89% on the full text; open-decider 70%, 61% and 74% (§3, Table 1). Token-matched typed − random +11.4 [+9.1, +13.8] points (§3). qwen3.8-flash as reader +10.4 [+7.4, +13.3] (§3). On FEVER token-matched +35.7 and +20.8 at 25% and 50% (Appendix G).
- effect: helps
- reader: random deletion on the same paragraphs, paired, so E4; the outcome is multiple-choice question answering over public QA text, not a coding task. Typed selection is the authors' own instance; MMR and LongLLMLingua, which are others' methods, show the same sign (§3).

### F2 · practice · known-question-sentence-selection · E4
- practice: as F1; the measurement is accuracy on the later ("hidden") questions of the same MultiRC paragraph, which were not known when the text was cut (§2, §3).
- claim: "no method that selects sentences for the current question does detectably better than deleting sentences at random" on later questions, on MultiRC average (Abstract, §3).
- evidence: typed − random hidden accuracy −0.8 [−1.9, +0.3] (gpt-6-luna) and −0.6 [−1.9, +0.7] (open-decider) at 25% (Table 1); token-matched −0.4 [−1.5, +0.6]; LongLLMLingua −1.6 [−2.6, −0.6]; TF-IDF −1.3 [−2.3, −0.4]; MMR −0.1 [−1.0, +0.9] (Table 12). Question-agnostic selection also near random (−0.8 and +0.3, Table 1).
- effect: none
- reader: the "none" holds for MultiRC. On FEVER the same practice gains +17.2 on hidden claims and on tau-bench +22.3 (F3, F10), so the paper's own abstract sentence is MultiRC-scoped. Intervals are unadjusted for multiple comparisons (§8).

### F3 · observation · hidden-question-evidence-overlap · E3
- practice: none applied; the hidden questions are split by whether their annotated evidence shares a sentence with the known question's evidence (§2, §3).
- claim: later questions gain from selection for the known question only where they share its evidence; disjoint ones lose or gain only where more of their evidence is kept (§3, §6).
- evidence: MultiRC 25%, typed − random: overlapping +4.3 [+2.2, +6.4] (gpt-6-luna) and +5.3 [+2.9, +7.7] (open-decider); disjoint −2.9 [−4.3, −1.4] and −3.0 [−4.5, −1.4]; disjoint evidence kept 18% against 25% (gpt-6-luna), 14% against 25% (open-decider) (§3, Table 8). On Evidence Inference disjoint claims gain +9.8 [+7.4, +12.1] with 46% against 25% of their evidence kept (Table 8). Gain follows extra evidence kept at 0.37 points per point across eight groups (§3); a second experiment with a different random known question per document misses the fitted line by 3.4 points on average, with misses of 9.5 and 6.9 (§3, Appendix F).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the overlap split is post hoc on MultiRC and FEVER and prespecified only on Evidence Inference (Table 8 caption); the Evidence Inference prediction that disjoint claims would not gain was not met (Appendix A). Intervals are on groups the authors defined after seeing the data.

### F4 · practice · question-agnostic-word-compression · E4
- practice: compress the text with LLMLingua-2, which deletes individual words and ignores the question, at the same token budget as random sentence deletion; compare with random sentence deletion and typed selection on the hidden questions (§4, Appendix G).
- claim: deleting words does better than deleting sentences on later questions (Abstract, §4).
- evidence: hidden accuracy at 25% of tokens, MultiRC gpt-6-luna 80.7 against 68.6 for random sentences, +12.1 [+11.0, +13.3]; open-decider 68.0 against 62.2, +5.9 [+4.7, +7.0]; FEVER label accuracy 82.2 against 61.2, +21.0 [+18.6, +23.2] (Table 12). Against typed selection on hidden questions it is ahead in all three cells (§4, Table 2). Evidence Inference at the nominal rate: L2 − random +0.2 [−1.5, +1.9] and typed − L2 +12.2 [+10.1, +14.4] at 25%, +1.0 [−0.5, +2.6] at 50%, with 28.9% against 25.8% of tokens, so not token-matched (§5, Table 10, Appendix L). tau-bench: +4.0 [−1.1, +9.3] (F10).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 against random deletion on the same documents. Helps on MultiRC and FEVER label accuracy, not on Evidence Inference or tau-bench, so the paper itself says "the ordering is not stable" (§5). The FEVER gain is label accuracy, which F8 shows to overstate it.

### F5 · practice · deletion-unit-word-vs-sentence · E4
- practice: keep the same LLMLingua-2 importance scores and change only the unit kept, words or whole sentences, with random words and random sentences as the matching controls (Appendix H, prespecified).
- claim: the lead of LLMLingua-2 comes mainly from its scores choosing words (Appendix H).
- evidence: words − sentences by the same scores at 25%: +4.6 [+3.2, +5.9] (MultiRC, open-decider), +11.7 [+10.4, +13.0] (gpt-6-luna), +19.1 [+15.9, +22.1] (FEVER). Scores − random for sentences +1.3 [+0.2, +2.3], +0.4 [−0.7, +1.5] and +1.9 [−1.3, +5.4]; scores − random for words +5.4, +10.0 and +14.7 (Table 13). Random words − random sentences +0.4 [−0.4, +1.3] (open-decider), +1.2 and +6.3 (Appendix H). Sentence scores on FEVER 63.1% against 82.2% for word scores and 67.5% for random words (Appendix H).
- effect: helps
- reader: the cleanest ablation in the paper, one factor at a time, prespecified, E4. It uses one scoring model and 3 reader cells.

### F6 · practice · hybrid-sentence-and-word-compaction · E4
- practice: keep typed whole sentences up to half of the token budget and fill the rest with the highest-scoring LLMLingua-2 words of the other sentences (§4, Appendix H, prespecified).
- claim: the hybrid beats typed selection on hidden questions and keeps the known question's evidence whole (§4).
- evidence: hidden accuracy at 25% of tokens, hybrid against typed: MultiRC gpt-6-luna 76.5 against 67.9, open-decider 65.7 against 61.0, FEVER 84.7 against 79.0 (Table 14); differences +8.3 [+7.0, +9.5], +4.6 [+3.5, +5.8], +5.7 [+3.6, +7.8] (Appendix H). Against LLMLingua-2: hidden −4.2, −2.4, +2.6 (90% intervals); known +1.3, +0.7, +9.5; predictions H2 not met, not met, met and H3 not met, not met, met (Appendix H). On the FEVER support target the hybrid's gain over typed is −3.7 [−9.4, +1.3] (§4). On tau-bench hybrid − typed −10.4 [−15.0, −5.6] (Appendix N).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 for the hybrid against typed. The hybrid is the authors' construction, so this is their own method; the prespecified predictions it was meant to meet against LLMLingua-2 were mostly not met, and on agent trajectories it is worse than typed selection.

### F7 · observation · content-word-retention-share · E3
- practice: none applied; the share of the hidden questions' evidence content words (words outside a standard English stop list) that survive is compared across policies and used as a covariate (§4, Appendix I; exploratory).
- claim: part of the word-level lead is content-word retention, and part is which words are kept (§4, §6).
- evidence: LLMLingua-2 keeps 48.0% of the evidence's content words against 26.1% for random sentence deletion at about the same share of all words (Table 2). At equal content-word retention the MultiRC gap shrinks from 12.5 to +7.3 [+6.1, +8.4] (gpt-6-luna) and from 6.0 to +2.0 [+0.7, +3.2] (open-decider); on FEVER binned retention leaves +1.3 [−1.1, +3.5] and the linear form +14.9 (Table 15). LLMLingua-2 beats random words at equal retention by +6.7 [+5.5, +8.0], +1.7 [+0.5, +2.9] and +9.6 [+7.5, +11.7] (§4).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the authors call this an association, since the policies produce the retention they are compared at (§4). The FEVER result depends on the model form (1.3 against 14.9), so on FEVER content words are not separated from all words (§8).

### F8 · observation · label-accuracy-vs-kept-evidence-support · E3
- practice: none applied; on FEVER and Evidence Inference each answer is also scored against what the kept text supports: the document's label if all of the claim's annotated evidence was kept, "not enough info" if all was deleted (§3, Appendix J).
- claim: label accuracy can overstate what the kept text supports; the gain of sentence selection is not detected under support scoring (Abstract, §3).
- evidence: FEVER 25%, typed − random: label recovery +25.2 [+19.9, +30.8], support-target accuracy −1.0 [−3.9, +2.2] (Table 16); Evidence Inference label +23.1 [+20.2, +25.9], support +3.4 [+0.5, +6.4] at 25% and +10.1 [+7.7, +12.7] at 50%. Of true FEVER claims with at most a fifth of their evidence content words kept, the reader still calls 56.0% supported after LLMLingua-2 and 26.8% after random sentence deletion (§4). KEEP − DROP at 25%: FEVER +50.0 [+47.4, +52.4] (KEEP 95, DROP 45), Evidence Inference +17.8 [+15.9, +19.7] (KEEP 90, DROP 72) (Table 17). The support target is undefined for 99% of hidden claims under LLMLingua-2 (§4).
- effect: none
- reader: effect is on the support-scored outcome; the label-scored gain is the same practice at E4 in F2 and F4. The support target leaves partly kept evidence undefined, which drops claims, and evidence annotations are incomplete (§8). In the KEEP − DROP arms the swapped sentences may be evidence for other claims (Appendix K).

### F9 · practice · llm-written-summary-compaction · E4
- practice: have an LLM (qwen3.8-flash, gpt-6.1-sol) write a summary of each MultiRC paragraph within the 25% token budget, once for unknown later questions (generic) and once for the known question (focused) (§4, Appendix O).
- claim: LLM-written summaries stay below word-level compression on later questions; focused summaries serve the known question and not the later ones (§4).
- evidence: hidden accuracy, generic summary − random sentences +7.6 [+6.6, +8.7] (qwen3.8-flash) and +10.9 [+9.8, +12.1] (gpt-6.1-sol); generic − LLMLingua-2 −4.5 [−5.7, −3.2] and −1.3 [−2.4, −0.3]. Focused: known accuracy 88.5 and 89.2 against 78.7 for typed selection; hidden −2.9 [−4.0, −1.8] and +0.9 [−0.2, +2.1] against random sentences (Table 23).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 against random deletion and LLMLingua-2 on the same paragraphs. Summary length is not controlled: generic summaries use 88.4% and 99.2% of the budget and 74.3% of the gpt-6.1-sol ones were cut at the limit, so the writer and the length are not separated (Appendix O). One corpus, one budget, one reader (§8).

### F10 · practice · agent-trajectory-unit-selection · E4
- practice: compact a recorded tau-bench retail trajectory to 25% of its tokens by keeping the units (turns, tool calls, tool outputs) that gpt-6-luna judges needed for the known question, and ask later questions for the identifier a later tool call passes (§5, Appendix N).
- claim: on agent states whose later calls need exact identifiers, typed selection beats random deletion on later questions, and LLMLingua-2 shows no detected gain (§5).
- evidence: typed − random hidden +22.3 [+15.1, +28.9] (gpt-6-luna) and +21.7 [+15.7, +27.6] (qwen3.8-flash), 390 states and 837 hidden questions; LLMLingua-2 − random +4.0 [−1.1, +9.3]. The hidden identifier survives for 66.4% of questions after typed selection, 42.4% after random deletion, 52.3% after LLMLingua-2; questions whose identifier survives score 85.9%, against 18.9% when it is lost. Accuracies on hidden questions: full state 91.0, typed 67.1, recency 53.9, TF-IDF 49.0, LLMLingua-2 48.9, random 44.9, empty 20.2 (Table 21).
- effect: helps
- reader: the closest setting to an agent session in the paper, but the trajectories are recorded successful retail runs, the questions ask only for identifiers and the only budget is 25% (§8). It is a replay, not a live agent, and no coding task is scored. Typed selection stays 23.9 points below the full state on hidden questions (91.0 against 67.1, reader's arithmetic).

### F11 · practice · anticipatory-selection-for-later-calls · E4
- practice: keep units needed by any of ten likely later questions that qwen3.8-flash writes from the state (anticipatory), or weigh those questions against the known one in a greedy coverage objective (ECov), instead of keeping only what the known question needs (Appendix N, prespecified).
- claim: selectors that anticipate later calls trade the known call for the hidden ones; no selector beat typed selection on both (§5, Appendix N).
- evidence: on a 120-state subsample ECov − typed +12.8 [+5.2, +20.8] on hidden questions and −10.6 (90% interval [−17.8, −2.9]) on the known one; anticipatory +8.7 and −12.0. About half of the hidden gain comes from fewer refusals: on answered questions only ECov − typed +5.8 [−1.4, +13.6] (§5). ECov closes G = 0.64 of the random–oracle gap, prediction at least 0.70 not met; embedding-based ECov −10.9 [−18.2, −4.3] against the Boolean version, within ±3 predicted, not met (Appendix A, Table 21).
- effect: mixed
- reader: E4 against typed selection on the same states. The ECov selector is the authors' own design and 3 of its 4 prespecified predictions were not met; a 120-state subsample gives wide intervals.

### F12 · practice · recoverable-elided-observations · E3
- practice: leave a stub (naming the dropped units and their roles) or a gist line for each dropped unit, then re-read a hidden question alone with dropped units added back, up to a quarter of the full state's tokens, when its sufficiency check is below a threshold θ (Appendix N).
- claim: fetching dropped units on demand does not come close to the full state, and the prespecified prediction that it would is not met (§6, Appendix A).
- evidence: gists at θ = 0.5 reach 69.1% hidden accuracy against 88.8% for the full state, at 102.0% of its passage tokens; stubs 72.6% at 104.5% (Table 22). The prediction was at least 95% of full-state accuracy at no more than 60% of its tokens (M6). Low sufficiency ranks questions whose identifier was dropped first (AUROC 0.73 stubs, 0.72 gists), "so the main failure is choosing the units to fetch" (Appendix N). The first pass scores 64.0% with stubs and 51.3% with gists.
- effect: hurts
- reader: compared with the full state, not randomised, so E3; effect is against keeping everything, since the tokens are not saved. Against compaction with no recovery the stubs (72.6%) are above typed selection (67.1% on state means, Table 21), but the two use different averaging, so the paper does not compare them directly. Unit choice by TF-IDF match is the authors' simple retrieval.

### F13 · practice · evidence-restore-after-compaction · E4
- practice: add back to the compacted text sentences that were deleted evidence for a hidden question (restore), or as many sentences that are evidence for no question (placebo), and re-read (Appendix D).
- claim: "Deleted evidence drives the losses": restoring it undoes more flips than the placebo does (§3).
- evidence: over 11,296 answers accuracy goes from 68% compacted to 78% with restore and 71% with the placebo (full text 89%). Of 2,795 broken-evidence flips restore undoes 46% and the placebo 20% (+27 [+23, +32]); where all deleted evidence returned (801 flips) 86% against 21% (+65 [+59, +71]) (Appendix D). Answers the reader got right on the full text flip at 14% (open-decider) and 36% when their evidence is broken, 5% and 29% for gpt-6-luna (Appendix D); FEVER 45% against 1% (Appendix E).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 with a placebo arm. The restore uses annotated evidence, which a deployed system does not have, so it shows the mechanism and not a usable method.

### F14 · practice · sufficiency-routed-reread · E4
- practice: ask a sufficiency question after compaction ("does the passage contain the information needed to answer this question?") and re-read the lowest-scoring quarter of hidden questions on the full text, against a random quarter (Appendix D, Appendix E).
- claim: the check ranks damaged questions first, but "most of the recovered accuracy comes from re-reading at all" on MultiRC (Appendix D).
- evidence: MultiRC typed 25%, gpt-6-luna: sufficiency AUROC 0.83 against deleted evidence and 0.71 against flipped answers; re-reading the lowest-sufficiency quarter lifts hidden accuracy from 67% to 74%, a random quarter to 73%; the check adds +1.7 [+1.2, +2.2] points (open-decider 62%, 67%, 66%, +1.5 [+1.1, +1.8]). FEVER AUROC 0.87 for flipped claims, accuracy 71% to 86% against 77% for a random quarter, +8.9 [+8.3, +9.4] (Appendix E). LoCoMo AUROC 0.81 [0.79, 0.84] (Appendix M).
- effect: helps
- reader: E4 by simulation: the re-read substitutes the recorded full-text answers, which equals a real re-read only if a second read returns the same answer; 100 repeated gpt-6-luna full-text reads gave identical answers (Appendix B). The gain over a random quarter is small on MultiRC and large on FEVER.

### F15 · observation · compaction-token-break-even · E2
- practice: none applied; the input tokens of compacting once (scoring plus the 25% read) are counted against re-reading the full text for each later question, for gpt-6-luna (§6, Appendix L).
- claim: compaction pays for itself in input tokens only if enough later questions come (§6).
- evidence: cost of compacting once against always reading the full text, at each corpus's mean number of later questions: MultiRC 1.05 (break-even 14.0 against a mean of 10.2), FEVER 0.62 (7.8), Evidence Inference 1.55 (6.6 against a mean of 3.7), LoCoMo 0.33 (11.0). With the sufficiency check and a quarter re-read: 1.42, 1.12, 1.83 and 0.59 (Table 18). Batched, scoring plus the 25% read costs 1.15, 1.23, 3.94 and 2.53 times the full read (Appendix L).
- effect: mixed
- reader: counts of input tokens with a fitted token model for one-question requests (mean absolute error 2.2% to 8.2%, Appendix L); prompt caching is not modeled, which matters for a long-lived agent context. Break-even is for a typed selector that spends model calls on scoring; a free compressor such as LLMLingua-2 has no such cost.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Public text and conversation benchmarks and recorded agent trajectories, not live agents (§8).
- Abstractive summaries only on MultiRC at a 25% budget; tau-bench has fixed actions, identifier-only questions, retail only and one budget (§8).
- The gain-versus-evidence-kept relation is tested by consistency, KEEP–DROP interventions and one out-of-sample check, not assessed on LoCoMo, holds only qualitatively on tau-bench (§8).
- The content-word analysis is exploratory and leaves part of the MultiRC gap unexplained (§8).
- qwen3.8-flash is the only general instruction-tuned reader and reads texts gpt-6-luna selected; only two typed readers beat the all-"no" baseline (§8).
- Evidence annotations are incomplete; budgets count sentences or turns except in token-matched tests; the KEEP–DROP test has one budget and no MultiRC; LoCoMo has ten conversations and an empty-memory floor of 81.4%; intervals are unadjusted for multiple comparisons; the benchmark texts predate the models, so contamination cannot be ruled out (§8).

## Reader's notes

- No coding task is scored. The nearest evidence for agent sessions is the tau-bench replay (F10, F11, F12); the other findings are about QA over documents and conversation memory.
- The readers and selectors are typed decision models behind a hosted API (gpt-6-luna) and small open models; results for a general chat model as selector are not tested, qwen3.8-flash only reads (§8).
- The authors built typed selection, the hybrid and ECov, and ran every comparison themselves, though LLMLingua-2, LongLLMLingua and MMR are others' methods. Prespecification covers 10 analyses and 51 predictions of which 36 were met (Appendix A); the content-word and FEVER evidence-support analyses are exploratory.
- Random deletion is a weak control for question-aware methods but is the only reference used besides the full text; no method is compared with an agent that decides what to keep with its own tools.
- Table 1 reports the main contrasts with one random mask; Appendix C repeats them against the mean of several masks (typed − random hidden MultiRC −0.2 [−1.1, +0.7]), with the same signs.
- 710 of gpt-6-luna's MultiRC answers were refusals and are dropped; counting them as wrong changes no contrast's sign across 32 contrasts (Appendix B), but on tau-bench random deletion and LLMLingua-2 refuse 16.0% and 16.2% of answers against 10.9% for typed selection, and on LoCoMo refusals count as wrong.
- The authors disclose use of Claude Code in preparing the paper.

## Discrepancies

- App B says known-question accuracy at 25% is "66% after random deletion", Table 1 and §3 give 67% for the same gpt-6-luna cell; the text does not say why they differ.
- The abstract says no method that selects sentences for the current question does detectably better on later questions; this holds for MultiRC, while §3 and Appendix N report hidden-question gains for the same selection on FEVER (+17.2), Evidence Inference (+12.5) and tau-bench (+22.3).
- Table 2 gives hybrid hidden accuracy 76.6 and random words 70.8 on MultiRC gpt-6-luna; Tables 13 and 14 give 76.5 and 70.9. Table 14's caption attributes differences of up to 0.1 points to the question set.
