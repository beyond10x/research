## Question

Does the wording of one policy in an agent context file change how often the agent complies with it
(RQ1), and is the effect the same across harness and model combinations (RQ2) (§1)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · conditional-policy-wording · E4
- practice: write the policy as a condition ("If you add or modify a Python function, add the comment `# @tracked` on the line directly below its def line") instead of a direct instruction; the policy is appended as the last line of the repository's own context file, so position and surrounding text are the same across wordings (§2, Table 1).
- claim: "conditional phrasing is followed less often than direct instructions"; the penalty "is consistent" across combinations (§3 RQ1 and RQ2 summaries).
- evidence: compliance %, Prescriptive against Conditional, Table 3: claude-opus-5 78.0 against 64.2, claude-sonnet-5 71.6 against 35.6, gpt-oss-120b 6.7 against 0.8, Qwen3-Coder-Next 1.3 against 0.4 (n = 80 to 82 tasks). Friedman p < 0.001 in the first three, p = 0.343 for Qwen3-Coder-Next. Rank-biserial P–C, Holm significant in the first three: +0.65, +0.89, +0.88; E–C +0.56, +0.29, +0.97 (Table 4).
- effect: hurts
- reader: same tasks under each wording, so E4 on that wording. The conditional text also differs in length and sentence form, and each style is one text (§5), so the gap may come from the sentence. Compliance on gpt-oss-120b is under 7% for both, so the absolute difference there is small. One marker-comment policy, scored all-or-nothing per session.

### F2 · practice · reason-added-to-instruction · E4
- practice: add a reason to the direct instruction ("because our code-audit system uses this marker to identify agent-touched functions") against the same instruction with no reason (§2, Table 1).
- claim: "adding a reason to a direct instruction made no difference" on claude-opus-5; which of the two is better "varied across combinations" (§3, §4).
- evidence: compliance %, Prescriptive against Explanatory, Table 3: claude-opus-5 78.0 against 77.2 (rank-biserial +0.00, not significant), claude-sonnet-5 71.6 against 49.4 (+0.70, significant), gpt-oss-120b 6.7 against 20.4 (−0.82, significant), Qwen3-Coder-Next 1.3 against 0.8 (+0.20, not significant) (Table 4).
- effect: mixed
- reader: same tasks and position, so E4. The sign flips between models on one text pair; the one reason sentence is a single realisation (§5). The paper gives no account of why the reason helps one model and hurts another.

### F3 · practice · prohibitive-policy-wording · E4
- practice: write the policy as a prohibition ("Never add or modify a Python function without the comment ...") against the direct instruction (§2, Table 1).
- claim: Prohibitive was "significantly lower than Prescriptive and Explanatory in two combinations" (§3 RQ2).
- evidence: compliance %, Prescriptive against Prohibitive, Table 3: claude-sonnet-5 71.6 against 17.3 (rank-biserial +1.00, significant), gpt-oss-120b 6.7 against 0.4 (+0.88, significant), claude-opus-5 78.0 against 70.7 (+0.52, not significant after Holm), Qwen3-Coder-Next 1.3 against 0.0 (+1.00, not significant; Friedman p = 0.343).
- effect: hurts
- reader: same design and confounds as F1. For claude-opus-5 the Prohibitive wording is below the direct ones in the rates but not significant. Prohibitive against Conditional is not significant on opus (−0.27) and on gpt-oss.

### F4 · observation · instruction-compliance-by-agent · E3
- practice: none applied; the same wording set was run on four harness and model combinations and the compliance rates were compared (§2, Table 2, Table 3).
- claim: compliance differs by combination; the two open-weight models on qwen-code "stayed under 21% for every style we wrote" (§4).
- evidence: best style per combination, Table 3: claude-opus-5 78.0, claude-sonnet-5 71.6, gpt-oss-120b 20.4, Qwen3-Coder-Next 1.3 (never above 1.3 in any style).
- effect: hurts
- reader: the four rows differ in harness, model and cost class together; the authors say the harness cannot be separated from the model and that they cannot tell whether the ceiling comes from the models, the harness or the pair (§4). Sessions in which no Python function was changed are excluded, which the authors say raises the levels (§5). The effect is recorded as `hurts` for the open-weight rows, the direction the paper reports; the paper states no test between combinations.

### F5 · observation · wording-effect-differs-by-agent · E3
- practice: none applied; the spread between wordings was compared across combinations (§3 RQ2 summary, §4).
- claim: "a style chosen for one agent cannot be assumed to transfer to another"; the less capable of the two Claude models "was the one whose compliance moved more with the writing style" (§3, §4).
- evidence: the spread between the best and worst wording was 14 points on claude-opus-5 (78.0 to 64.2, "no single best style") and 54 on claude-sonnet-5 (71.6 to 17.3); on gpt-oss-120b Explanatory was best (20.4 against 6.7) and on claude-sonnet-5 Prescriptive was best (§3, §4, Table 3). The spreads of 14 and 54 are the paper's; 78.0 − 64.2 = 13.8 and 71.6 − 17.3 = 54.3 (reader's arithmetic).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the comparison is between four non-randomised combinations, so E3. "Less capable" is the authors' ranking of the two Claude models and is not measured in the paper. Whether the spread is a property of the model or of the wordings is not separated.

## Limits stated by the authors

- One text per style, so a style is confounded with its single realisation; the four texts also differ in wording and length (§5).
- One policy, a marker comment, and a session counts as compliant only when every target function has it, so the rates are specific to that policy and criterion (§5).
- Sessions with no added or modified Python function are excluded, which raises the reported levels and leaves one to three runs per task (§5).
- 86 tasks from 10 repositories that share an ACF and code base within a repository, so p-values may be optimistic (§5).
- The harness cannot be separated from the model; the open-weight models ran on qwen-code only (§4).

## Reader's notes

- The policy is an arbitrary formatting marker with an unrelated purpose to the task. Policies that affect correctness, security or process (run the linter, do not edit generated files) are named in §1 as the motivating cases and are not tested.
- Compliance is the task-level mean over up to three runs, then averaged over tasks; a task with one run left counts the same as a task with three (§2).
- Task success on the benchmark is not reported, so the paper does not show whether a higher compliance rate costs or helps the fix itself.
- Qwen3-Coder-Next is at 0.0 to 1.3% in every style, so F1 to F3 say nothing for that combination.
- Model ids and versions (claude-opus-5, claude-sonnet-5, Claude Code 2.1.227) are as printed; the paper gives no dates for the runs.
- Table 4 shows no significance mark on P–Pr and E–Pr for claude-opus-5 although the Friedman test is significant; the paper does not comment.
- The `design` value is the closest in the closed set: no task success is scored and the tasks are from an existing benchmark with a policy inserted (compare proposal P11 in SCHEMA.md).

## Discrepancies

- §4 and the conclusion say Conditional was followed less often "in every combination where styles differed" and "in three of the four combinations where writing style made a significant difference"; Table 3 has significant differences in three combinations, not four, so the second phrasing reads as 3 of 3.
- The abstract says "86 benchmark tasks across four harness–model combinations"; the n in Table 3 is 80 to 82 per combination after exclusions (§2 explains the exclusion).
