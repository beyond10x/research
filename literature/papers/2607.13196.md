## Question

How does review efficiency and review quality change as projects move from human-only review to LLM-assisted review and to review by AI agents, and which ways of adopting AI reviewers and which human-AI reviewer sequences go with better or worse outcomes (§I)?

## Findings

### F1 · practice · rapid-llm-reviewer-adoption · E3
- practice: adopt LLM reviewers early and heavily: LLM reviewers join 91% of PRs on average in the LLM era and AI agent reviewers 93% in the agent era (45 of 207 projects, 22%). Projects were grouped by soft-DTW clustering of their adoption time series, not assigned (§III.A, Fig. 2).
- claim: "Rapid LLM Adoption is significantly associated with an increase in code review smell prevalence" and shows no efficiency gain (§I, §III.A).
- evidence: Table IV, against each project's own pre-LLM era. Efficiency 5.4 days/KLOC before, change +0.6 (LLM era) and -0.1 (agent era), not significant. Share of PRs with at least one smell 83.6% before, +8.0 points (LLM era) and +4.4 points (agent era), both significant. Review Buddies 45.9% before, +26.0 and +23.2 points; the only significant smell type (§III.A). Wilcoxon signed-rank with Bonferroni correction.
- effect: hurts
- reader: before and after inside the same projects, with no control group, so E3. Time is confounded with the era: tools, models and project growth change over the same window. The authors suggest repeated use of the same LLM reviewer account (for example LlamaPReview) as the cause of Review Buddies; no test of that is given. Review Buddies is defined as one reviewer, human or AI, reviewing at least 50% of an author's PRs (Table II), so an always-on bot triggers it by design.

### F2 · practice · gradual-ai-reviewer-adoption · E3
- practice: move from human review to AI reviewers in steps: LLM reviewers join 8% of PRs on average in the LLM era and AI agent reviewers 36% in the agent era (95 of 207 projects, 46%) (§III.A, Fig. 2).
- claim: review is "significantly more efficient" in the agent era than in the pre-LLM era, with no significant change in review smells (§III.A, §VII).
- evidence: Table IV, Gradual AI Adoption. Efficiency 8.5 days/KLOC pre-LLM, -1.6 in the LLM era (not significant), -2.5 in the agent era (significant); "review time drops by 2.5 days/KLOC". Smell share 83.8% pre-LLM, +3.8 and +2.0 points, not significant.
- effect: helps
- reader: E3, within-project before and after. The efficiency measure is days to decision per thousand changed lines, so a larger PR gets a lower value with no change in time; the paper finds agent-involved reviews cover larger changesets (F8). The outcome is speed, and smells are the only quality proxy.

### F3 · practice · rapid-agent-reviewer-adoption · E3
- practice: little LLM reviewer use (19% of PRs in the LLM era), then rapid uptake of AI agent reviewers (76% of PRs in the agent era); 67 of 207 projects, 32%, including 12 Microsoft and 3 Google projects (§III.A).
- claim: review is "significantly more efficient" in the agent era; review smells do not improve (§III.A, §VII).
- evidence: Table IV, Rapid AI Agent Adoption. Efficiency 10.4 days/KLOC pre-LLM, -3.4 in the LLM era (not significant), -4.5 in the agent era (significant). Smell share 81.0%, +4.4 and +2.5 points, not significant. Review Buddies 36.4%, +16.6 points in the LLM era (significant), +12.4 in the agent era (not significant).
- effect: helps
- reader: same design and the same per-KLOC and calendar-time confounds as F2. Group sizes differ between practices, and the groups differ in kind (large-company projects in this one).

### F4 · practice · agent-initiated-or-multi-agent-review · E3
- practice: let an AI agent open the review (agent-init), or let several AI agents review the same PR (multi-agent), before or besides a human (§III.B). Patterns were found by fitting Markov chains with expectation-maximisation to the order of reviewer types in each PR; the lowest-BIC model gave 10 patterns.
- claim: "In the agent era, agent-init and multi-agent reviews are significantly faster than human-only review under Gradual AI and Rapid AI Agent Adoption", and agents "take over the inspection step" (§III.B, RQ2 summary).
- evidence: Table V Scott-Knott ESD ranks: agent-initiated and multi-agent reviews rank ahead of human-only review (R1-R2 against R2-R3). In 75% to 95% of agent-init and multi-agent PRs the agent posts a summary and the human answers with one short comment. Under Rapid LLM Adoption no significant difference; human-only review ranks first there, with 61% of its PRs resolved in a two-turn exchange.
- effect: helps
- reader: E3, between patterns within a practice and era, no control. Efficiency is time per KLOC, and agent-involved patterns go with larger changesets (F8). One short human reply after an agent summary shortens review whether or not the review was thorough; no defect data is used. Patterns are not assigned; the PRs that get an agent first may differ in kind.

### F5 · practice · ai-reviewer-participation-in-pr-review · E3
- practice: have an LLM or AI agent reviewer participate in the PR at all, against a human-only review in the same era (§III.B).
- claim: human-only review has "significantly lower review smell prevalence than most AI-involved collaboration patterns", mainly through Review Buddies (§III.B).
- evidence: Table V and text. Share of PRs with at least one smell: 69% to 76% for human-only, 78% to 94% for AI-involved patterns. Review Buddies: 16% for human-only reviews, 60% for LLM-involved and 53% for agent-involved patterns on average (from the replication package). Sleeping Review declines and Large Changeset stays flat across eras. Chi-square with Bonferroni correction.
- effect: hurts
- reader: E3. Review Buddies counts the same reviewer account on at least 50% of an author's PRs, which a standing AI reviewer meets mechanically; the paper reads it as narrower reviewer perspectives but does not show that feedback was narrower. The 16% for human-only reviews is far below the 36% to 48% pre-LLM shares in Table IV (see Discrepancies).

### F6 · practice · llm-assist-review-after-human-comment · E3
- practice: a single LLM reviewer adds comments to a PR that a human reviews (LLM-Assist pattern); the alternative is human-only review or several LLM reviewers (Multi-LLM) (§III.B).
- claim: "a single LLM comment is often insufficient to resolve a pull request by itself" (§III.B).
- evidence: Table V, LLM era. LLM-Assist reviews rank lower than human-only (R2-R3 against R1) in all three adoption practices. In LLM-Assist PRs humans comment first and continue the discussion: median 4 human comments against 2 in human-only review. Multi-LLM reviews are as efficient as human-only in Gradual and Rapid Agent Adoption, but less so in Rapid LLM Adoption; the LLM contributes a median 2 comments and in 75% of these PRs the human answers once. Under Rapid LLM Adoption every LLM-involved pattern is slower than human-only review.
- effect: hurts
- reader: E3, no control. The comment counts come from the replication package and are not in the text beyond these medians. Humans may add comments because the LLM did, or LLMs may be applied to PRs that need more discussion.

### F7 · practice · agent-initiated-or-multi-agent-review · E3
- practice: AI agent reviewers in agent-init or multi-agent patterns, against human-only review, with the other review factors held in a logistic regression (§III.C).
- claim: agent-era patterns reduce Sleeping Review (decision later than two days) under Gradual and Rapid Agent Adoption, but not under Rapid LLM Adoption (§III.C, RQ3 summary).
- evidence: impact scores (change in predicted probability, equation 4). Agent era, Gradual and Rapid Agent Adoption: Multi-Agent and Agent-Init -16 to -48. Rapid LLM Adoption: Agent-Init +42, Agent-ML +94. LLM era: LLM-Assist and LLM-Bot-Assist +26 to +214. 36 logistic models (9 efficiency, 27 smell), trained on 90% and tested on 10% of the data; the AUC values are not in the parts of the text read.
- effect: mixed
- reader: an association with covariates in a regression, so E3; the paper says it is "explanatory, not causal" (§V). The direction differs by practice with no stated reason. Time to decision is also what efficiency measures, so F4 and F7 are not independent evidence.

### F8 · observation · agent-review-of-larger-changesets · E3
- practice: observed, not assigned: which PRs get agent-involved review patterns.
- claim: "agent-involved collaboration is more frequently associated with the review of larger code changes than in the Pre-LLM and LLM eras" (§III.C).
- evidence: impact scores for Large Changeset (churn above 500 changed lines), agent era: +25 to +48 under Gradual AI Adoption, +20 to +54 under Rapid AI Agent Adoption. Under Rapid LLM Adoption the effect is not stated in the prose. Commit count, inline threads and author experience also associate positively with Large Changeset where significant.
- effect: hurts
- reader: this is a smell outcome, but it more likely shows which PRs get an agent reviewer than harm done by the agent; the authors say the patterns "are more frequently used to review larger code changes" (§I). It matters for F2 to F4, because efficiency is divided by size.

### F9 · observation · review-factors-persist-with-ai-reviewers · E3
- practice: observed, not assigned: traditional review factors (author experience, PR type, commit count, inline threads) in the same models as the AI collaboration patterns (§III.C).
- claim: "Traditional review factors remain important after generative AI reviewers are adopted"; with LLM and agent reviewers present, "AI agent collaboration becomes the dominant factor associated with review efficiency" in the agent era, except under Rapid LLM Adoption, where traditional factors are no longer significant (§III.C).
- evidence: Pre-LLM era, author experience goes with less review delay, impact -17 to -22 in all three practices. Sleeping Review: more commits +27 to +70 and more inline threads +26 to +51, more author experience and build or chore PRs -10 to -50. Review Buddies: build PRs negative (-43, -55 under Rapid LLM Adoption), author experience positive in all eras.
- effect: helps
- reader: the `helps` is for author experience and for build and chore PRs, which go with faster and less smelly review. Observational; factors were added one at a time with a likelihood-ratio test, and the garbled Table VI limits what can be checked. The unit is a statistical association inside each practice and era.

## Limits stated by the authors

- The logistic models in RQ3 are explanatory, not causal (§V).
- The agent era is a chronological period that starts at the first AI agent reviewer PR, so agent-era PRs may still contain LLM reviewers; LLM patterns with under 5% support in the agent era were left out of Table V (§V).
- AI reviewer capability is changing fast, so the patterns may shift (§V).
- The study does not explain why developers choose a given human-AI review practice (§IV).

## Reader's notes

- Every comparison is a project against its own earlier era, or a pattern against human-only review in the same era. There is no control for calendar time; the eras of different projects start at different dates, which spreads but does not remove the time confound.
- "Review quality" is measured as time to decision per KLOC and six process smells, three of them modelled. No defect, post-merge bug or review-comment-usefulness data is used. An agent summary followed by one short human reply (F4) counts as an efficient review.
- Reviewer identity comes from account types and tool documentation; 360 of 384 sampled agent-era PRs showed agentic behaviour, and the other 24 were labelled agent from official documentation (§II.B). PR types come from GPT-4.1-mini on titles and descriptions, agreement with 384 manual labels Cohen's kappa 0.91 (§II.C).
- Projects needed more than 400 reviewed PRs in each of the three eras, so a project that stopped using AI reviewers early or never reached the agent era is out of the sample.
- Per-KLOC normalisation (equation 1) makes larger PRs look faster; see F2, F4, F8.
- The authors are the evaluators and no tool of theirs is evaluated.
- Table VI's extracted text is garbled, so no number from it is used; the prose summaries in §III.C are the source.

## Discrepancies

- §III.B gives Review Buddies as 16% for human-only reviews; Table IV gives 36.4% to 47.8% for the pre-LLM era, when review is human-only or human with bots, and §III.C gives 46.9% for all modelled PRs. Not explained in the parts read.
- §III.A writes smell changes in points ("8.0% points") and the Review Buddies changes as "26%" and "23.2%"; Table IV lists both columns with the same notation, so the Review Buddies values read as points.
- The abstract and §VII say efficiency gains do not come with better quality, while the RQ3 summary says agent-era patterns "reduce Sleeping Review" in the same practices; Sleeping Review is one of the smells.
- §I and the RQ1 summary say Rapid LLM Adoption "significantly increases review smells" and Gradual and Rapid Agent Adoption "significantly improve review efficiency"; §V says only the RQ3 models are non-causal, while all of the paper's comparisons are before and after with no control.
