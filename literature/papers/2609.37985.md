## Question

How do maintainers decide on a performance fix that a coding agent opens, which properties of the fix
or the repository go with a merge, and does the claimed improvement hold when the fix is run (§1)?

## Findings

### F1 · observation · operator-self-merge-of-agent-pr · E3
- practice: observed how acceptance of agent performance fixes differs by whether the agent's PR is opened under a human operator's account (Codex, Cursor, Claude Code) or under a bot account (Copilot, Devin, Jules), which the paper calls the account model (§3.1, §4.1).
- claim: "The agent ordering largely follows the account model": the operator opens the PR and usually merges their own agent's work, while for a bot account someone else makes each merge (§4.1).
- evidence: acceptance of closed fixes, Table 6: Codex 73% (296/408), Claude Code 73% (37/51), Jules 73% (11/15), Copilot 50% (200/397), Cursor 42% (37/89), Devin 30% (44/145); chi2 = 104, V = 0.31 (§4.1). Operators self-merge 81%, 95% and 84% of merged Codex, Cursor and Claude Code fixes. Of 306 self-merged operator fixes, 209 were merged within an hour and 97% of those carry no human text (§4.1).
- effect: helps
- reader: no assignment of accounts; the agents also differ in tasks, repositories and PR description style. Cursor is an operator-account agent and has 42%, below Copilot's 50%, so the account model does not order all six agents. Operator self-merge is a merge with no second reader, which is why the high rate is not a quality signal.

### F2 · observation · repository-prior-merge-rate-of-agent-prs · E3
- practice: observed acceptance of a fix against the share of the repository's other, already decided agent PRs that had been merged when the fix opened (the pre-opening merge rate, §5.4).
- claim: "Acceptance differs more across the repository's history with agent PRs ... than across any measured property of the fix" (§5.4); studies that use merge as a quality label should control for the repository's history with the agent (§5 intro, §10).
- evidence: acceptance 33% where at most 40% had been merged, 57% in the middle band, 84% where over 90% had been merged (n = 165, 521, 255; chi2 = 109.7, p < 0.001) (§5.4). In the nested logistic model, odds ratio 13.7 from rate 0 to 1 (95% CI 6.3-30.0); the block lifts pseudo R2 from 0.145 to 0.232 (Table 8). Snapshot variant over all of AIDev v4: 27%, 39%, 67%, 69%, 89% by quintile (Table 9). Stars lose their effect once the rate is in: OR 0.58 per log unit becomes 0.98, p = 0.90 (§5.1).
- effect: helps
- reader: an association in one snapshot. The rate measures how the repository's maintainers or operators have treated agent PRs, so it is not an independent cause (§9); for operator accounts it partly measures the operator's own habit. The authors say so and do not test a change in the rate.

### F3 · observation · same-agent-track-record-in-repository · E3
- practice: observed acceptance of a fix against the number of PRs by the same agent that the repository had merged before the fix opened (§5.4).
- claim: the same gradient as the repository's merge rate (§5.4); the track record is collinear with that rate (Table 8 caption).
- evidence: acceptance 37% when the repository had never seen the agent, 31% when it had seen the agent and merged none, 51% after 1-4 merged, 58% after 5-19, 70% after 20 or more (§5.4, Figure 8b). The abstract gives "31-37% to 70%". Silent Copilot fixes (41 merged, 70 rejected, no human text): merged ones come from repositories with 13 merged Copilot PRs on average against 1.5 for rejected ones (§5.4).
- effect: helps
- reader: a raw count mixes prior success with exposure and repository activity, and for bot accounts an author association of NONE coincides with an empty track record (§9). Exposure to the agent is not separated from the repository's openness to agents.

### F4 · observation · deleted-line-share-of-fix · E3
- practice: observed the share of a fix's changed lines that are deletions, in merged against rejected fixes, pooled, within agent and within repository (§5.3).
- claim: the deleted-line share is "the one fix-level signal that holds up within agent and within repository" (§8.1); the paper does not test whether changing a fix's shape changes its outcome (§8.1, §10).
- evidence: share 0.26 merged against 0.15 rejected pooled; Copilot 0.20 and 0.10, Codex 0.33 and 0.21, Devin 0.28 and 0.20 (all p <= 0.001). Merged fixes delete a larger share in 50 of 74 repositories that hold both outcomes, a smaller share in 23, the same in 1 (Wilcoxon p < 0.001) (§5.3). Odds ratio 7.1 per unit of share in M2 (95% CI 2.9-17.0) and 10.5 in M4 (4.4-25.2) (§5.1). Within the 521 middle-band fixes OR 8.6, p < 0.001; within the 165 low-band fixes OR 1.5-2.1, p >= 0.23 (§5.4).
- effect: helps
- reader: logistic models with clustering by repository, so repository and agent are held fixed statistically; the signal vanishes where a repository rarely merges agent PRs. The authors call the several analyses dependent checks on one dataset (§9), and the share enters as one block of four.

### F5 · observation · fix-replaces-rather-than-adds-machinery · E3
- practice: observed, with a blind language-model judge given 40 pairs of one merged and one rejected fix from the same repository and the same agent, which fix it would merge and what it saw in the diffs (§5.3, §3.3.1).
- claim: the property the judge reads, "whether the fix installs a mechanism that the problem did not require", is detectable in same-repository, same-agent pairs, but the coded proxies do not measure it (§5.3).
- evidence: the judge named the merged fix as the one it would merge in 33 of 40 pairs (binomial p = 4 x 10^-5). It named the rejected fix as adding more machinery in 25 pairs and the merged one in 9; change style of the merged fix "replaces" 26 times and "adds" 5 times against 13 and 19 for the rejected fix; merged fix easier to read and trust in 31 of 40 (reading cost 2.4 against 3.3 on a 1-5 scale); rejected description overclaiming 14 times against 7. The higher deleted-line share is the merged fix in only 24 of 40 pairs, and the judge is right in 12 of the 16 pairs where the share points the wrong way (§5.3).
- effect: helps
- reader: matching on repository and agent is a design control, but no assignment: both fixes are existing PRs. The judge is a Claude Opus model that may have seen these public PRs in training and that reads the head commit, which can include changes made after review (§9). The authors state that this "is evidence that the difference is visible to a careful reader, not a measurement of how often maintainers read that carefully".

### F6 · observation · fix-content-and-description-vs-acceptance · E3
- practice: observed whether the coded content of the fix (root cause, performance category, locality, attached measurement) and the style of its description differ between merged and rejected fixes (§5.1, §5.2).
- claim: "the coded content of the fix, its description, its tests and its attached measurements do not" distinguish merged from rejected fixes (§5 intro); the lean in the pooled description statistics comes from each agent's template (§5.2).
- evidence: root cause p = 0.44 and category p = 0.25 (RC 59%, RDP 56%, GIC 50%, ISC 56% accepted); description features add 0.013 to pseudo R2 over the agent alone (0.106) (§5.1, §6.1). Pooled, rejected descriptions are wordier (median 271 against 97 words); within Copilot, Codex and Devin length does not differ (p = 0.75, 0.89, 0.53) (§5.2). Attached measurement 20% of rejected against 15% of merged (p = 0.024) and not significant once the agent is held fixed (M4) (§5.1). Codex bodies have a median of 39 words and Codex is accepted most; Copilot bodies 393 words (Figure 5).
- effect: none
- reader: "no difference detected" in observational data, with 1,102 fixes and repository-clustered errors. The authors state this is not evidence that no difference exists (§5 intro). The coded flags are coarse, and F5 shows that the judge sees a difference the flags do not.

### F7 · observation · test-kind-and-acceptance · E3
- practice: observed whether a fix that changes tests, or changes a particular kind of test, is accepted more often (§7.2).
- claim: "we detect no association between the kind of test and acceptance" (§7 intro).
- evidence: accepted 55% with a test change and 58% without (p = 0.49); by kind 51% with a performance test, 54% functional tests only, 60% adaptation only, 58% no test change (p = 0.53), also within each agent and with or without third-party review. Refit of M0 with the four kinds: every odds ratio 0.95-1.22 (p > 0.37) (§7.2). Agents change a test in 37% of 1,259 fixes (472), Copilot 47%, Codex 31%, Devin 29% (Table 12); the paper cites 15% of 570 human fixes (Zhao et al. 2023) from different corpora (§7.1).
- effect: none
- reader: tests are a property of the submission that nobody assigned. Maintainers may not read tests in a fix that an operator merges within minutes (F1), so a null here may reflect the review that happens, not the value of tests.

### F8 · observation · agent-fix-tests-pass-on-unfixed-base · E2
- practice: observed what the tests that come with an agent's performance fix check, and whether they fail on the code before the fix (§7.2, §7.3).
- claim: "the tests in the merged fixes do not carry the information a merge would need"; "a changed test is weak evidence that the claimed benefit was verified" (§7.3, §8.1).
- evidence: only 11% of 1,259 fixes include a performance test or benchmark (140 fixes: 66 sized workload, 49 toy, 23 parameterized, 2 none; 91 run in CI); 63% change no test (§7.2). In the 472 fixes with a test change the tests assert functional behavior in 51% and a performance quantity in 20% (§7.2). Among 30 merged fixes re-executed, the PR's own tests pass unchanged on the base in 17 of the 28 where they run, and only 2 of 30 fail on the base for a performance reason; in 4 fixes the PR's tests fail on the merged head (§7.3). In rsbuild PR 6060 the added test asserts under 100 ms for a call that takes about 41 microseconds on the base, so it passes on both trees (§7.2, Figure 12). With a performance assertion 3 of 9 fixes delivered, without 15 of 21 (Fisher p = 0.10; the authors draw no conclusion) (§7.3).
- effect: hurts
- reader: counts and cases, nothing to compare against, so E2. The 30 merged fixes were selected for a test change and for feasibility (§3.1 table, §9), which favors small self-contained fixes. The test judgements are by coders who knew the outcome.

### F9 · practice · agent-claimed-speedup-reexecuted · E2
- practice: re-execute the claimed improvement of 30 merged agent fixes (selected for a test change and a deterministic in-process harness) on 12 interleaved forks at three workload sizes, and judge against a delivery criterion defined in §3.5.2 (not read) (§7.3).
- claim: "a merge records that a maintainer accepted the fix and not that the fix does what its description says" (§7.3).
- evidence: 18 of 30 met the delivery criterion at the primary workload, 3 improved but fell short of the claimed gain, 9 showed no significant gain or regressed (6 no gain of at least 5%, 3 slower); median base-to-head cost ratio 1.8x. Of 10 fixes that claim a number, the measurement reaches it in 4, falls short in 3, and shows no gain or a loss in 3. 8 fixes are slower than the base at one of three sizes; 5 of the 18 that deliver gain only at the larger sizes (§7.3, Table 13, Figure 13). The protocol costs one container and at most 30 minutes per fix (§8.1).
- effect: mixed
- reader: no control arm: the cost of an unmeasured merge is not compared with a measured one. The sample is small and chosen by feasibility, which the authors say may overstate the non-delivery rate (§9). The workloads and tests were generated by Claude Opus agents (§9). Majority delivered, a third did not, hence `mixed`.

### F10 · observation · merged-fixes-later-found-incorrect · E2
- practice: observed behavior differences between base and merged head on inputs the PR's tests do not reach, using tests the authors' executing agent generated over the changed lines (§7.3).
- claim: nearly half of the 30 merged fixes "change behavior on inputs their tests never reach" (§7.3, abstract).
- evidence: generated-test outputs differ in 24 of 30 fixes; in 10 the difference is the intended effect or harmless; in 14 the fix introduces a functional change its description does not mention, and in 10 of those the head fails or returns a different result on inputs within the changed function's intended use. Cases: java-util binary search misses a key inserted in descending order (fixed later by the repository); rbac permission check lets a failed conditional permission of a child role shadow a parent's grant; numbagg one-pass covariance reaches relative error 0.13 at an offset the text prints as "106" (superscript lost in extraction); an AGI-Alpha-Agent-v0 rate limiter lets a throttled client through above 1,024 active clients (§7.3). Among 10 reproduced or partly reproduced rejected fixes, 2 break behavior (§4.3).
- effect: hurts
- reader: counts and cases from a sample of 30; no human-written fixes were tested the same way, so how this compares with human performance fixes is unknown. Whether the generated-test differences matter to callers was judged by the authors and the executing agent, with the outcome known.

### F11 · practice · agent-claimed-speedup-reexecuted · E2
- practice: re-execute the claimed improvement of rejected agent fixes whose PR text attaches a measurement: 30 drawn, 23 ran, three repetitions each on a workload the executing agent mostly built on the changed path (§4.3, §9).
- claim: "most rejected fixes with an attached measurement did not deliver the claimed improvement when re-executed" (§4.3); a maintainer who cares about the improvement must measure it (§4 intro).
- evidence: of 23, the claim reproduced in 6 (26%), partly in 4 (17%), not at all in 13 (57%): 6 slower, 5 no change within noise (0.97-1.02x), 1 head crash, 1 head without the claimed change. Median speed-up 1.02x (IQR 0.86-2.20x). Where a claim gave a number, the measurement fell short of it in all but 2 of 16 fixes. The tests the fix touched passed on the head in 13 cases (57%) (§4.3, Figure 4). 7 of the 30 could not run (3 need a live database, 4 are whole-workspace Rust builds) (Figure 4 caption).
- effect: hurts
- reader: for 16 of the 23 the executing agent built the workload, so a mismatch with what the PR measured is possible; the authors say it "gives no population rate" (§9). The reproduction criterion (Eq. 2) was not read. The two arms are measured under different protocols and the authors draw no conclusion from 18 of 30 against 6 of 23 (§7.3).

### F12 · observation · human-review-of-agent-pr · E2
- practice: observed what maintainers state when they close an agent's performance fix, and how fast and how silently fixes are decided (§4.2, §4.3).
- claim: maintainers decide quickly and mostly without stating a reason; "a rejection thread rarely shows whether anyone checked the claimed improvement" (§4 intro, §4.3).
- evidence: no stated reason in 61% of 480 rejection threads and 86% of direct rejections (close within 24 hours with no human text, 37% of rejections); only 3% of rejection threads ask for a measurement (13 rejections in which a reviewer asks for one, §4.3); a human engages substantively in 34%. Most common stated reasons: inactivity 9%, regression or incorrectness 8%, rejected design 6%, doubt about the benefit 6%, superseding change 5%. Merged fixes close in a median of 1.2 hours (IQR 0.1-16) against 24.3 hours for rejected (IQR 1.6-175) (§4.2, §4.3).
- effect: hurts
- reader: counts with nothing to compare against, so E2; `hurts` reads the silence as a gap in verification, which is the authors' framing. Devin's inactivity template accounts for 37 closures that are not human decisions (§4.2).

### F13 · observation · approval-funnel-agent-vs-human-pr · E3
- practice: observed the steps from review to merge of agent PRs against human PRs reviewed by the same maintainers, in five repositories and in a paired set of performance fixes (§8.2).
- claim: the lower acceptance of agent PRs is located at the approval step; once approved the two merge at the same rate; counting only merges by someone other than the author the populations are level (§8.2).
- evidence: five repositories, January to June 2025, 1,059 agent PRs (almost all Devin) and 953 human PRs: merged 41% against 80% (OR 0.17 with author association, size and repository fixed); third-party approval 35% against 53% (OR 0.23); merged among approved 95% against 96% (p = 0.39); merged among not approved 12% against 61%; merged by someone other than the author 39% against 35% (p = 0.052, OR 0.87). Paired set of 358 closed performance fixes: merged 40% against 79%, approved 24% against 41%, merged after approval 95% against 94% (Table 14). Among unapproved human PRs 61% are merged and the author merges 88% of these (§8.2).
- effect: hurts
- reader: a comparison with no assignment; five repositories, one agent (Devin) in most. Human authors merge their own PRs without approval and agent accounts often cannot, which the authors state as the account model. AI-written reviews posted from maintainer accounts count as third-party review (§9).

### F14 · observation · architectural-level-fix-scope · E3
- practice: observed acceptance of fixes coded architectural-level (several files, one mechanism) against functional-level fixes (§6.2).
- claim: acceptance penalty of architectural-level fixes is concentrated in the categories that accept the fewest fixes; maintainers of content, library and server-client projects do not distinguish the scopes (§6.2).
- evidence: 46% of fixes are architectural-level (582 of 1,259). Accepted 53% against 60% (p = 0.044) pooled; 67% against 70% in Content, Library and Server-client (p = 0.55); 37% against 56% in Language, Big-data and Build Management (p = 0.003). By file count acceptance falls from 62% (functional-level) to 57% (two files) to 51% (three or more) (p = 0.004) (§6.2). The new shared abstraction pattern changes the most production lines (258, medians) with deleted-line share 0.10 and adds a production file in 63% of cases; acceptance does not differ across the four patterns (47-59%, p = 0.48) (§6.3, Table 11, §6.2).
- effect: mixed
- reader: the pooled difference is weak (p = 0.044) and the authors call content differences of this size "suggestive" that "would weaken under any correction" (§9). Category and scope are confounded with agent and repository. Counts for scope are by the authors' coding with the outcome visible.

## Limits stated by the authors

- Findings are associations in one snapshot of AIDev v4 up to October 2025; the 157 open fixes may since have closed; agents released later are outside it (§9, §10).
- Whether changing a fix's shape would change its outcome is untested (§8.1, §10).
- The re-execution pilot has 23 fixes, 16 on workloads the executing agent constructed; it gives no population rate. The merged arm was selected for feasibility and may overstate non-delivery (§9).
- The two re-execution arms use different protocols and cannot be compared (§7.3).
- Many significance tests; subgroup and category comparisons are exploratory; the several analyses are dependent checks on one dataset, not replications (§9).
- The text filter's miss rate is roughly a tenth of the population (§9). Function counts are a lower bound (§9).
- The blind-pair judge and the executing agent are Claude Opus models and 60 of the 1,262 fixes are by Claude Code; same-vendor bias cannot be ruled out. The judge may have seen the outcomes in training (§9).
- The coders were not blind to the outcome (§9).
- The funnel comparison rests on five repositories and one paired set; its agent PRs are almost all Devin (§9, §10).

## Reader's notes

- The data and scripts are offered "upon acceptance" (Declarations); no link is given, so none of the findings can be re-run from this version.
- No finding has a control. Everything that looks like an effect of a practice is an association with a repository, account or agent property; the pre-opening merge rate, the account model and the agent identity overlap, and the paper's own model (M4) cannot separate them from the fix.
- The headline contrast "merged, not measured" mixes two arms under different protocols; the paper says so (§7.3) and the abstract still lists both numbers.
- The "delivery" and "reproduction" verdicts depend on criteria (Eq. 2 and §3.5.2) that were not in the read range, and on a primary workload chosen by the authors or their executing agent. Reading them as a rate of real improvement needs those definitions.
- Cursor (42%) and Copilot (50%) sit below Codex, Claude Code and Jules (73%), but Cursor is an operator-account agent. With 89 Cursor fixes and 15 Jules fixes the per-agent rates have wide error.
- Table 6 reports 502 repositories for 1,105 closed fixes; the consensus set has 582 repositories for 1,262 fixes.
- AI-written review comments from maintainer accounts (220 of 283 agent PRs in crewAI) inflate "third-party review" (§8.2).
- Claude Sonnet and a GPT model did the first coding pass; the authors audited every tenth rejected candidate (604) with kappa 0.96 (§3.1). The judged population is therefore conditional on that pass.
